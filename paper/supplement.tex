\section*{What this document is}

The main paper states every claim it makes and is complete without this
file. What it cannot carry, at eight pages, is the full numeric backing
behind five of its sentences. That backing is here, one table per sentence,
each reproducing the pointer the main text gives.

Every table below is \textbf{generated} from one hash-manifested frozen
snapshot by a single script; no number in either document is hand-typed.
Each table carries its own provenance header in the source. The tables are
reproduced here unchanged --- same files, same bytes --- so a number cannot
drift between the paper and its supplement.

\paragraph{Reading the denominators.} Two populations recur and are not
interchangeable. The \emph{marginal} population is every trajectory in a
cell, with each condition on its own denominator. The \emph{paired}
population is only those problems first-pass wrong under \emph{both}
conditions, on one shared denominator; that is the actual test, and it is
what every reported $p$-value is computed on (exact McNemar on discordant
pairs). Where a table shows both, they are labeled and must not be read
across. Conflating them has already produced one error in this project.

\paragraph{Recovery denominator.} Throughout, recovery is conditioned on a
recorded \emph{first} evaluator verdict of incorrect --- including
trajectories that later died in regeneration, which score as failures under
the pre-registered rule. Excluding those was a real defect in an earlier
analysis and it moved results materially; it is noted here so a reader
recomputing from the released data gets the same denominator.

\section{The pre-registered endpoints in full}

The main paper reports, in its Intervention section, that the pre-registered
primary endpoint P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on
accuracy --- is null in all three pairwise difference-in-differences, and
that the \Pone{} manipulation objective failed. Table~\ref{tab:prereg} is
the complete endpoint table promised there: all five endpoints as written
before any intervention data existed, what each one returned, and the
Holm-corrected verdict.

Two things in it deserve to be read rather than skimmed. First, only one of
the three P4 contrasts is an \emph{informative} null: the two involving
Llama-3.3 cannot detect an interaction of any plausible size at their
sample size, and are uninformative rather than evidence of absence.
Second, the contrast this paper is organized around --- role-structured
versus role-free --- was pre-registered as \textbf{secondary and
exploratory}. It is labeled post-hoc in the main paper and is labeled
post-hoc here.

\section{First pass and recovery, both populations}

Table~\ref{tab:recovery} gives the per-actor numbers behind the main
paper's central result, in both populations, so that the marginal rates and
the paired test can be checked against each other.

The gemma-4 row is the one the paper's headline rests on. Its first-pass
rates are equal to four decimal places, which is why the recovery contrast
in that actor is an exact reparameterization of the randomized
intent-to-treat effect rather than a conditional on a post-treatment
variable: with equal first-pass rates the two populations coincide in
expectation, and the role-free team does not enter recovery already behind.
That argument does \emph{not} transfer to the other two actors, and the
paper does not make it there.

\section{Cross-domain replication}

Table~\ref{tab:scibench} is the SciBench replication in full. The main
paper carries gemma-4 in Figure~2 and in prose; this is both halves with
their denominators.

The domain contract is \emph{not} byte-identical to Omni-MATH. SciBench
problems are free-response college science with units, so the answer
normalizer and the problem framing are domain-adapted; the role contracts,
topology, budget, decoding and evaluator protocol are unchanged. This is a
replication of the \emph{mechanism} under a matched protocol, not a rerun
of identical prompts, and the main text says so.

\section{Structural enforcement: both scorings, and the attrition that
separates them}

The main paper reports that enforcement un-freezes the answer in all three
actors but does not become end-to-end accuracy, and that the apparent
accuracy benefit is survivorship. Table~\ref{tab:enforcement} is the evidence
for both halves: the manipulation check, the one-sided attrition, and the
same accuracy column under both scorings with intervals.

This is the table behind the sentence in the main text that the enforced
recovery and first-pass rates \textbf{do not compose} into the accuracy
column. They cannot: trajectories that never finish are never graded, so
the three quantities do not share a denominator and the
$(1-\text{first-pass})\times\text{recovery}$ identity does not hold. The
pre-registration fixed in advance that unparseable trajectories count as
incorrect rather than being dropped, and that rule is what makes the
as-graded reading survivorship rather than a finding.

\section{The single-agent comparator}

Table~\ref{tab:singleagent} is the budget-matched single-agent reference. The
main paper uses it in one direction only --- as a guard against reading
``collapse costs nothing'' as ``the system was one agent all along'' --- and
this table is why that restraint is necessary.

The comparator comes from a \textbf{previously released corpus whose two
actor arms were built from different source commits}. Each direction within
an actor is therefore a sound guard, and \emph{the gap between actors is
not}. The result cuts both ways: a collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own
single agent, while gemma-4's is significantly worse in both conditions.
Neither ``multi-agent systems are just single agents'' nor ``more agents
help'' survives contact with it.

\section{Accumulated context as a competing explanation}

Accumulated context is the obvious competing cause of recovery failure, and
Table~\ref{tab:context} is the full panel promised in the main text.

It is reported with a caveat that is not decoration. Quartile cut points
are computed over both conditions \emph{pooled}, and accumulated context is
nearly a function of condition --- the role-free team accumulates less
because it stops reworking. The strata are therefore not balanced, and this
comparison cannot separate context from condition. It is
\textbf{directional only}, and it is included because it does not behave
uniformly: the gap survives in the lower quartiles, but in gpt-oss's top
quartile the ordering reverses. A stratification that had come out cleanly
everywhere would have been weaker evidence, not stronger, because it could
not have failed.
