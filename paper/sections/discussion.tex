\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Function of roles: gating submission vs.\ driving revision.}
Producing a first answer is a \emph{generation} problem. Having three agents
reach the same place by three routes costs nothing but tokens, and
first-pass accuracy is nearly indifferent to the division of labor. After a
failure, the team must produce something unlike the answer that just failed.
Except for the gpt-oss model, teams rarely do so unless told that the
candidate failed. Given that knowledge, the role contract adds little
capacity to revise. When matched on the number of verdicts, the two teams
differ little. The role contract primarily decides \emph{when} the team asks
for evaluation. The role-structured Reviewer withholds approval until it is
satisfied, so the team draws fewer verdicts and sometimes none at all.
Overriding the Reviewer closed the gap for the gemma-4 model. Under a
ground-truth evaluator and a fixed cycle budget, fewer verdicts mean fewer
recoveries. Where verification is costly, noisy, or unavailable, a team that
asks less often could be the better design; this experiment cannot tell.

\paragraph{Practical implications for multi-agent system design.} First, if
a cheap and reliable verifier is available, a Reviewer role that withholds
submission can cost recoveries. It is better to let the team submit and gate
on the verifier rather than on an internal critic. Second, regardless of the
role structure, the verifier's feedback must be routed to every agent that
can act on it. In our pipeline, the teams of the gemma-4 and Llama-3.3
models without a feedback slot almost never recovered, whereas the same
prompts recovered far more often once the feedback was added.

\paragraph{Methodological pitfalls: the necessity of information audits.} In
a prompted multi-agent system, feedback, tool outputs and verifier verdicts
reach the agents through slots in the same templates that carry the role
instructions, so rewriting those templates can silently remove a channel.
Nothing in the budget record, call counts or accuracy metrics reports the
absence. A role ablation therefore needs an information audit alongside the
budget audit: the runtime inputs each template consumes must be documented,
and a contrast is matched only when those input sets are identical. Our
role-structured and feedback-withheld conditions differ in one such input,
the feedback itself.

\paragraph{Reassessing the value of role diagrams.} Precise reviewing does
not necessarily mean the critique is adopted~\cite{yang2026precise}.
Similarly, a clean division of labor does not guarantee that a team adapts.
What matters is whether corrective information changes the next action taken
by the system. Task-outcome evaluation alone cannot test this because an
aggregate score averages stages where the organization is idle with stages
where it does all its work. We recommend two additions to standard
evaluations: report performance conditioned on a failed first attempt, and
measure the realized organization rather than assume the specified one.
