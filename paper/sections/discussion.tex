\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Roles allocate decisions as well as work.} The experiments
separate two functions of role instructions. Removing roles largely
eliminates specialization without reducing first-pass accuracy, whereas
recovery differs when teams use different numbers of feedback opportunities.
The role-structured Reviewer often withholds approval, and its team receives
fewer verdicts, sometimes none. The small pooled difference after matching
verdict counts is descriptive; it does not establish equal revision ability.
The forced-submission intervention provides more direct evidence: for gemma-4
in one exploratory run, recovery improves with the role prompts unchanged.
Together, these observations support treating submission decisions and
feedback access as part of the organization itself.
\paragraph{A design trade-off, not a defect.} A Reviewer that withholds
approval conserves external evaluations, which is valuable when evaluations
are costly, noisy or unavailable. In our setting, with a ground-truth
evaluator and a fixed cycle budget, a strict Reviewer instead limits the
team's opportunities to obtain corrective feedback; teams receiving fewer
verdicts also recover less often. We measure this side of the trade-off, not the full cost
and performance balance. Two design implications follow for similar settings.
First, with reliable external feedback and a fixed call budget, evaluate
submission policy separately from role specialization: keeping role prompts
while relaxing an internal approval gate can improve recovery.
Second, whatever the role structure, route the verifier's feedback to every
agent that can act on it, as the feedback-withheld diagnostic shows
(\S\ref{subsec:mechanism}).

\paragraph{Information audits.} In a prompted multi-agent system, feedback,
tool outputs and verifier verdicts reach the agents through slots in the
same templates that carry the role instructions. Rewriting these templates
during an ablation can therefore silently remove a channel, and no budget
record, call count or accuracy metric reports the absence. A role ablation
thus requires an information audit alongside the budget audit: the runtime
inputs each template consumes must be documented, and a contrast is matched
only when these input sets are identical. Even with matched access, the
realized use can differ, because the organization decides how often the
channels are used.

\paragraph{Decision rights and information pathways.} Prior work shows that
precise reviewing does not guarantee that a critique is
adopted~\cite{yang2026precise}. Similarly, a clean division of labor does
not guarantee that a team recovers from errors. The critical factor is
whether corrective information reaches the team and changes its next
actions. A task-outcome evaluation alone cannot reveal this, because an
aggregate score averages the stages where the organization sits idle with
those where it does its work. We recommend reporting performance
conditioned on a failed first attempt, measuring the realized organization
instead of assuming the specified one, and treating who may submit and
which agents see the feedback as core parts of the organization's
specification.
