\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What a role diagram is evidence of.} Both interventions changed
the realized organization dramatically and the score barely at all, in
opposite directions. A diagram is therefore a hypothesis about a system, and
task-outcome evaluation --- the only evaluation most multi-agent systems
receive --- cannot test it. This is not an argument against role
specifications, but against conflating two claims: ``the roles are working''
and ``the system is accurate'' require separate measurements, and reporting
only the second licenses no inference about the first. The corollary for
interface design is that enforcement governs speech, not thought: making an
answer unrepresentable drove authorship compliance to $1.000$ and left the
Planner's computation untouched. A schema can control who may \emph{emit} an
answer and has no purchase on who \emph{works it out}.

\paragraph{Why the monotone expectation fails.} Capability-dependence
accounts predict one ordering: structure helps weaker models and matters less
as models improve~\cite{canedo2026architecture,yu2026bottleneck}, with the
self-organization literature drawing the same line from the other
end~\cite{dochkina2026drop}. Our panel does not order that way, and the
mechanism explains why the monotone framing cannot accommodate it: what roles
supply is
not scaffolding for a weak solver but \emph{sustained disagreement}, and
whether an actor needs that supplied externally is a property of its
consensus behavior, not of its capability. This predicts where the result
should generalize --- any actor reaching near-total agreement with itself
should depend on differentiation for recovery regardless of capability ---
and testing that requires actors selected by consensus behavior rather than
by size, which this panel does not do. It also blocks the efficiency reading:
the collapsed team still beats its budget-matched single agent, and the same
ablation that is free for one actor costs another. A pruning decision
validated on one substrate does not transfer; what to measure instead is
whether \emph{your} actor escapes its own wrong consensus.

\paragraph{What our own measurement error implies.} Our first reading of this
experiment was a contrast between actors that an exact-duplication metric
said lost different amounts of organization. It did not survive a blinded
judge, because the metric cannot see an actor whose agents paraphrase rather
than copy. The failure mode is general: an instrument detecting one surface
realization of redundancy will report that whichever actor does not use that
realization has kept its organization. Any claim that a multi-agent system's
structure is intact should state what the instrument would have to see to
report otherwise, and show that it can.
