\subsection{Validation of the measures}
\label{sec:measurement}

The two clause measures were hand-adjudicated against definitions frozen
\emph{before} the adjudication sheet was opened, so they could not be tuned
to flatter the automated scores. Both clear a pre-set agreement threshold of
$0.80$: \Pone{} reaches agreement $0.903$ and $\kappa=0.806$ (precision
$0.943$, recall $0.868$, confusion 33/2/3/34) on $n=72$, and \Etwo{}
agreement $0.923$ and $\kappa=0.843$ (precision $0.833$, recall $1.000$) on
$n=13$.

The error structure matters more than those figures. \Pone{} agreement
\emph{fell} from $0.967$ at $n=30$ to $0.903$ at $n=72$ as adjudication
continued, and we report the final figure; its residual errors point in
\emph{opposite} directions (two false positives are symbolic results carrying
no digits, three false negatives contract-permitted content), so they do not
bias the actor comparison consistently. One systematic error was repaired and
one proposed fix rejected: the first Executor measure matched ``Step 0''
inside the Executor's own construction --- the solving-versus-following
confusion the construct exists to avoid --- and was rebuilt to require an
explicit plan reference, while a \Pone{} variant adding a symbolic-result
detector \textbf{was rejected when agreement fell to $0.694$ and recall to
$0.421$}, the detector having fired on 9 of 9 messages judged compliant
including the contract's \emph{required} answer-form section, penalizing
compliance with the contract it was scoring.

\paragraph{Collapse is audited separately.} Because collapse carries the
headline, it is validated against blinded adjudication and stress-tested
against deterministic decoding, normalization sensitivity, retry convergence
and problem easiness in \S\ref{subsec:collapse} --- which is also where the
byte measure's own failure, and the reason the primary measure is functional
rather than byte-level, are reported.
