\section{Definitions and Measurement}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{What ``load-bearing'' means here}

An organization is specified by a tuple: agents, an interaction order, a
message-passing graph, a context-visibility policy, and a \emph{role contract}
per agent. A contract asserts that an agent performs some function
(\textsc{must} decompose the problem) or that it does not (\textsc{must not}
state a final answer); clauses of the second kind make the specification a
\emph{division} of labor. The \emph{realized} organization is what occurs when
a particular actor executes that specification. Because a clause is prose
handed to a language model, realization is not guaranteed and nothing records
which clauses were honored.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Definition.} A component of an organization is \textbf{load-bearing}
if removing or altering it reproducibly degrades task performance
\emph{under matched compute and information budgets}.
\end{quote}

The budget clause makes the definition usable: removing role instructions
\emph{and} agents, calls, or context tests scale, not organization. Every
intervention below holds the full budget fixed and varies only what each agent
is told it is or what its interface will accept. Because
performance itself decomposes --- producing a first answer, and repairing a
wrong one --- the definition is applied \emph{per phase}, and
\S\ref{sec:results} finds the two phases answer differently.

\subsection{Measuring the realized organization}

\paragraph{Functional collapse (primary).} A first pipeline pass
\emph{collapses} if \emph{all three} agents independently assert their own
final answer, making the pipeline three solvers rather than a division of
labor. It targets the property the diagram asserts rather than the property a
hash can see, is measured on the first pass so it is not confounded with
trajectory length, and is \textbf{not applicable to Arms~C/C$'$}, where the
schema forbids an answer field and a zero would record the constraint rather
than the behavior. It is inverted \Pone{}, made presence-based rather than
length-based --- not a new construct.

\paragraph{Exact output duplication (secondary, a lower bound).} Two or more
role messages byte-identical, compared as \texttt{sha256} of the raw
completion before parsing. Precision $1.000$, recall $0.262$ against blinded
adjudication, so it establishes that at least a given amount of collapse
occurred and is \textbf{never used to compare how much organization two
actors lost} --- its recall differs by actor.

\paragraph{Answer authorship.} Whether a Planner authors a final answer. This
is the clause Arm~C structurally enforces and the manipulation check.

\paragraph{Computation (\Pone{} and density).} Whether a Planner abstains from
computing, scored as containing no evaluated numeric equality. Being the
absence of a pattern, it is mechanically anti-correlated with message length,
so every computation claim cites \textbf{evaluated equations per 1{,}000
characters} instead. This matters: Arm~B's gpt-oss Planner scores $0.722$ on
the binary rate --- apparently the study's most compliant Planner --- on a
37-character message. A metric that rewards silence cannot measure a division
of labor.

\paragraph{Recovery.} The fraction of trajectories whose first submitted
answer was graded wrong that nonetheless finish correct. The metric is not
new~\cite{jiang2026exactfeedback}; contrasting it across a role ablation is.
It conditions on a post-treatment variable and is descriptive throughout.
