\section{Definitions and Measurement}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{What ``load-bearing'' means here}

An organization is specified by a tuple: a set of agents, an interaction
order, a message-passing graph, a context-visibility policy, and a
\emph{role contract} per agent. A contract is a set of clauses asserting
either that the agent performs some function (\textsc{must} decompose the
problem) or that it does not (\textsc{must not} state a final answer).
Clauses of the second kind make the specification a \emph{division} of labor:
they assign work away from one agent and implicitly to another.

The \emph{realized} organization is the behavior that occurs when that
specification is executed by a particular actor substrate. Because a clause
is prose handed to a language model, realization is not guaranteed, and
nothing in the specification records which clauses were honored. An
evaluation that observes only task outcome cannot distinguish an organization
that divided the work as specified from one in which every agent did
everything. Both appear as a score.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Definition.} A component of an organization is \textbf{load-bearing}
if removing or altering it causes a reproducible degradation in task
performance \emph{under matched compute and information budgets}.
\end{quote}

The budget clause is what makes the definition usable. Removing role
instructions \emph{and} agents, calls, or context tests scale, not
organization. Every intervention below holds the agent count, interaction
order, message-passing graph, total call budget, retry budget, decoding
parameters, verifier and verifier prompt, stopping rule, and grading fixed,
and varies only what each agent is told it is or what its interface will
accept. The definition is deliberately behavioral: it makes ``the roles
matter'' a claim that can fail, and in \S\ref{sec:results} it fails for one
actor and holds for the other.

\subsection{Measuring the realized organization}

Three measures are used, each answering a different question. Full
definitions, the frozen annotation rules, and the validation sheet are
carried over unchanged from the measurement apparatus this study inherits.

\paragraph{Organizational collapse (primary).} A trajectory \emph{collapses}
if two or more of its role messages are byte-identical. The compared object
is \texttt{sha256} of the raw model completion, taken before parsing and
before any scaffold field is added, so it is what the model actually emitted
and carries no role wrapper. Two rules are reported: the \textbf{count-matched}
rule inspects only the first pipeline pass (first three role messages) and is
primary, because the all-message rule gives longer trajectories more chances
to collapse; the all-message rule is reported alongside it. Collapse is a
measure of whether the agents are behaviorally distinct at all --- the
weakest possible reading of ``the organization exists.''

\paragraph{Answer authorship (\Ptwo{}).} Whether a Planner message contains a
final answer. This is the clause Arm~C structurally enforces, and it is the
manipulation check.

\paragraph{Computation (\Pone{} and density).} Whether a Planner abstains
from computing, scored as containing no evaluated numeric equality, with
symbolic restatement of givens explicitly permitted. \Pone{} is frozen at the
validated version (agreement $0.903$, $\kappa = 0.806$ against hand
adjudication on 72 messages) and is \emph{not} redefined post-hoc. Because it
is defined as the absence of a pattern, it is mechanically anti-correlated
with message length, so every computation claim in this paper cites the
length-robust \textbf{evaluated equations per 1{,}000 characters} instead,
with the binary rate reported alongside. This matters: Arm~B's gpt-oss
Planner scores $0.739$ on binary \Pone{} --- apparently the most compliant
Planner in the study --- while emitting a bare boxed answer and no work at
all. A metric that rewards silence cannot measure a division of labor.

\paragraph{Why three measures and not one.} They come apart, which is the
point. An agent can be perfectly distinct from its neighbors while honoring
no clause of its contract, and perfectly compliant while indistinguishable.
Collapse asks whether the agents differ; answer authorship asks who may
speak; density asks who does the work. \S\ref{subsec:structnotcog} shows the
second can be driven to $1.000$ while the third does not move.
