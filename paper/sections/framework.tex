\section{Definitions and Measurement}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{What ``load-bearing'' means here}

An organization is specified by a tuple: agents, an interaction order, a
message-passing graph, a context-visibility policy, and a \emph{role
contract} per agent. A contract asserts that an agent performs some
function (\textsc{must} decompose the problem) or that it does not
(\textsc{must not} state a final answer); clauses of the second kind make
the specification a \emph{division} of labor. Because a clause is prose
handed to a language model, the \emph{realized} organization is not
guaranteed to match it, and nothing records which clauses were honored.

\textbf{Definition.} A component of an organization is
\textbf{load-bearing} if removing or altering it reproducibly degrades
task performance \emph{under matched call and information budgets}. The
budget clause is what makes it usable: removing role instructions
\emph{and} agents, calls or context tests scale, not organization. Because
performance itself decomposes --- producing a first answer, repairing a
wrong one --- the definition is applied \emph{per phase}.

\subsection{Measuring the realized organization}

\paragraph{Functional collapse (primary).} A first pipeline pass
\emph{collapses} if \emph{all three} agents independently assert their own
final answer, making the pipeline three solvers rather than a division of
labor. It targets the property the diagram asserts rather than what a hash
can see, is measured on the first pass so it is not confounded with
trajectory length, and is not applicable to the structurally
enforced conditions, whose schema forbids an answer field.

\paragraph{Exact output duplication (secondary, a lower bound).} Two or
more role messages byte-identical, as \texttt{sha256} of the raw
completion. Precision $1.000$, recall $0.262$ against the adjudication (a
within-item comparison, so unaffected by that adjudication's condition
leak, \S\ref{subsec:collapse}): it establishes that at least a given
amount of collapse occurred and, its recall differing by actor, is
never used to compare how much organization two actors lost.

\paragraph{Answer authorship, and computation.} Whether a Planner authors
a final answer --- the clause structural enforcement makes
unrepresentable, and the manipulation check. Separately, whether a Planner
abstains from \emph{computing}, scored as containing no evaluated numeric
equality (\textbf{\Pone{}}, a pre-registered fidelity measure). Being the
absence of a pattern, \Pone{} is anti-correlated with length, so every
computation claim cites evaluated equations per 1{,}000
characters instead: the role-free gpt-oss Planner scores $0.722$ on the
binary rate --- apparently the study's most compliant --- on a
37-character message. A metric that rewards silence cannot measure a
division of labor.

\paragraph{First pass and recovery.} \emph{First-pass} accuracy grades
the first answer submitted to the evaluator --- for the role-structured team
possibly after internal revision, never after external feedback.
\emph{Recovery} is the fraction of trajectories
whose first submitted answer was graded wrong that nonetheless finish
correct --- not a new
metric; contrasting it across a role ablation
is. It conditions on a post-treatment variable, so recovery contrasts are
descriptive; for gemma-4 the claim rests on the unconditional
final-accuracy contrast they account for (\S\ref{subsec:recovery}).
