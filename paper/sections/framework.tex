\section{Definitions and Measurement}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{What ``load-bearing'' means here}

An organization is specified by a tuple: agents, an interaction order, a
message-passing graph, a context-visibility policy, and a \emph{role
contract} per agent. A contract is a set of clauses asserting that the agent
performs some function (\textsc{must} decompose the problem) or that it does
not (\textsc{must not} state a final answer); clauses of the second kind make
the specification a \emph{division} of labor, assigning work away from one
agent and implicitly to another.

The \emph{realized} organization is the behavior that occurs when that
specification is executed by a particular actor. Because a clause is prose
handed to a language model, realization is not guaranteed, and nothing
records which clauses were honored. An evaluation observing only task outcome
cannot distinguish an organization that divided the work as specified from
one in which every agent did everything. Both appear as a score.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Definition.} A component of an organization is \textbf{load-bearing}
if removing or altering it causes a reproducible degradation in task
performance \emph{under matched compute and information budgets}.
\end{quote}

The budget clause is what makes the definition usable: removing role
instructions \emph{and} agents, calls, or context tests scale, not
organization. Every intervention below holds the agent count, interaction
order, message-passing graph, total call budget, retry budget, decoding
parameters, verifier and verifier prompt, stopping rule, and grading fixed,
and varies only what each agent is told it is or what its interface will
accept. The definition is deliberately behavioral: it makes ``the roles
matter'' a claim that can fail, and in \S\ref{sec:results} it fails for one
actor and holds for another.

\subsection{Measuring the realized organization}

Four measures are used, each answering a different question. Full
definitions and the frozen annotation rules are carried over unchanged from
the measurement apparatus this study inherits.

\paragraph{Functional collapse (primary).} A first pipeline pass
\emph{collapses} if \emph{all three} agents independently assert their own
final answer, making the pipeline three solvers rather than a division of
labor. This targets the property the role diagram asserts --- that the
agents do different jobs --- rather than the property a hash can see. It is
measured on the first pipeline pass so that it is not confounded with
trajectory length, and it is \textbf{not applicable to Arms~C/C$'$}, where
the schema forbids the Planner and Reviewer an answer field and a zero would
therefore record the constraint rather than the behavior.

\paragraph{Exact output duplication (secondary, a lower bound).} A trajectory
\emph{duplicates} if two or more role messages are byte-identical, compared
as \texttt{sha256} of the raw completion before parsing and before any
scaffold field is added. Against blinded adjudication it has precision
$1.000$ and recall $0.343$ (\S\ref{subsec:collapse}), so we use it to
establish that at least a given amount of collapse occurred and
\textbf{never to compare how much organization two actors lost}. We report
it on the first pipeline pass, with the all-message rule alongside.

\paragraph{Answer authorship (\Ptwo{}).} Whether a Planner message contains a
final answer. This is the clause Arm~C structurally enforces, and it is the
manipulation check.

\paragraph{Computation (\Pone{} and density).} Whether a Planner abstains
from computing, scored as containing no evaluated numeric equality, with
symbolic restatement of givens explicitly permitted. \Pone{} is frozen at the
validated version (agreement $0.903$, $\kappa = 0.806$ against hand
adjudication on 72 messages) and is \emph{not} redefined post-hoc. Because it
is defined as the absence of a pattern, it is mechanically anti-correlated
with message length, so every computation claim in this paper cites the
length-robust \textbf{evaluated equations per 1{,}000 characters} instead,
with the binary rate reported alongside. This matters: Arm~B's gpt-oss
Planner scores $0.726$ on binary \Pone{} --- apparently the most compliant
Planner in the study --- while emitting a bare boxed answer and no work at
all. A metric that rewards silence cannot measure a division of labor.

\paragraph{Why several measures and not one.} They come apart, which is the
point: collapse asks whether the agents differ, answer authorship who may
speak, density who does the work. \S\ref{subsec:structnotcog} drives the
second to $1.000$ without moving the third, and \S\ref{subsec:collapse} has
two collapse measures disagreeing on an entire actor.
