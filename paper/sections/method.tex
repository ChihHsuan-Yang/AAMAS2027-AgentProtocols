\section{Intervention}
\label{sec:method}

To ask whether organization is load-bearing we vary it in both directions
from the same baseline: remove it, and enforce it. All arms share three
agents, the same interaction order
(Planner~$\rightarrow$~Executor~$\rightarrow$~Reviewer), message-passing
graph, total call budget, verifier and verifier prompt, retry budget,
decoding parameters (temperature $0$, identical \texttt{max\_tokens}),
stopping rule, and grading. This is what makes the comparison a test of
organization rather than of scale.

\paragraph{Arm~A --- soft-role \PER{} (control).} Role prompts as ordinarily
written. The organization as specified.

\paragraph{Arm~B --- generic roles (organization removed).} Identical
topology and budget; all three agents receive the same generic prompt
(``You are one of three agents solving this problem collaboratively;
continue the work''). Role names are retained for routing only, so the
pipeline still runs and the budget is untouched. Arm~B is \emph{not} ``\PER{}
minus role words'' but the same topology with no division of labor specified
at all --- the ablation the load-bearing definition calls for.

\paragraph{Arm~C --- architectural enforcement (organization made
unavoidable).} Specialization is enforced by the interface rather than by
wording:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Planner.} Emits JSON parsed against a schema with \emph{no
  answer field}: \texttt{\{givens[], target\_form, lemmas[], steps[]\}}. A
  final answer is \emph{unrepresentable}; free text is rejected and
  regenerated.
\item \textbf{Executor.} Receives the parsed plan object and the problem ---
  never the Planner's raw text --- and emits \texttt{\{derivation, answer\}}.
\item \textbf{Reviewer.} Emits \texttt{\{verdict, target\_span, critique\}}.
  \texttt{accept} promotes the Executor's answer verbatim \emph{by
  reference}; the Reviewer has no field in which to \emph{author} one.
\end{itemize}

Contract violation is therefore unrepresentable rather than discouraged.
Submit-by-reference exists because deleting the Reviewer's answer field
outright would change \emph{who submits} as well as who may \emph{author}.
Arm~C removes no capability: planning, computing, answering and critiquing
are all still performed, each by one agent, under the same call budget.

\paragraph{Arm~C$'$ --- context control.} Arm~C with shared history restored
in all three role templates; schema, routing and budgets unchanged. Since
Arm~C necessarily carries less context than Arm~A, C$'$ licenses the
identified contrast ``the effect of adding context at fixed enforcement.'' It
is a \emph{partial} control: matching C and A exactly would require feeding
raw prose back in, dissolving the constraint that defines the arm.

\paragraph{Single-agent reference.} A budget-matched single-agent protocol
runs on the same problems. Without it, ``collapse costs nothing'' cannot be
distinguished from ``the system was one agent all along''
\cite{zhang2026rethinking}. It is a required control, not an extra.

\subsection{Instrumentation}

An intervention that never engaged cannot explain an outcome, so engagement
is measured rather than assumed. Every rejected-and-regenerated emission is
logged with its reason, and the enforcement rate is computed from
\textbf{role-boundary violations only} --- formatting coercions are logged
separately and excluded, a definition fixed in advance. Answer-bearing calls
are counted per arm, so an arm cannot win or lose by producing fewer answers.
Evaluator calls and the pinned serving backend are logged per trajectory,
because the same cell has shown a $9.6\times$ throughput difference and a
large accuracy difference across backends. One instrumentation gap escaped
this discipline and is reported in \S\ref{subsec:logging}.

\subsection{Pre-registered analyses, and what became of them}
\label{subsec:prereg}

All endpoints were fixed in writing and committed before any intervention
data existed. Units are (problem, actor) pairs; all arms run on the same
problems, so every test is paired with bootstrap clustering by problem, and
P1--P5 carry a Holm correction.

\begin{description}\itemsep1pt
\item[P1] Intervention $\rightarrow$ fidelity, per actor. Also the
  manipulation check.
\item[P2] Actor $\times$ intervention on fidelity.
\item[P3] Intervention $\rightarrow$ accuracy, per actor.
\item[P4] Actor $\times$ intervention on accuracy. \textbf{The primary test
  of the study's original thesis.}
\item[P5] $\Delta$fidelity versus $\Delta$outcome; association only, and
  explicitly not causal, since both are consequences of one intervention.
\end{description}

Two things belong here rather than in Results, because they concern
provenance. \textbf{First, P4 is null} and we report it as the failure of the
thesis it was written to test. \textbf{Second, the A-versus-B contrast this
paper is organized around was pre-registered as secondary and exploratory},
and is labeled post-hoc everywhere it appears. The pre-registered P1
manipulation objective --- that enforcement would drive Planner
computation-abstention to ${\approx}1.0$ --- also \textbf{failed}, because
the check named a clause the intervention does not enforce; it was
re-designated to answer authorship and the failure reported in
\S\ref{subsec:structnotcog}.

Exclusions were fixed in advance: unparseable trajectories count as incorrect
rather than dropped; in Arm~C the regenerated message is analyzed and the
rejection count reported; no arm is dropped after results are seen, and an
arm failing its manipulation check is reported as having failed it.
