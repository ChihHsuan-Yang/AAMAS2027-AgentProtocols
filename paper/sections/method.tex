\section{Intervention}
\label{sec:method}

To ask whether organization is load-bearing we vary it in both directions
from one baseline: remove it, and enforce it. All conditions share three
agents, the same interaction order
(Planner~$\rightarrow$~Executor~$\rightarrow$~Reviewer), message-passing
graph, total call budget, verifier and verifier prompt, retry budget,
decoding parameters (temperature $0$, identical \texttt{max\_tokens}),
stopping rule and grading; every trajectory in the frozen snapshot carries
the identical budget record --- a test of organization, not of scale. One
asymmetry we state rather than smooth, the one place the conditions are
\emph{not} matched: the evaluator fires on Reviewer approval, so a team
that submits more \emph{consumes} more oracle verdicts even under an
identical cap. The role-free team draws $1.95$/$1.85$/$3.82$ evaluator
calls per trajectory against the role-structured team's
$1.11$/$1.35$/$1.03$ (gemma-4 / gpt-oss / Llama-3.3). \textbf{This runs
against our result} and we report it for that reason: a ground-truth
verdict is the only external information in the loop, and the condition we
find worse receives more of it in every actor.

\paragraph{Role-structured \PER{} (baseline).} Role prompts as ordinarily
written. The organization as specified.

\paragraph{Role-free (organization removed).} Identical topology and
budget; all three agents receive the same generic prompt (``You are one of
three agents solving this problem collaboratively; continue the work''),
with role names retained for routing only. This is \emph{not} ``\PER{}
minus role words'' but the same topology with no division of labor
specified at all.

\paragraph{Structurally enforced.} Specialization is enforced by the
interface rather than by wording. The \textbf{Planner} emits JSON against
a schema with \emph{no answer field}, so a final answer is
unrepresentable; the \textbf{Executor} receives the parsed plan object and
the problem --- never the Planner's raw text --- and emits
\texttt{\{derivation, answer\}}; the \textbf{Reviewer} emits
\texttt{\{verdict, target\_span, critique\}}, and \texttt{accept}
promotes the Executor's answer verbatim \emph{by reference}, having no
field in which to \emph{author} one. Enforcement removes no capability ---
each function is still performed by one agent under the same budget --- at
the cost that a rejected emission can exhaust the regeneration budget. A
\textbf{context-restored control} repeats it with shared history restored;
matching the baseline exactly would require feeding raw prose back in,
dissolving the constraint that defines the condition.

\paragraph{Single-agent reference.} A budget-matched single-agent protocol
on the same problems, from a previously released corpus rather than this
campaign (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). Without it, ``collapse costs nothing''
cannot be told from ``the system was one agent all
along''~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}.

\subsection{Instrumentation and pre-registered analyses}
\label{subsec:prereg}

An intervention that never engaged cannot explain an outcome, so
engagement is measured rather than assumed: every
rejected-and-regenerated emission is logged with its reason, the
enforcement rate counts \textbf{role-boundary violations only}, and
answer-bearing calls are counted per condition. One instrumentation gap
escaped this discipline (\S\ref{subsec:logging}).

All five endpoints were fixed in writing and committed before any
intervention data existed; the full table, with what happened to each, is
in the supplement. Units are (problem, actor) pairs and all conditions run
the same problems, so every test is paired with bootstrap clustering by
problem. \textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on
accuracy --- is \emph{the} primary test of the original thesis}, and the
pre-registered Holm correction alters no verdict.

\paragraph{The pre-registered primary endpoint is null.}
\label{subsec:p4null}
\textbf{P4 is null in all three pairwise difference-in-differences, before
and after Holm --- every interval contains zero} --- and we report it as
the failure of the theory it was written to test. \textbf{Only one of the
three is an informative null}: a true interaction is plausibly
${\leq}0.040$ and only the gpt-oss$-$gemma-4 contrast can see that (MDE
$0.033$ at $80\%$ power), while the two Llama-3.3 contrasts (MDE $0.069$,
$0.075$ at $n=400$) \textbf{cannot detect an interaction of any plausible
size} and are uninformative rather than evidence of absence. Our
pre-declared not-submit condition was a null P4 \emph{and} a main effect
matching the published capability-equalizer direction; the pre-registered
effect matches no monotone direction, so that condition is met only under
the as-graded scoring \S\ref{subsec:enforce} shows to be survivorship.
\textbf{The role-structured-versus-role-free contrast this paper is
organized around was pre-registered as secondary and exploratory}, and is
labeled post-hoc everywhere. The \Pone{} manipulation objective --- that
enforcement would drive Planner \emph{computation}-abstention to
${\approx}1.0$ --- also \textbf{failed}, naming a clause the intervention
does not enforce: the schema forbids emitting an \emph{answer}, and
nothing stops a Planner writing $=1/27$. It was re-designated to answer
authorship. Exclusions were fixed in advance, including the rule that
decides \S\ref{subsec:enforce}: \textbf{unparseable trajectories count as
incorrect rather than dropped}.
