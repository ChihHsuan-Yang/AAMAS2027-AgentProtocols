\section{Intervention}
\label{sec:method}

To ask whether organization is load-bearing we vary it in both directions
from one baseline: remove it, and enforce it. All conditions share three
agents, the same interaction order
(Planner~$\rightarrow$~Executor~$\rightarrow$~Reviewer), message-passing
graph, total call budget, verifier and verifier prompt, retry budget,
decoding parameters (temperature $0$, identical \texttt{max\_tokens}),
stopping rule and grading; every trajectory in the frozen snapshot carries
the identical budget record --- a test of organization, not of scale.
\textbf{One channel differs by condition: feedback.} After a FAIL the
evaluator returns a non-leaking repair hint, which the role-structured and
enforced prompts receive through an advice slot. The role-free template has
no such slot: its agents are not told that, or why, an answer failed, and
see only that another cycle runs. The role-free condition is therefore
reported in two forms, without and with the feedback.

\paragraph{Role-structured \PER{} (baseline).} Role prompts as ordinarily
written: the organization as specified, including one clause the role-free
prompts lack --- in a cycle that receives a FAIL, the Reviewer must route
the work back to the Planner or Executor and may not approve.

\paragraph{Role-free without feedback.} Identical topology and
budget; all three agents get one byte-identical prompt: ``You are one of
three agents collaboratively solving this problem. Continue the work,''
the problem, the shared history, the agent's slot number, and a footer
asking for its current best answer in \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}} and
a closing \texttt{[Agree]} (ready to submit) or \texttt{[Continue]} ---
the conventions the role-structured Executor and Reviewer use. Removed is
every clause saying who does what, above all the Planner's \textsc{must
not}s on computing and answering. In both prose conditions the third
agent's \texttt{[Agree]} is approval (untagged counts as
\texttt{[Continue]}) and the latest boxed answer is submitted. Nothing in
this template carries the evaluator's verdict or hint.

\paragraph{Role-free with feedback.} The same role-free prompt, byte for byte, plus the evaluator's verdict and
repair hint --- the string the role-structured team receives --- shown to
all three agents as an \texttt{[Evaluator]} entry in the cycle after a FAIL.
It differs from the role-free condition only in feedback, and from the
role-structured one in roles, in the Reviewer clause above, and in how the
feedback is framed (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Structurally enforced.} Specialization is enforced by the
interface rather than by wording. The Planner emits JSON against
a schema with \emph{no answer field}, so a final answer is
unrepresentable; the Executor receives the parsed plan object and
the problem --- never the Planner's raw text --- and emits
\texttt{\{derivation, answer\}}; the Reviewer emits
\texttt{\{verdict, target\_span, critique\}}, and \texttt{accept}
promotes the Executor's answer verbatim \emph{by reference}, having no
field in which to \emph{author} one. Enforcement removes no capability ---
each function is still performed by one agent under the same budget --- at
the cost that a rejected emission can exhaust the regeneration budget. A
\emph{context-restored control} repeats it with shared history restored;
matching the baseline exactly would require feeding raw prose back in,
dissolving the constraint that defines the condition.

\paragraph{Single-agent reference.} A budget-matched single-agent protocol
on the same problems, from a previously released corpus rather than this
campaign (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). Without it, ``collapse costs nothing''
cannot be told from ``the system was one agent all
along''~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}.

\subsection{Instrumentation and pre-registered analyses}
\label{subsec:prereg}

An intervention that never engaged cannot explain an outcome, so
engagement is measured rather than assumed: every
rejected-and-regenerated emission is logged with its reason, the
enforcement rate counts role-boundary violations only, and
answer-bearing calls are counted per condition. One instrumentation gap
escaped this discipline (\S\ref{subsec:logging}).

All five endpoints were fixed in writing and committed before any
intervention data existed; the full table, with what happened to each, is
in the supplement. Units are (problem, actor) pairs and all conditions run
the same problems, so every test is paired with bootstrap clustering by
problem. P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on
accuracy --- is \emph{the} primary test of the original thesis, and the
pre-registered Holm correction alters no verdict.

\paragraph{The pre-registered primary endpoint is null.}
\label{subsec:p4null}
\textbf{P4 is null in all three pairwise difference-in-differences, before
and after Holm --- every interval contains zero} --- and we report it as
the failure of the theory it was written to test. Only one of the
three is an informative null: a true interaction is plausibly
${\leq}0.040$ and only the gpt-oss$-$gemma-4 contrast can see that (MDE
$0.033$ at $80\%$ power), while the two Llama-3.3 contrasts (MDE $0.069$,
$0.075$ at $n=400$) cannot detect an interaction of any plausible
size and are uninformative rather than evidence of absence. Our
pre-declared not-submit condition was a null P4 \emph{and} a main effect
in the published capability-equalizer direction. Under the pre-registered
scoring the effects are ordered by actor weakness but are not gains:
enforcement significantly \emph{lowers} gemma-4's accuracy ($-0.0225$, the
only per-actor contrast to survive Holm) and leaves the other two null, so
that condition is met only under the as-graded scoring
\S\ref{subsec:enforce} shows to be survivorship.
\textbf{The role-structured-versus-role-free contrast this paper is
organized around was pre-registered as a secondary, exploratory
replication of a published null}; splitting it into a first-pass and a
recovery phase is post hoc. The role-free condition with feedback is not
pre-registered either: it was added and run after the snapshot was frozen,
with its design and analysis committed in writing before any of its data
existed. The \Pone{} manipulation objective --- that
enforcement would drive Planner \emph{computation}-abstention to
${\approx}1.0$ --- also failed, naming a clause the intervention
does not enforce: the schema forbids emitting an \emph{answer}, and
nothing stops a Planner writing $=1/27$. It was re-designated to answer
authorship. Exclusions were fixed in advance, including the rule that
decides \S\ref{subsec:enforce}: \emph{unparseable trajectories count as
incorrect rather than dropped}.
