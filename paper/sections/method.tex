\section{Intervention}
\label{sec:method}

To ask whether organization is load-bearing we vary it in both directions
from one baseline: remove it, and enforce it. All conditions share three
agents, the same interaction order
(Planner~$\rightarrow$~Executor~$\rightarrow$~Reviewer), message-passing
graph, total call budget, verifier and verifier prompt, retry budget,
decoding parameters (temperature $0$, identical \texttt{max\_tokens}),
stopping rule and grading; every trajectory in the frozen snapshot carries
the identical budget record --- a test of organization, not of scale. One
asymmetry we state rather than smooth, because it is the one place the
conditions are not matched: the evaluator fires on Reviewer approval, so a
team that submits more \emph{consumes} more oracle verdicts even under an
identical cap. The role-free team draws $1.95$/$1.85$/$3.82$ evaluator calls
per trajectory against the role-structured team's $1.11$/$1.35$/$1.03$
(gemma-4 / gpt-oss / Llama-3.3), the enforced condition $1.20$/$1.24$/$1.43$.
\textbf{This runs against our result} and we report it for that reason: a
ground-truth verdict is the only external information in the loop, the
role-free team receives more of it in every actor, and it still recovers
less. An unmatched budget that favors the condition we find worse cannot
explain why it is worse. Realized actor calls run the other way and are
close ($6.76$ versus $5.87$, gemma-4).

\paragraph{Role-structured \PER{} (baseline).} Role prompts as ordinarily
written. The organization as specified.

\paragraph{Role-free (organization removed).} Identical topology and budget;
all three agents receive the same generic prompt (``You are one of three
agents solving this problem collaboratively; continue the work''), with role
names retained for routing only, so the pipeline still runs. This is
\emph{not} ``\PER{} minus role words'' but the same topology with no
division of labor specified at all.

\paragraph{Structurally enforced.} Specialization is enforced by the
interface rather than by wording. The \textbf{Planner} emits JSON against a
schema with \emph{no answer field}, so a final answer is unrepresentable and
free text is rejected and regenerated. The \textbf{Executor} receives the
parsed plan object and the problem --- never the Planner's raw text --- and
emits \texttt{\{derivation, answer\}}. The \textbf{Reviewer} emits
\texttt{\{verdict, target\_span, critique\}}, and \texttt{accept} promotes
the Executor's answer verbatim \emph{by reference}, having no field in which
to \emph{author} one. Enforcement removes no capability: planning,
computing, answering and critiquing are each still performed by one agent
under the same budget, at the cost that a rejected emission can exhaust the
regeneration budget. A \textbf{context-restored
control} repeats it with shared history restored in all three role
templates, schema, routing and budgets unchanged; matching the baseline
exactly would require feeding raw prose back in, dissolving the constraint
that defines the condition.

\paragraph{Single-agent reference.} A budget-matched single-agent protocol
on the same problems, from a previously released corpus rather than this
campaign (\S\ref{sec:experiments}), consuming more oracle feedback than
either team. It answers whether multi-agent execution earns its budget --- a
different question from which phase organization carries --- and without it
``collapse costs nothing'' cannot be told from ``the system was one agent
all along''~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}.

\subsection{Instrumentation and pre-registered analyses}
\label{subsec:prereg}

An intervention that never engaged cannot explain an outcome, so engagement
is measured rather than assumed. Every rejected-and-regenerated emission is
logged with its reason; the enforcement rate counts \textbf{role-boundary
violations only}, formatting coercions excluded by a definition fixed in
advance; answer-bearing calls are counted per condition. One instrumentation
gap escaped this discipline (\S\ref{subsec:logging}).

All endpoints were fixed in writing and committed before any intervention
data existed. Units are (problem, actor) pairs; all conditions run the same
problems, so every test is paired with bootstrap clustering by problem. The
endpoints are \textbf{P1} intervention $\rightarrow$ fidelity (also the
manipulation check); \textbf{P2} actor $\times$ intervention on fidelity;
\textbf{P3} intervention $\rightarrow$ accuracy; \textbf{P4} actor $\times$
intervention on accuracy, \emph{the primary test of the original thesis};
\textbf{P5} $\Delta$fidelity versus $\Delta$outcome, association only. The
pre-registered Holm correction is applied to the two families yielding
p-values and alters no verdict (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{The pre-registered primary endpoint is null.}
\label{subsec:p4null}
\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy --- is
null in all three pairwise difference-in-differences: $+0.0175$
$[-0.0050,+0.0412]$, $+0.0400$ $[+0.0000,+0.0794]$, $+0.0225$
$[-0.0188,+0.0631]$}, before and after Holm, and we report it as the failure
of the theory it was written to test. \textbf{Only one of the three is an
informative null.} The largest per-actor enforcement main effect is
$|{-}0.0225|$, so a true interaction is plausibly ${\leq}0.040$; only the
gpt-oss$-$gemma-4 contrast can see that (MDE $0.033$ at $80\%$ power,
$n=1{,}600$). The two Llama contrasts (MDE $0.069$, $0.075$ at $n=400$)
\textbf{cannot detect an interaction of any plausible size} and are
uninformative rather than evidence of absence. Our pre-declared not-submit
condition was a null P4 \emph{and} a main effect matching the published
capability-equalizer direction; the pre-registered effect matches no
monotone direction, so the condition is met only under the as-graded scoring
\S\ref{subsec:enforce} shows to be survivorship. \textbf{The
role-structured-versus-role-free contrast this paper is organized around was
pre-registered as secondary and exploratory}, and is labeled post-hoc
everywhere. The pre-registered \Pone{} manipulation objective --- that
enforcement would drive Planner \emph{computation}-abstention to
${\approx}1.0$ --- also \textbf{failed}, naming a clause the intervention
does not enforce: the schema forbids emitting an \emph{answer}, and nothing
stops a Planner writing $=1/27$. It was re-designated to answer authorship
(\S\ref{subsec:enforce}).

Exclusions were fixed in advance: \textbf{unparseable trajectories count as
incorrect rather than dropped} --- the rule that decides
\S\ref{subsec:enforce}; under enforcement the regenerated message is
analyzed and the rejection count reported; a condition failing its
manipulation check is reported as having failed it.
