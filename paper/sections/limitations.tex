\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{A logging defect limits what the duplication rate can mean.} Our
runner fell back to a response's reasoning field only when its content field
was empty, so a short answer carrying a long hidden reasoning trace was
logged as the short answer (\S\ref{subsec:logging}). \textbf{This affects
$84.7\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B messages and $97.3\%$ of its first passes}; the
flag meant to document it reports $5.7\%$. The campaign was \textbf{not}
re-run: the defect was found late, and the measures the paper's claims rest
on --- accuracy, graded from a final answer that was always present, and
functional collapse, read from answer assertions --- do not depend on the
discarded text. We accept in exchange that no point estimate of gpt-oss's
duplication rate is recoverable, that we cannot claim the hidden computation
was redundant, and that our similarity metrics for that cell describe only
the visible tip of what the model produced. The logger now captures reasoning
unconditionally, which does not repair data already collected.

\paragraph{The collapse validation is model adjudication, not human
annotation.} The 180-item validation in \S\ref{subsec:collapse} was performed
by a frontier language model against a rubric frozen before the first
judgement, with actor, arm, correctness and the automated label withheld. It
is \textbf{not ground truth}. There was a \textbf{single annotator and
therefore no inter-annotator agreement}; the judge's stability is estimated
from repeated independent calls instead. \textbf{Blinding is partial}: role
labels are retained inside the transcripts, because the question ``did this
agent perform a distinct role function'' is unanswerable without them, so a
determined judge could sometimes infer the arm from content even after we
neutralized the explicit role words. It covers one snapshot of one domain.
And precision is estimable only for gpt-oss, which has 14 flagged positives
in the sample against gemma-4's 3 and Llama-3.3's none, so we report it per
actor and never pool it.

\paragraph{Collapse is post-treatment.} Whether a trajectory collapses is an
outcome of the intervention, not a randomized factor, so conditioning on it
licenses no causal reading of collapse~$\rightarrow$~accuracy. The
unconditional arm contrasts, randomized by problem, are reported alongside as
the weaker but cleaner statement.

\paragraph{Provenance of the headline.} The pre-registered primary endpoint
(P4) is null, and the A-versus-B contrast this paper is organized around was
pre-registered as secondary and exploratory. It should be read as a
well-audited observation meriting pre-registered replication, not a confirmed
primary result. The pre-registered \Pone{} manipulation objective also
failed; we report that as a failed objective before reporting what we learned
from it.

\paragraph{Three actors, one organization, one domain.} The result rests on
three actors, on competition mathematics, in one
Planner--Executor--Reviewer organization. All three lose the division of
labor, but only two carry usable accuracy contrasts: Llama-3.3-70B was
launched late, reaches $n=125$, and scores $0.25$--$0.31$ here, so we use it
to corroborate the collapse rate only. With one significant accuracy loss
among three actors we can say the amount of organization lost does not
predict the cost --- a negative claim, and the one we make --- but not what
\emph{does} predict it. \S\ref{subsec:mechanism} proposes consensus
behavior; testing that requires actors selected for consensus behavior
rather than size, which this panel does not do.

\paragraph{The recovery loop is oracle-gated.} The evaluator that triggers
retries sees ground truth, so the mechanism claim does not establish that
role differentiation aids recovery under a self-assessed stopping rule
(\S\ref{subsec:mechanism}). Relatedly, stop-on-correct censors destructive
revision, so this design cannot measure how often collaboration destroys a
correct answer.

\paragraph{Arm~C$'$ is a partial context control.} Arm~C carries less context
than Arm~A by construction, because compact structured objects replace raw
transcripts. C$'$ restores shared history at fixed enforcement, licensing
the contrast ``the effect of adding context at fixed enforcement.'' It does
\emph{not} make C and A context-matched: that would require feeding raw prose
back in, dissolving the constraint that defines the arm.

\paragraph{Attrition is arm-specific.} Arm~C hard-fails on $7.9\%$ and
$5.9\%$ of trajectories against $0.000$ in Arms~A and B, because it rejects
unparseable structured output where the other arms tolerate truncated prose.
Dropping failures instead of applying the pre-registered rule would make
Arm~C look better by 7.1 and 8.0 points; both are reported. The structural
penalty for requiring structured output is confounded with the intervention
and cannot be separated here, and the same attrition is why the two grading
conventions disagree on C$'-$C for gemma-4 (\S\ref{subsec:p4null}).

\paragraph{Measurement blind spots.} Exact duplication (precision $1.000$,
recall $0.343$) undercounts near-identical outputs and says nothing about
semantic redundancy, which we do not claim to have measured. The functional
measure replacing it is also a proxy: it detects that every agent asserted
its own answer, not that their reasoning was the same, and it is
\textbf{inapplicable to Arms~C and C$'$}, where the schema forbids an answer
field. \Pone{} is blind to symbolic results carrying no digits and slightly
over-credits compliance; a variant closing this made agreement worse and was
rejected rather than silently adopted. It is also mechanically
anti-correlated with message length, which is why every computation claim
cites the length-robust density instead.

\paragraph{Power and the evaluator.} Interaction endpoints need roughly twice
the sample of a main effect for equal power, so a null on P4 is ``no large
interaction detected.'' The evaluator is one of the actor models under study,
so its independence from that actor's outputs is not established. Backends
have shown large accuracy differences for an identical cell; ours is pinned
and uniform.
