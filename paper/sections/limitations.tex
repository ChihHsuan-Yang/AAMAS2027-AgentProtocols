\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Evaluation paradigm and oracle-gated recovery.} The recovery loop
in our experiments is gated by an oracle. The evaluator triggering retries
sees the ground truth, so every reported recovery rests on a
ground-truth-informed failure signal rather than unaided self-correction.
Stopping at the correct answer also hides revisions that would have turned a
correct answer into a wrong one. An evaluator that can be queried multiple
times favors the team that queries it most. As shown in
\S\ref{subsec:mechanism}, when matched on the number of verdicts, the
role-free advantage disappears.

\paragraph{Scope, provenance, and experimental disclosures.} We test one
\PER{} organization, with an evaluator that is itself one of the models
under study. While the ordering returns across a second domain of college
science problems, the findings are limited to text-based reasoning tasks.
The feedback-matched condition and the forced-submission run were added
after the freeze and are outside the pre-registration. The forced-submission
run covers only one actor, one domain, and a single run, and it removes the
entire gate rather than only the clause forbidding approval after a failure.
The feedback-matched condition also differs in feedback presentation:
role-structured agents receive feedback inside role-specific instructions,
whereas role-free agents view it as a bare \texttt{[Evaluator]} entry.
Finally, the context-restored control is only a \emph{partial} control due
to one-sided attrition (\S\ref{subsec:enforce}), and our functional measure
only detects whether every agent asserted an answer, not whether their
reasoning was identical.

\paragraph{A scoring defect.} The automated runner graded the last
\emph{submitted} answer but recorded the last \emph{held} answer. For the
gemma-4 model on the Omni-MATH benchmark, the two disagree on $17\%$ of
role-structured first-pass-wrong trajectories, and on more in some other
cells (per-cell rates in the supplement). Recoding the affected trajectories
narrows the unadjusted recovery gap and shifts the pooled verdict-matched
difference to $+0.029$ $[+0.004,+0.054]$, which significantly favors the
role-structured team (\S\ref{subsec:mechanism}). The primary analysis keeps
the scoring rule that was fixed before the data were collected.
