\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The recovery loop is oracle-gated.} The evaluator triggering
retries sees ground truth and no cell recovers before the first
oracle verdict, so every recovery number is recovery \emph{given} a
ground-truth-informed failure signal: not unaided self-correction, and
stop-on-correct censors destructive revision. An oracle that can be queried six times
also favors the team that queries it most (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{The feedback-matched condition.} It was added after the
snapshot was frozen and is not pre-registered; a role-structured re-run on
$400$ problems shows no endpoint drift, but the two runs are separate. It
matches the role-structured condition on the feedback string, not on its
framing: role-structured agents receive it inside role-specific
instructions, including the Reviewer clause that forbids approval after a
FAIL, and role-free agents as a bare \texttt{[Evaluator]} entry. Running
the role-structured prompts without that clause would separate the
contract from the clause; we did not. On SciBench it was run for gemma-4 and
gpt-oss, the two actors with frozen SciBench cells.

\paragraph{A scoring defect that favors our result.} Beyond the
logging defect of \S\ref{subsec:logging}, the runner grades the last answer
\emph{submitted} but records the last answer \emph{held}; for gemma-4 these
disagree on $17\%$ of role-structured, $0\%$ of role-free and $13\%$ of
feedback-matched role-free first-pass-wrong trajectories (gpt-oss $16\%$/$7\%$, Llama-3.3
$6\%$/$0.4\%$) --- an error proportional to how much a team revises, the
behavior our headline is about. Recoding the $62$ trajectories (all cells)
whose held answer matched the key but was graded wrong (positive control:
$0$ of $10{,}734$), with the $3$ such in gemma-4's feedback-matched run,
moves gemma-4's role-structured recovery to $0.275$ and the feedback-matched
role-free one to $0.618$; we keep the pre-registered outcome as primary, since changing it after seeing
which way it moves is what preregistration prevents.

\paragraph{Scope, provenance, and blind spots.} Enforcement's $8$--$17\%$
one-sided attrition (\S\ref{subsec:enforce}) is why its as-graded gain is
survivorship, why its recovery gain resolves only for Llama-3.3, and why
the context-restored condition is only a \emph{partial} control. We test
one \PER{} organization, with an evaluator that is itself one of the
actors under study; the second domain (\S\ref{subsec:xdomain}) keeps the
role-free prompt byte-identical but adapts the role-structured and
\emph{evaluator} prompts, so it replicates the mechanism under a matched
protocol, not identical text. Finally, the functional measure
detects that every agent asserted an answer, not that their reasoning was
the same.
