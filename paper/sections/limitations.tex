\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The recovery loop is oracle-gated.} The evaluator that triggers
retries sees ground truth, and \textbf{no cell in the study recovers before
the first oracle verdict}, so every recovery number is recovery \emph{given} a
ground-truth-informed failure signal --- none of this is unaided multi-agent
self-correction. Stop-on-correct also censors destructive revision, so this
design cannot measure how often collaboration destroys a correct answer.

\paragraph{Two instrument defects, both disclosed rather than repaired.}
First, a \textbf{logging defect}: the runner fell back to a response's
reasoning field only when its content field was empty, so a short answer
carrying a long hidden reasoning trace was logged as the short answer,
affecting $93.4\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B first passes. This bounds two things, not
one: no point estimate of that actor's duplication rate is recoverable,
\emph{and} its 37-character planner cannot be interpreted as a behavior ---
at $2.85$ visible characters per second against $195$ in its own Arm~A, the
work was done and discarded, so we report it as uninstrumented, not
abstaining. Second, a \textbf{scoring defect}: the runner records the grade of
the last answer \emph{submitted} while also recording the answer the team last
\emph{held}, and these disagree on $17\%$ of Arm~A and $0\%$ of Arm~B
first-pass-wrong trajectories --- a measurement error whose size is
proportional to how much an arm revises, which is the behavior our headline is
about. It runs \emph{against} our claim: recoding the 54 trajectories whose
held answer exactly matches gold while recorded wrong (positive control: $0$
of $10{,}734$ non-mismatched rows show that pattern) makes every recovery gap
we report wider. We keep the pre-registered outcome as primary because
changing it after seeing which way it moves is what preregistration exists to
prevent. Neither defect was fixed mid-campaign, which would have split the
corpus under two outcome definitions.

\paragraph{Every conditional analysis carries a post-treatment label.}
Conditioning on first-pass-wrong, on cycles used, on accumulated context and
on whether a trajectory collapsed all condition on outcomes of the
intervention. The recovery decomposition is a decomposition, not a causal
mediation; the unconditional arm contrasts, randomized by problem, are
reported alongside as the weaker but cleaner statement.

\paragraph{Grader noise bounds two of three accuracy contrasts.} Repeated
identical evaluator prompts at temperature $0$ contradict themselves on $24$
of $2{,}791$ distinct graded strings ($0.0086$ group-level;
$0.0020$--$0.0086$ across estimators). Only gemma-4's B$-$A clears that floor,
by $3.9\times$; \textbf{gpt-oss's and Llama-3.3's do not}, so those
preregistered nulls are nulls the instrument could not have resolved either
way. The recovery and lock-in contrasts are far above any version of the
floor.

\paragraph{The collapse validation is model adjudication, not human
annotation.} It used a frontier language model against a rubric frozen before
the first judgement, with actor, arm, correctness and the automated label
withheld. It is \textbf{not ground truth}: a single annotator, so no
inter-annotator agreement, with stability estimated from repeated independent
calls ($0.953$ on the headline question). Blinding is partial, since role
labels must remain in the transcripts for the question to be answerable.
Precision for the byte metric is estimable only where it fires (gpt-oss 14
positives, gemma-4 3, Llama-3.3 none), so we report it per actor and never
pool it. The Arm~C reading is partly mechanical: its validator forbids an
answer field, so some restored ``division of labor'' is the constraint by
construction.

\paragraph{Arm~C's attrition is one-sided and its as-graded gain is
survivorship.} Arm~C leaves $8.0\%$/$9.9\%$/$17.0\%$ of trajectories ungraded
against $0.0\%$/$0.1\%$/$0.7\%$ in Arm~A. Dropping them turns a significant
negative and two nulls into three significant positives ordered by actor
weakness --- the published capability-equalizer pattern, and the exact pattern
the preregistration told us not to submit on. Both scorings are in
\S\ref{subsec:enforce} with the attrition beside them; the pre-registered one
is primary. Arm~C$'$ is likewise only a \emph{partial} context control:
matching C to A exactly would require feeding raw prose back in, dissolving
the constraint that defines the arm.

\paragraph{Provenance of the headline, and of the single-agent control.} The
pre-registered primary endpoint (P4) is null, and the A-versus-B contrast this
paper is organized around was pre-registered as secondary and exploratory: an
observation meriting pre-registered replication, not a confirmed primary
result. The pre-registered \Pone{} manipulation objective also failed and is
reported as such. The single-agent comparator is drawn from a previously
released corpus rather than this campaign (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Three actors, one organization, one domain, one evaluator.} All
three actors lose the division of labor, but \textbf{Llama-3.3 does not
replicate the recovery result}: at $0.307$ first-pass accuracy it recovers at
$0.015$ in both arms, a floor effect rather than a null, so the recovery claim
rests on two actors, one domain and one Planner--Executor--Reviewer
organization. The evaluator is one of the actor models under study, so its
independence from that actor's outputs is not established.

\paragraph{Measurement blind spots.} Exact duplication undercounts
near-identical outputs and says nothing about semantic redundancy, which we do
not claim to have measured. The functional measure replacing it detects that
every agent asserted its own answer, not that their reasoning was the same,
and is inapplicable to Arms~C/C$'$. Its Arm~A level is marker-dependent
($0.000$--$0.892$ across three definitions) even though the A-vs-B ordering is
not, so the Arm~A column is ``low,'' not a point estimate. \Pone{} is blind to
symbolic results carrying no digits and is mechanically anti-correlated with
message length, which is why every computation claim cites density instead.
