\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The recovery loop is oracle-gated.} The evaluator that triggers
retries sees ground truth, and \textbf{no cell in the study recovers before
the first oracle verdict}, so every recovery number is recovery \emph{given}
a ground-truth-informed failure signal --- none of this is unaided
multi-agent self-correction. Stop-on-correct also censors destructive
revision, so this design cannot measure how often collaboration destroys a
correct answer.

\paragraph{Two instrument defects, both disclosed rather than repaired.}
First, a \textbf{logging defect}: the runner fell back to a response's
reasoning field only when its content field was empty, so a short answer
carrying a long hidden reasoning trace was logged as the short answer,
affecting $93.4\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B first passes. This bounds two things, not
one: no point estimate of that actor's duplication rate is recoverable,
\emph{and} its 37-character planner cannot be interpreted as a behavior ---
at $2.85$ visible characters per second against $195$ in its own Arm~A, the
work was done and discarded, so we report it as uninstrumented, not
abstaining. Second, a \textbf{scoring defect}: the runner records the grade of
the last answer \emph{submitted} while also recording the answer the team last
\emph{held}, and these disagree on $17\%$ of Arm~A and $0\%$ of Arm~B
first-pass-wrong trajectories --- a measurement error whose size is
proportional to how much an arm revises, which is the behavior our headline is
about. It runs \emph{against} our claim: recoding the 54 trajectories whose
held answer exactly matches gold while recorded wrong (positive control: $0$
of $10{,}734$ non-mismatched rows show that pattern) makes every recovery gap
we report wider. We keep the pre-registered outcome as primary because
changing it after seeing which way it moves is what preregistration exists to
prevent. Neither defect was fixed mid-campaign, which would have split the
corpus under two outcome definitions.

\paragraph{Every conditional analysis carries a post-treatment label.}
Conditioning on first-pass-wrong, on cycles used, on accumulated context and
on whether a trajectory collapsed all condition on outcomes of the
intervention. The recovery decomposition is a decomposition, not a causal
mediation; the unconditional arm contrasts, randomized by problem, are
reported alongside as the weaker but cleaner statement.

\paragraph{Grader noise bounds two of three accuracy contrasts.} Repeated
identical evaluator prompts at temperature $0$ contradict themselves on $20$
of $2{,}790$ distinct graded strings ($0.0072$ group-level;
$0.0017$--$0.0072$ across estimators). We do not divide an effect size by
that rate --- the two are different quantities, and grader noise largely
cancels in a paired contrast --- but propagate it into the bootstrap: only
gemma-4's B$-$A survives ($[-0.0494,-0.0175]$); \textbf{gpt-oss's and
Llama-3.3's do not}, so those preregistered nulls are nulls the instrument
could not have resolved either way. The recovery contrasts are far larger
than any version of the rate.

\paragraph{The collapse validation is model adjudication, not human
annotation, and not condition-blind.} It used a frontier language model
against a rubric frozen before the first judgement, with actor, arm,
correctness and the automated label withheld. It is \textbf{not ground
truth}: a single annotator, so no inter-annotator agreement, with stability
estimated from repeated independent calls ($0.953$ on the headline question).
\textbf{It is also not condition-blind} (\S\ref{subsec:collapse}): role-token
presence separates Arm~A from Arm~B at $p=1.5\times10^{-24}$ and output
format separates Arm~C, so a judge could infer the arm without reading
content. This is why A1's arm contrast rests on the mechanical measures and
the adjudication is reported only as an outcome-blind second opinion.
Precision for the byte metric is estimable only where it fires (gpt-oss 14
positives, gemma-4 3, Llama-3.3 none), so we report it per actor and never
pool it. The Arm~C reading is partly mechanical: its validator forbids an
answer field, so some restored ``division of labor'' is the constraint by
construction.

\paragraph{Arm~C's attrition is one-sided and its as-graded gain is
survivorship.} Arm~C leaves $8.0\%$/$9.9\%$/$17.0\%$ of trajectories
ungraded against $0.0\%$/$0.1\%$/$0.7\%$ in Arm~A. Dropping them turns a
significant negative and two nulls into three significant positives ordered
by actor weakness --- the published capability-equalizer pattern, and the
exact pattern the preregistration told us not to submit on. Both scorings are
in \S\ref{subsec:enforce} with the attrition beside them; the pre-registered
one is primary. The same attrition is why Arm~C's recovery gain is resolvable
only for Llama-3.3: restoring the $173$ trajectories that received a wrong
first verdict and then died in regeneration costs gemma-4 and gpt-oss their
paired significance ($p=0.298$ and $p=0.105$). Arm~C$'$ is likewise only a \emph{partial} context control:
matching C to A exactly would require feeding raw prose back in, dissolving
the constraint that defines the arm.

\paragraph{Provenance of the headline, and of the single-agent control.} The
pre-registered primary endpoint (P4) is null, and the A-versus-B contrast
this paper is organized around was pre-registered as secondary and
exploratory: an observation meriting pre-registered replication, not a
confirmed primary result. The pre-registered \Pone{} manipulation objective
also failed and is reported as such. The single-agent comparator is drawn
from a previously released corpus rather than this campaign
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}), and \textbf{its two actor arms were produced under
different source commits}, which makes it a directional guard only and not
evidence about how the gap differs between actors.

\paragraph{Three actors, one organization, one domain, one evaluator.} All
three actors lose the division of labor, but \textbf{Llama-3.3 does not
replicate the recovery result}: it recovers at $0.015$ in both arms. This is
a genuine null specific to the role manipulation and \textbf{not} a floor
effect --- enforcement raises the same actor's recovery to $0.139$
($p=3.8\times10^{-6}$), so the repair channel exists and role labels simply
do not open it here. The test has ${\approx}80\%$ power against an effect of
$0.04$--$0.05$ where gemma-4's is $0.171$, making it informative against a
gemma-sized effect and uninformative below ${\approx}0.04$. The recovery
claim therefore rests on two actors, one domain and one
Planner--Executor--Reviewer organization. The evaluator is one of the actor models under study, so its
independence from that actor's outputs is not established.

\paragraph{Measurement blind spots.} Exact duplication undercounts
near-identical outputs and says nothing about semantic redundancy, which we
do not claim to have measured. The functional measure replacing it detects
that every agent asserted its own answer, not that their reasoning was the
same, and is inapplicable to Arms~C/C$'$. Its Arm~A level is marker-dependent
($0.000$--$0.892$ across three definitions) even though the A-vs-B ordering
is not, so the Arm~A column is ``low,'' not a point estimate. \Pone{} is
blind to symbolic results carrying no digits and is mechanically
anti-correlated with message length, which is why every computation claim
cites density instead.
