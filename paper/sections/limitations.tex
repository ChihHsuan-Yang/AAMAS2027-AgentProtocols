\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The recovery loop is oracle-gated.} The evaluator triggering
retries sees ground truth and \textbf{no cell recovers before the first
oracle verdict}, so every recovery number is recovery \emph{given} a
ground-truth-informed failure signal: not unaided self-correction, and
stop-on-correct censors destructive revision.

\paragraph{Two instrument defects, disclosed rather than repaired.} The
\textbf{logging defect} of \S\ref{subsec:logging} bounds two things: no
point estimate of gpt-oss's duplication rate is recoverable, \emph{and} its
37-character planner cannot be read as a behavior. The \textbf{scoring
defect}: the runner grades the last answer \emph{submitted} but records the
last answer \emph{held}, and these disagree on $17\%$ of role-structured
and $0\%$ of role-free first-pass-wrong trajectories --- an error
proportional to how much a team revises, the behavior our headline is
about. It runs \emph{against} us: recoding the $62$ affected trajectories
(positive control: $0$ of $10{,}734$ non-mismatched rows) widens every
recovery gap. We keep the pre-registered outcome as primary; changing it
after seeing which way it moves is what preregistration prevents.

\paragraph{Every conditional analysis carries a post-treatment label.}
First-pass-wrong, cycles used, accumulated context and collapse are all
outcomes of the intervention. The recovery decomposition is a decomposition,
not a causal mediation --- except for gemma-4, whose recovery contrast is
algebraically the intent-to-treat effect (\S\ref{subsec:recovery}). Grader
noise bounds the other two accuracy contrasts, which are nulls the
instrument could not resolve rather than measured absences.

\paragraph{The collapse validation is model adjudication, not human
annotation, and not condition-blind.} It is \textbf{not ground truth}: one
annotator, no inter-annotator agreement, stability from repeated calls
($0.953$); \textbf{nor is it condition-blind} (\S\ref{subsec:collapse}),
which is why the collapse claim rests on the mechanical measures. Precision
for the byte metric is estimable only where it fires, so we never pool it.
The enforced reading is partly mechanical: its validator forbids an answer
field, so some restored ``division of labor'' is the constraint.

\paragraph{Enforcement attrition is one-sided and its as-graded gain is
survivorship.} Enforcement leaves $8.0\%$/$9.9\%$/$17.0\%$ of trajectories
ungraded against $0.0\%$/$0.1\%$/$0.7\%$ in the baseline; dropping them
turns a significant negative and two nulls into three significant positives
ordered by actor weakness --- the capability-equalizer pattern the
preregistration told us not to submit on. Both scorings are in
\S\ref{subsec:enforce}, the pre-registered one primary. That attrition is
also why the enforced recovery gain is resolvable only for Llama-3.3 and
why its columns do not compose into its accuracy row. The context-restored
condition is only a \emph{partial} control.

\paragraph{Provenance, scope, and blind spots.} The pre-registered primary
endpoint (P4) is null and the \Pone{} manipulation objective failed
(\S\ref{subsec:prereg}); the contrast this paper is organized around was
pre-registered as secondary and exploratory --- an observation meriting
replication, not a confirmed result --- and the single-agent comparator is a
directional guard only, on a different question
(\S\ref{subsec:notsingle}). \textbf{All three actors lose the division of
labor, but the recovery result is carried by gemma-4 alone}: the claim rests
on one clean actor and one \PER{} organization, with the evaluator itself
one of the actor models under study, and \S\ref{subsec:xdomain} extends it
to a second domain for gemma-4 only, with a baseline prompt that is
domain-adapted rather than byte-identical. Finally, the functional measure
detects that every agent asserted an answer, not that their reasoning was
the same.
