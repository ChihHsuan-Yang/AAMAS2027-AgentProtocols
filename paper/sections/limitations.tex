\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{A logging defect limits what the duplication rate can mean.} Our
runner fell back to a response's reasoning field only when its content field
was empty, so a short answer carrying a long hidden reasoning trace was
logged as the short answer, affecting \textbf{$97.3\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B first
passes} (\S\ref{subsec:logging}). The campaign was \textbf{not} re-run: the
defect was found late, and the measures the paper's claims rest on ---
accuracy, graded from a final answer that was always present, and functional
collapse, read from answer assertions --- do not depend on the discarded
text. We accept in exchange that no point estimate of gpt-oss's duplication
rate is recoverable, that we cannot claim the hidden computation was
redundant, and that our similarity metrics for that cell describe only the
visible tip of what the model produced.

\paragraph{The collapse validation is model adjudication, not human
annotation.} The 180-item validation in \S\ref{subsec:collapse} used a
frontier language model against a rubric frozen before the first judgement,
with actor, arm, correctness and the automated label withheld. It is
\textbf{not ground truth}. There was a \textbf{single annotator and
therefore no inter-annotator agreement}; the judge's stability is estimated
from repeated independent calls instead. \textbf{Blinding is partial}: role
labels are retained in the transcripts, because ``did this agent perform a
distinct role function'' is unanswerable without them, so a determined judge
could sometimes infer the arm from content even after we neutralized the
explicit role words. It covers one snapshot of one domain, and precision is
estimable only for gpt-oss (14 flagged positives against gemma-4's 3 and
Llama-3.3's none), so we report it per actor and never pool it.

\paragraph{Collapse is post-treatment.} Whether a trajectory collapses is an
outcome of the intervention, not a randomized factor, so conditioning on it
licenses no causal reading of collapse~$\rightarrow$~accuracy. The
unconditional arm contrasts, randomized by problem, are reported alongside as
the weaker but cleaner statement.

\paragraph{Provenance of the headline.} The pre-registered primary endpoint
(P4) is null, and the A-versus-B contrast this paper is organized around was
pre-registered as secondary and exploratory: a well-audited observation
meriting pre-registered replication, not a confirmed primary result. The
pre-registered \Pone{} manipulation objective also failed; we report that as
a failed objective before what we learned from it.

\paragraph{Three actors, one organization, one domain.} The result rests on
three actors, on competition mathematics, in one
Planner--Executor--Reviewer organization. All three lose the division of
labor, but only two carry usable accuracy contrasts: Llama-3.3-70B was
launched late, reaches $n=125$, and scores $0.25$--$0.31$ here, so we use it
to corroborate the collapse rate only. With one significant accuracy loss
among three actors we can say the amount of organization lost does not
predict the cost --- a negative claim, and the one we make --- but not what
\emph{does}. Testing \S\ref{subsec:mechanism}'s consensus-behavior proposal
needs actors selected for that behavior rather than size.

\paragraph{The recovery loop is oracle-gated.} The evaluator that triggers
retries sees ground truth, so the mechanism claim does not establish that
role differentiation aids recovery under a self-assessed stopping rule; and
stop-on-correct censors destructive revision, so this design cannot measure
how often collaboration destroys a correct answer.

\paragraph{Arm~C$'$ is a partial context control.} It restores shared history
at fixed enforcement but does \emph{not} make C and A context-matched: that
would require feeding raw prose back in, dissolving the constraint that
defines the arm.

\paragraph{Attrition is arm-specific.} Arm~C hard-fails on $7.9\%$ and
$5.9\%$ of trajectories against $0.000$ in Arms~A and B, because it rejects
unparseable structured output where the other arms tolerate truncated prose.
Dropping failures rather than applying the pre-registered rule would make
Arm~C look better by 7.1 and 8.0 points; both are reported. That penalty is
confounded with the intervention, and the same attrition is why the grading
conventions disagree on C$'-$C for gemma-4 (\S\ref{subsec:p4null}).

\paragraph{Measurement blind spots.} Exact duplication undercounts
near-identical outputs and says nothing about semantic redundancy, which we
do not claim to have measured. The functional measure replacing it is also a
proxy: it detects that every agent asserted its own answer, not that their
reasoning was the same, and it is \textbf{inapplicable to Arms~C and C$'$},
where the schema forbids an answer field. \Pone{} is blind to symbolic
results carrying no digits, slightly over-credits compliance, and is
mechanically anti-correlated with message length --- which is why every
computation claim cites the length-robust density instead.

\paragraph{Power and the evaluator.} Interaction endpoints need roughly twice
the sample of a main effect for equal power, so a null on P4 is ``no large
interaction detected.'' The evaluator is one of the actor models under study,
so its independence from that actor's outputs is not established. Backends
have shown large accuracy differences for an identical cell; ours is pinned.
