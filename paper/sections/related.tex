\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Four literatures bear on this result, and most of what we might have claimed
belongs to one of them. We state what is owned before what is left.

\paragraph{Role specialization and role drift.} That prompted roles are not
reliably obeyed is established: Cemri et al.~\cite{cemri2025why} name
disobeying the role specification a failure mode from 1{,}600+ annotated
traces; Cao et al.~\cite{cao2026modules} give the training-time account of
\emph{role drift}; Orogat et al.~\cite{orogat2026architectural} find
prompt-level role conditioning does not induce the intended specialization;
Patel~\cite{patel2026representational} measures collapse as near-identical
rationales, while Kandoussi~\cite{kandoussi2026whoami} reports
differentiation arising \emph{without} assigned roles. Enforcement-side work
makes role scope structural rather than
advisory~\cite{stettler2026progressive,ge2026roles}. \emph{Owned:} role
collapse as an observation, and the decoupling of a role-quality metric from
task performance~\cite{cao2026modules,yang2026precise}. We claim neither.

\paragraph{Redundancy, and the parity we do not claim.} Dylan et
al.~\cite{dylan2026equal} hold total LLM calls fixed and find an evolved
Planner--Executor--Critic team no better than an evolved single agent
($p=0.80$). \emph{Owned:} \textbf{first-pass parity under role ablation.} Our R2
is our control condition, not a finding, and only a \emph{partial}
replication of their null: gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are flat, but gpt-oss
\emph{improves} by $+0.0503$ with an interval excluding zero. Zhang et
al.~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} argue reported multi-agent gains are setup
artifacts of structural collapse --- an objection aimed exactly at our Arm~B,
which is why \S\ref{subsec:notsingle} runs the single-agent control and
reports that it cuts \emph{both} ways; Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2026agentslimming} prune redundant agents on the same premise.

\paragraph{Failure to revise, and answer lock-in.} That models restate answers
under external correction is established for complete verifier
feedback~\cite{jiang2026exactfeedback} and binary
backprompts~\cite{stechly2024selfverification}; the phenomenon already has a
name, \textbf{Degeneration-of-Thought}~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and belief
entrenchment is studied directly~\cite{oh2026entrenchment}. Huang et
al.~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect} are frequently misread here, so we are
explicit: their
\emph{oracle} condition, which uses the correct label as a stopping rule,
\textbf{improves} accuracy ($75.9 \to 84.3$ on GSM8K); the \emph{intrinsic}
condition is the one that degrades, and they separately report the model
retaining its initial answer $74.7\%$ of the time. Our loop is oracle-gated
like theirs. Closest to our lever is Chen et al.~\cite{chen2026relabeling},
who hold an erroneous claim byte-identical and vary only the \emph{role
label} it carries, moving explicit-correction rate $23$--$93$pp --- the
strongest published evidence that a role label is causal for error
correction, but \textbf{single-agent}, with chat-template message roles
rather than agent personas, and no recovery conditional, diversity measure or
budget matching. \emph{Owned:} the failure, the name, and recovery-after-error as a
metric~\cite{jiang2026exactfeedback,liang2024divergent}. Not owned is the
\textbf{inversion}: multi-agency is the prescribed cure for
Degeneration-of-Thought, and we show a multi-agent system without role
differentiation does not escape it --- the same oracle-gated loop is worth $+0.044$ to a
role-labeled team and $+0.010$ to the identical team with the labels
removed.

\paragraph{Premature consensus, which we test rather than propose.} Early
convergence is a widely proposed explanation for multi-agent failure and
sustaining diversity a widely proposed remedy~\cite{oh2026entrenchment};
self-correction's limits are surveyed by~\cite{kamoi2024when}. \emph{We
propose neither explanation nor remedy.} \S\ref{subsec:mechanism} treats
premature stopping as a \textbf{standing hypothesis and rejects it} on
matched realized budget. Our lever is the role \emph{label}, not a diversity
intervention --- and one actor's content diversity runs opposite to its
recovery, which is why we do not call diversity the active ingredient.

\paragraph{Coordination versus capability.}
Canedo~\cite{canedo2026architecture} frames structured specification as a
\emph{capability equalizer} whose value is inversely proportional to model
strength; \cite{yu2026bottleneck} reaches the same prediction from theory;
Dochkina~\cite{dochkina2026drop} argues the complementary case. Together they
predict one monotone ordering. Our Arm~C reproduces that ordering exactly ---
\emph{but only under a scoring rule that drops $8$--$17\%$ of trajectories},
and under our pre-registered rule the ordering disappears
(\S\ref{subsec:enforce}). We report both because a reader who re-derives
either needs to find the other.

\paragraph{The gap, stated narrowly.} Prior work establishes that roles are
often not obeyed, that role ablation leaves first-pass accuracy intact, that
models fail to revise under external feedback, and that structure tends to
help weaker models. Missing is the \emph{phase} decomposition: a
budget-matched, two-directional role intervention measuring the organization
directly and asking which stage of the work it carries. That the answer is
``not the first answer, but the recovery'' --- with the standing mechanism
refuted --- is what we contribute.

\paragraph{Studies of the same trace corpus.} Three prior studies analyze the
corpus this work instruments~\cite{jiang2026confidence,yang2026protocol,%
yang2026precise}, and the single-agent control of \S\ref{subsec:notsingle}
is drawn from its released form. Two are purely observational; the third runs
within-\PER{} interventions that hold model family, reviewer backbone and
budget fixed while varying how critique \emph{reaches} the
solver~\cite{yang2026precise}. \textbf{None manipulates the role structure
itself}, none measures whether the specified division of labor is realized,
and the controlled arms of \S\ref{sec:method} do not exist in that corpus.
