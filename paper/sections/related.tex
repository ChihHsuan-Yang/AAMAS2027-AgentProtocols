\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{The question predates the LLM literature that reopened it.}
Organization-centred MAS gave roles a formal existence independent of
their occupants: Moise$^+$ separates a \emph{deontic} specification ---
what a role-filler is obliged and permitted to do --- from the structural
and functional ones~\cite{hubner2002moise}, and OperA makes role adoption
an explicit social contract~\cite{dignum2004opera}. Both treat \emph{when
a role specification binds} as the design problem and answer it with
enforcement outside the agent. An LLM role prompt is the degenerate case: a
deontic clause in prose, with no middleware to make it bind and no record
of whether it did. Our baseline is that case measured; our enforced
condition returns the binding to the interface.

\paragraph{Role specialization and role drift.} That prompted roles are not
reliably obeyed is established: Cemri et al.~\cite{cemri2025why} name
disobeying the role specification a failure mode across 1{,}600+ annotated
traces, Cao et al.~\cite{cao2026modules} give the training-time account of
\emph{role drift}, Orogat et al.~\cite{orogat2026architectural} find
role-label conditioning does not induce the intended
specialization, Patel~\cite{patel2026representational} measures collapse
as near-identical rationales, Kandoussi~\cite{kandoussi2026whoami} reports
differentiation arising \emph{without} assigned roles, and
enforcement-side work makes role scope structural rather than
advisory~\cite{stettler2026progressive,ge2026roles}. \emph{Owned:} role
collapse, and the decoupling of role quality from task
performance~\cite{cao2026modules,yang2026precise}.

\paragraph{Redundancy, and the parity we do not claim.} Dylan et
al.~\cite{dylan2026equal} hold total LLM calls fixed and find an evolved
Planner--Executor--Critic team no better than an evolved single agent
($p=0.80$). \emph{Owned:} first-pass parity under role ablation,
which we replicate only in part, since gpt-oss \emph{improves} by $+0.0503$ with
an interval excluding zero. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} argue
gains of multi-agent efficiency methods often arise where one agent
suffices ---
an objection aimed at our role-free condition, which is why
\S\ref{subsec:notsingle} runs that control and reports that it cuts
\emph{both} ways; Chen et al.~\cite{chen2026agentslimming} prune redundant
agents on the same premise.

\paragraph{Failure to revise, and answer lock-in.} That models repeat
earlier outputs under exact verifier feedback is
established~\cite{jiang2026exactfeedback}; under self-reflection the
failure has a name,
Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and belief
entrenchment is studied directly~\cite{oh2026entrenchment}. Huang et
al.~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect} are frequently misread here: their
\emph{oracle} condition, which uses the correct label as a stopping rule,
\emph{improves} accuracy ($75.9 \to 84.3$ on GSM8K) and only the
\emph{intrinsic} condition
degrades~\cite[see also][]{stechly2024selfverification}. Our loop is
oracle-gated like theirs.
Closest to our lever is Chen et al.~\cite{chen2026relabeling}, who hold an
erroneous claim byte-identical and vary only the \emph{role label} it
carries, moving explicit-correction rate $23$--$93$pp --- the strongest
published evidence that a role label is causal for error correction, but
single-agent, with chat-template message roles rather than agent
personas, and no recovery conditional or budget matching. \emph{Owned:} the
failure and the
name~\cite{jiang2026exactfeedback,liang2024divergent}. What we add is the
multi-agent case: a role-free team repeats itself exactly as
Degeneration-of-Thought describes when the verdict does not reach it, and
stops when it does --- the repetition follows the information, not the
organization.

\paragraph{Premature consensus, which we test rather than propose.} Early
convergence is a widely proposed explanation for multi-agent failure and
sustaining diversity a widely proposed
remedy~\cite[e.g.,][]{oh2026entrenchment};
self-correction's limits are surveyed by~\cite{kamoi2024when}. \emph{We
propose neither.} The team in our data that looks most like premature
consensus --- approving and resubmitting one answer until the budget runs
out --- is the one whose prompts never carried the verdict; told, the same
team revises. Our lever is the role \emph{contract}, not a diversity
intervention, and one actor's content diversity runs opposite to its
recovery, which is why we do not call diversity the active ingredient.

\paragraph{Coordination versus capability.}
Canedo~\cite{canedo2026architecture} frames structured specification as a
\emph{capability equalizer} whose value falls with model strength;
\cite{yu2026bottleneck} reaches the same prediction from theory and
Dochkina~\cite{dochkina2026drop} the complementary case. Our enforced
condition reproduces that direction --- \emph{but only
under a scoring rule that drops $8$--$17\%$ of trajectories}, and under
our pre-registered rule it disappears (\S\ref{subsec:enforce}). We report
both, because a reader who re-derives either needs to find the other.

\paragraph{What is open, stated narrowly.} These literatures establish four
things --- roles are often not obeyed; deleting them leaves first-pass
accuracy intact; models fail to revise under external feedback; structure
tends to help weaker models --- each about a \emph{different stage of the
work}, and no study holds them in one frame, so the field has an implicit
synthesis (role prompts are decorative) that no single experiment has
tested. \textbf{It has not been tested because the two findings most
responsible for it were measured in different places}: parity at the first
answer, failure-to-revise after feedback and almost always single-agent,
where there is no organization to ablate. Nothing licenses carrying parity
across that phase boundary, and nothing has stopped the field from doing
so.

\paragraph{Prior analyses of a released corpus.} Three studies analyze a
publicly released multi-agent trace corpus on this
benchmark~\cite{jiang2026confidence,yang2026protocol,yang2026precise},
from which our single-agent comparator is also drawn; two are
observational, the third varies how critique \emph{reaches} the
solver~\cite{yang2026precise}. \textbf{None manipulates the role structure
itself}, none measures whether the specified division of labor is
realized, and the controlled conditions of \S\ref{sec:method} do not
exist there.
