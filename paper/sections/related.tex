\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Three literatures bear on when organization is load-bearing; the gap is
stated at the end.

\paragraph{Role specialization and role drift.}
That prompted roles are not reliably obeyed is established. Cemri et
al.~\cite{cemri2025why} derive MAST from 1{,}600+ annotated traces, with
disobeying the role specification a named failure mode. Cao et
al.~\cite{cao2026modules} give the training-time account --- \emph{role
drift}, where modules preserve end-task performance while deviating from
assigned roles, and 86\% of one pipeline's apparent RL gain vanishes once the
decomposer is held to its role --- while Orogat et
al.~\cite{orogat2026architectural} find prompt-level role conditioning does
not induce the intended specialization. Patel~\cite{patel2026representational}
measures the phenomenon we call collapse directly, reporting near-identical
rationales across role-prompted committee members;
Kandoussi~\cite{kandoussi2026whoami} reports the converse, differentiation
arising \emph{without} assigned roles. Enforcement-side work makes role scope
structural rather than advisory~\cite{stettler2026progressive,ge2026roles},
though the behavioral payload remains a prompt and no agent is structurally
prevented from answering. Barrak~\cite{barrak2025traceability} reports
configuration-dependent deltas across three LLMs in this architecture --- an
organization~$\times$~actor interaction present in the data but never tested
as such.

\emph{What is already owned here.} ``A role-quality metric can decouple from
task performance'' is not available as a contribution: Cao et
al.~\cite{cao2026modules} own it for training-time drift and Yang et
al.~\cite{yang2026precise} for this architecture and one of these actors, on
the same corpus this work instruments. We do not claim the decoupling
itself.

\paragraph{Redundancy and pruning in multi-agent systems.}
A second literature asks whether the agents are needed at all. Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2026agentslimming} prune redundant agents under a
baseline-anchored acceptance rule, cutting token cost by up to 78.9\%. Dylan
et al.~\cite{dylan2026equal} hold total LLM calls fixed and find an evolved
Planner--Executor--Critic team no better than an evolved single agent
($0.769$ vs.\ $0.754$, $p=0.80$), attributing the entire benefit to the
executor. Yu et al.~\cite{yu2026bottleneck} give an information-bottleneck
account in which structure pays only under bounded relay bandwidth, and
predict gains shrink or reverse for stronger models. Zhang et
al.~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} sharpen this into a critique of the
evaluations: reported multi-agent gains are largely setup artifacts arising
from \emph{structural collapse}, disabled tool paths, or baselines that never
demanded multi-agent structure, in settings where random pruning already
preserves accuracy.

\emph{What is already owned here, and the objection we must answer.}
``Agents are redundant'' is owned by this literature, and
\cite{zhang2026rethinking} is aimed at exactly the observation we report for
gpt-oss --- collapse with no accuracy cost --- which it would read as
evidence the setup never required multi-agent structure. We treat this as a
test rather than a discussion point: \S\ref{subsec:notsingle} reports a
budget-matched single-agent control on the same problems, and the collapsed
team beats it.

\paragraph{Coordination versus capability.}
The third literature asks who benefits from structure, and it is consistent
in direction. Canedo~\cite{canedo2026architecture} gives one coding agent
five informationally equivalent specification formats across six models and
reports a strong format~$\times$~model interaction --- format barely matters
for the strongest models and matters greatly for the weakest --- framing
structured specification as a
\emph{capability equalizer} whose value is inversely proportional to model
strength; \cite{yu2026bottleneck} reaches the same monotone prediction from
theory. Dochkina~\cite{dochkina2026drop} argues the complementary case and is
most directly opposed to our framing: given minimal scaffolding, agents
self-organize and outperform designed structures, with autonomy's payoff
\emph{increasing} in capability. Together the field predicts one monotone
ordering --- imposed structure helps weak models, autonomy suits strong ones.

\emph{We address \cite{dochkina2026drop} head-on because half our result
resembles it and half contradicts it.} One of our actors does discard its
assigned organization and lose nothing, which is what a self-organization
account predicts. But it does not self-organize into anything: it collapses
into three agents each independently asserting its own answer, which is the
opposite of spontaneous differentiation, and its advantage over a single agent
survives that collapse rather than arising from any new structure. Meanwhile
every other actor we tested collapses just as thoroughly, and one of them
loses accuracy for it. Neither account predicts a panel in which the
organizational measure is flat across actors and the performance cost is
not.

\paragraph{The gap.}
Prior work establishes that roles are often not obeyed, that agents are often
redundant, and that structure tends to help weaker models. Missing is a test
of whether \emph{organization} is load-bearing that holds compute and
information fixed, measures the organization directly rather than inferring
it from outcome, intervenes in both directions, and runs on more than one
actor substrate. Doing so yields a result no monotone capability account
predicts: three actors lose their division of labor at essentially one rate
while their accuracy costs differ in sign and significance. What we claim is
the dissociation between the amount of organization lost and the cost of
losing it, not an ordering of actors along a collapse axis --- an axis our
own measurement work found to be an artifact of the metric.

\paragraph{Studies of the same trace corpus.}
Three prior studies analyze the corpus this work instruments: an audit of
confidence-routed broadcast~\cite{jiang2026confidence}, cost-aware routing
\emph{across} protocols~\cite{yang2026protocol}, and reviewer precision
versus critique uptake~\cite{yang2026precise}. All three are observational
and none measures whether the specified division of labor is realized; the
controlled arms of \S\ref{sec:method} do not exist in that corpus.
