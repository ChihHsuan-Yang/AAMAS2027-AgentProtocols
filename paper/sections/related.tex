\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{The question predates the LLM literature that reopened it.}
Organization-centered multi-agent systems gave roles a formal existence independent of
their occupants: Moise$^+$ separates a structural, a functional and a
\emph{deontic} specification --- what a role-filler is obliged and
permitted to do~\cite{hubner2002moise} --- OperA makes role adoption an
explicit social contract~\cite{dignum2004opera}, and electronic
institutions bind norms through middleware outside the
agents~\cite{esteva2001formal}, which can regiment a norm (make violation
impossible) or enforce it (sanction violation)~\cite{grossi2007ubilex}. An
LLM role prompt is the degenerate case: functional and deontic clauses
written in prose, with no middleware to make them bind and no record of
whether they did. In these terms our role-free condition keeps the structure --- order,
graph, a positional approver --- and deletes the functional and deontic
specifications together; our enforced condition regiments the functional
part at the interface. ``Adaptability'' here means revising an answer
within an episode, not organizational
reorganization~\cite{horling2004survey}; teamwork models treat repair of a
failed joint plan as an obligation of the team~\cite{tambe1997flexible},
which a prompt can state but not secure.

\paragraph{Role specialization and role drift.} That prompted roles are not
reliably obeyed is established: Cemri et al.~\cite{cemri2025why} name
disobeying the role specification a failure mode across 1{,}600+ annotated
traces, Cao et al.~\cite{cao2026modules} give the training-time account of
\emph{role drift}, Orogat et al.~\cite{orogat2026architectural} find
role-label conditioning does not induce the intended
specialization, Patel~\cite{patel2026representational} measures collapse
as near-identical rationales, Kandoussi~\cite{kandoussi2026whoami} reports
differentiation arising \emph{without} assigned roles, and
enforcement-side work makes role scope structural rather than
advisory~\cite{stettler2026progressive,ge2026roles}. Role collapse, and
the decoupling of role quality from task performance, are
known~\cite{cao2026modules,yang2026precise}; we measure them under a
controlled ablation.

\paragraph{Redundancy and parity.} Dylan et al.~\cite{dylan2026equal} hold
total LLM calls fixed and find an evolved Planner--Executor--Critic team no
better than an evolved single agent ($p=0.80$). We replicate first-pass
parity only in part: gpt-oss \emph{improves} by $+0.0503$ with an interval
excluding zero. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} argue that gains of
multi-agent efficiency methods often arise where one agent suffices --- an
objection aimed at our role-free condition, which is why we report a
single-agent reference (\S\ref{sec:method}); Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2026agentslimming} prune redundant agents on the same premise.

\paragraph{Feedback, revision and answer lock-in.} Iterative refinement
with self-generated or tool feedback is a standard
pattern~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine,shinn2023reflexion,gou2024critic}, as
is multi-agent debate~\cite{du2024debate,liang2024divergent}. That models
repeat earlier outputs under exact verifier feedback is
established~\cite{jiang2026exactfeedback}; under self-reflection the
failure has a name, Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and
belief entrenchment is studied directly~\cite{oh2026entrenchment}. Huang et
al.~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect} are frequently misread here: their
\emph{oracle} condition, which uses the correct label as a stopping rule,
\emph{improves} accuracy ($75.9 \to 84.3$ on GSM8K) and only the
\emph{intrinsic} condition
degrades~\cite[see also][]{stechly2024selfverification,kamoi2024when}. Our
loop is oracle-gated like theirs, and with a verifier in the loop more
attempts buy more coverage~\cite{brown2024monkeys} --- the regime in which
our role-structured team's restraint costs it. Closest to our lever is Chen
et al.~\cite{chen2026relabeling}, who hold an erroneous claim
byte-identical and vary only the \emph{role label} it carries, moving
explicit-correction rate by $23$--$93$ percentage points --- strong evidence
that a role label is causal for error correction, but single-agent and
without budget matching. We add the multi-agent case: a role-free team largely
repeats itself when the verdict does not reach it and revises when it does.
The repetition follows the information, not the organization.

\paragraph{Coordination versus capability.}
Canedo~\cite{canedo2026architecture} frames structured specification as a
\emph{capability equalizer} whose value falls with model strength;
\cite{yu2026bottleneck} reaches the same prediction from theory and
Dochkina~\cite{dochkina2026drop} the complementary case. Our enforced
condition reproduces that direction \emph{only under a scoring rule that
drops $8$--$17\%$ of trajectories}; under our pre-registered rule it
reverses for gemma-4 and vanishes for the others (\S\ref{subsec:enforce}).

\paragraph{Prior analyses of a released corpus, and critique uptake.}
Three studies analyze a publicly released multi-agent trace corpus on this
benchmark~\cite{jiang2026confidence,yang2026protocol,yang2026precise},
from which our single-agent comparator is also drawn. The closest separates
reviewer \emph{precision} from critique
\emph{uptake}~\cite{yang2026precise}: a planner--executor--reviewer
pipeline's reviewer flags real errors more precisely than broadcast-style
peer discussion does, yet its correct critique far less often changes the
next candidate, and uptake improves when critique is placed in the solver's
working context. We ask the organizational counterpart under controlled
conditions: with the corrective signal held fixed, does the role structure
itself make a team more adaptive? \textbf{None of these studies manipulates
the role structure}, measures whether the specified division of labor is
realized, or matches feedback access across conditions.

\paragraph{What is open.} These literatures establish that roles are often
not obeyed, that deleting them leaves first-pass accuracy intact, that
models fail to revise under external feedback, and that structure tends to
help weaker models. Each concerns a \emph{different stage of the work}, and
the failure to revise is almost always measured in a single agent, where
there is no organization to ablate. No study holds them in one frame.
