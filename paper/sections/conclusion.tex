\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Multi-agent roles organize a team: deleting them destroyed the intended
division of labor in all three actors. They did not make the team better at
the task. First-pass accuracy held, and when corrective feedback reached
both teams alike, the role-free team recovered more than the role-structured
one in all three actors and in a second domain (paired $p=6\times10^{-22}$
for gemma-4 on Omni-MATH), while a role-free baseline with the feedback
withheld almost never recovered. What roles changed was how the budget was
spent. \textbf{Structure is not adaptability: evaluating agent organization
means asking not only whether the specified division of labor is realized,
but whether corrective information changes what the team does next.}
