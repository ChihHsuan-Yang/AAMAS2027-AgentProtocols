\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Multi-agent roles organize a team: deleting them destroyed the intended
division of labor in all three actors on Omni-MATH, while first-pass
accuracy did not fall. They looked load-bearing for repairing a rejected answer only against
a role-free team that never received the evaluator's feedback. Given the
same feedback, the role-free team recovered more in every cell (paired
$p=6.2\times10^{-22}$ for gemma-4 on Omni-MATH), and matched on the number of
verdicts the two recovered alike (pooled difference $+0.009$, $95\%$ CI
$-0.014$ to $+0.033$): roles changed not how a team uses
corrective feedback but how often it asks for it, and in one exploratory
gemma-4 run, submitting in every cycle gave the role-structured team a
recovery as high as the role-free team's.
\textbf{Structure is not adaptability: evaluating agent organization means
asking not only whether the specified division of labor is realized, but
whether corrective information reaches the team and changes what it does
next.}
