\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Organization and multi-agent computation are separable, and measuring the
organization does not tell you which carries the load. Deleting every role
instruction destroys the functional division of labor in all three actors
($0.846$--$0.975$ of first pipeline passes become three independent solvers)
while first-pass accuracy is unchanged or improves --- a parity consistent
with prior role ablations~\cite{dylan2026equal,orogat2026architectural}. The
cost appears only after a wrong answer, and not because the role-free team is
stopped early: it holds the same six-cycle budget, submits on almost every
cycle rather than revising, and at full spend never revises its answer at all
in $0.866$ of trajectories against $0.353$ under roles. What roles preserve is
the production of a second distinct candidate.

This sharpens a known limit rather than discovering one. That models restate
answers under correction is Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent},
and it persists under complete external
feedback~\cite{jiang2026exactfeedback,stechly2024selfverification}; the
standard remedy prescribed for it is multi-agency. Our result inverts that
prescription --- a multi-agent system without role differentiation does not
escape it --- and makes the effect a matter of degree rather than kind. Oracle
self-correction is worth $+8.4$ points to a single
agent~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect}; the same oracle loop is worth $+4.1$ points
to our role-labeled team and $+0.9$ to the same team with the labels removed.
Role structure is a control variable on what an external correctness signal
can buy.

Two guards keep this from overreaching: a collapsed team still beats its
budget-matched single agent ($+0.0247$), and our pre-registered primary test
was null --- refuting ``multi-agent systems are just single agents'' and
``roles do not matter'' alike, from our own data. \textbf{Whether an
organization is load-bearing cannot be read off how much visible
differentiation an intervention destroys}: it has to be measured on the
outcome, at the phase where it acts, with an instrument shown to see the
failure it is meant to detect.
