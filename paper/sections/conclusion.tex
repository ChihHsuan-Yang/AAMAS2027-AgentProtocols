\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Multi-agent roles organize a team into a structured workflow. Deleting these
roles destroyed the intended division of labor across all tested models, but
first-pass accuracy did not fall. Roles only appeared load-bearing for
repairing rejected answers when compared against a role-free team that never
received the evaluator's feedback. When both teams received the identical
feedback string, the role-free team recovered more trajectories across every
tested configuration. When matched on the number of evaluator verdicts, this
advantage disappeared.

These findings suggest that roles change little about how a team uses
corrective feedback but substantially alter how often the team asks for it.
Structure is not adaptability. Evaluating agent organization therefore
requires checking both whether the specified division of labor is realized
and whether corrective information reaches the team and changes its next
actions.
