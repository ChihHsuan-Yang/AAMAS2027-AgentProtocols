\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Roles govern decision rights and information flow as well as the division
of labor. Across the tested models, removing roles largely eliminated
functional specialization without lowering first-pass accuracy. With
feedback available to both teams, the role-free team recovered more often
in all five model--benchmark settings. Submission logs, a post hoc
verdict-matched analysis, and an exploratory forced-submission intervention
point to the importance of opportunities for external correction. In the
intervention, gemma-4 recovery rose from $0.198$ to $0.613$ while role prompts
stayed unchanged. For systems with reliable external feedback, evaluating
an organization therefore requires measuring who may submit, who receives
the response, and whether the team recovers from a failed first answer.
