\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Deleting multi-agent roles largely eliminated the intended division of
labor in every tested model, yet first-pass accuracy did not fall. Roles do
more than allocate work: they also allocate decisions. A role-structured
Reviewer controls when the team submits to the external evaluator, and it
frequently withholds approval. In exploratory comparisons with feedback
available to both teams, the role-free team recovered more often in every
tested setting. In a post hoc comparison at equal numbers of verdicts the advantage
disappeared,
and for gemma-4, forcing a submission in every cycle with the role prompts
kept raised the role-structured team's recovery to the same level. Roles do
not just assign responsibilities; they also govern decisions and
information flow, which shape how a team recovers from failure. Building an
effective multi-agent organization therefore goes beyond assigning roles
correctly. It requires designing decision rights and information pathways
that support the desired system behavior.
