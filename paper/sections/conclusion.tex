\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A specified organization and a realized one are different objects, and in
multi-agent LLM systems the distance between them is measurable. On a large
corpus of role messages, prompted roles turn out to be highly distinguishable
-- the strong claim that role labels are cosmetic is false and we abandoned it
-- yet that distinguishability is carried by output formatting and answer
commitment rather than by the division of cognitive labor the contract
specifies, and it collapses to near a length control once restricted to the
governed behavior. Compliance varies by an order of magnitude across clauses of
a single contract, and it varies sharply across the model filling the role
under byte-identical prompts. Realization is a property of the
actor--specification pair, not of the specification.

This reframes what a role specification is: not a description of the system
that will run, but a hypothesis about it -- one that a system evaluated only on
task outcome never tests. The measurement apparatus we validate here is
deliberately modest in what it claims and explicit about where it fails,
including one proposed refinement that we rejected for making agreement worse.
