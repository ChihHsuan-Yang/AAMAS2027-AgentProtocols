\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Role structure \emph{can} be load-bearing for recovering from a
wrong answer where it is not load-bearing for producing the first one} ---
and the two halves do not have the same scope. Deleting every role instruction destroys the functional division of labor in
\textbf{all three} actors while first-pass accuracy is unchanged or improves
(\S\ref{subsec:firstpass}). \textbf{The recovery half holds cleanly in one actor}
(gemma-4, paired $p=2\times10^{-8}$, intent-to-treat-equivalent),
\textbf{directionally in a second} (gpt-oss, $p=0.208$) and \textbf{not at
all in a third} (Llama-3.3, a genuine null of the role manipulation, not a
floor). Where it holds, the cost is not that the role-free team stops early or gets
less feedback: it holds the same six-cycle budget, draws \emph{more} oracle
verdicts, and never revises its answer in $0.845$ of first-pass-wrong
trajectories against $0.288$ under roles. What roles preserve is a second
distinct candidate --- the prescribed remedy for
Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent} inverted. Making the
division of labor structurally unavoidable (Arm~C) restores the organization
and un-freezes the answer but not accuracy: enforcement governs who may
\emph{speak}, not whether the agents \emph{differ}. Four guards hold it down: the pre-registered primary test was
null; the loop is oracle-gated, so none of this is unaided self-correction;
one \PER{} topology was tested; and \textbf{gemma-4, which carries the
recovery result, is also the actor whose team loses to its own
budget-matched single agent in both arms} ($-0.0225$, $-0.0563$;
\S\ref{subsec:notsingle}). \textbf{How much differentiation an intervention destroys
does not say whether the organization was load-bearing}: measure it at the
phase where it acts.%
