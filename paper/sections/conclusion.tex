\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Multi-agent roles need not improve what a team knows on its first attempt,
and in our setting they do not supply the ability to revise after failure
either: that comes from the feedback reaching the team. Deleting the role
instructions destroyed the division of labor in all three actors while
first-pass accuracy held. A role-free team whose prompts carried no
feedback resubmitted the answer the evaluator had rejected; the same team,
given the feedback, recovered more than the role-structured one in two
domains (paired $p=6\times10^{-22}$ on Omni-MATH). What roles changed was
how the budget was spent. \textbf{Evaluating agent organization means
auditing what each agent is told, not only how many calls it gets --- a
role can look load-bearing simply by being the only place the feedback
goes.}
