\subsection{Campaign}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Task and problems.} Competition mathematics (Omni-MATH), sampled
by a deterministic seeded manifest so every prefix of the run is a valid
random sample. All arms run the same problems, so every comparison is paired
at the problem level. A second domain was designed and dropped: its Planner
contract carries no computation clause, so its components would not be
comparable.

\paragraph{Actors and scale.} Three actor models served from one pinned vLLM
endpoint: \textbf{gpt-oss-120b}, \textbf{gemma-4-31B} and
\textbf{Llama-3.3-70B}. The analysis reads a single frozen, sha256-manifested
snapshot: \textbf{11{,}600 trajectories over 83{,}320 role messages and
17{,}486 gradings}, on 1{,}600 distinct problems. Arms~A, B and C run
$n=1{,}600$ per cell for the first two actors and $n=400$ for Llama-3.3, plus
$n=400$ per actor in Arm~C$'$. Every trajectory carries the same budget record
(3 outer $\times$ 2 inner cycles, temperature $0$, identical
\texttt{max\_tokens}); the snapshot contains exactly one distinct budget. Two
further actor candidates were probed and rejected as unavailable rather than
assumed available. The evaluator is gpt-oss-120b in every cell, itself one of
the actors under study (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Provenance of the single-agent control.} The budget-matched
comparator in \S\ref{subsec:notsingle} is \textbf{not} part of this campaign
or its frozen snapshot. It is the \texttt{single\_agent} protocol from a
previously released trace corpus of the same benchmark, joined by problem
identifier, covering the two actors at $n=1{,}600$; a control a reader cannot
locate is not a control. \textbf{Its two actor arms were built under
different source commits}, so it is a directional guard and cannot support a
statement about how the gap differs between actors. It is also conservative
in one direction and generous in another: that protocol consumed \emph{more}
oracle feedback than our Arm~A ($1.66$--$1.69$ evaluator calls against
$1.11$--$1.35$) and roughly half the actor calls.

\paragraph{Power.} Computed by simulation (4{,}000 replicates, two-sided
$\alpha=0.05$) from discordance rates in the pre-existing corpus. The paired
per-actor main effect is well powered at this scale; the
actor~$\times$~intervention interaction needs roughly twice the sample, so
\textbf{a null on P4 means ``no large interaction detected,'' not ``no
interaction''} --- and for the two Llama pairs, whose minimum detectable
effects are $0.069$ and $0.075$, not even the former
(\S\ref{subsec:p4null}). Organizational measures are computed per message,
so the measurement half is well powered where the outcome half is not.

\paragraph{A confound caught before it became a result.} The main run was
gated on a smoke test requiring non-zero Arm~C boundary rejections with
reasons logged, answer-bearing calls equal across arms, and a pinned backend.
An early campaign was stopped after 121 trajectories when $74\%$ of Arm~C
rejections proved to be the model cut off at the token limit mid-JSON rather
than violating a role boundary. Truncation was not arm-specific but its
\emph{cost} was: Arms~A and B tolerate a truncated prose message where Arm~C
rejects unparseable JSON, so left alone this would have produced a convincing
``enforcement lowers accuracy'' artifact. The limit was doubled, a logged
salvage path added, and the pre-fix rows never pooled. The residual cost is
Arm~C's attrition, reported in \S\ref{subsec:enforce} rather than removed.
