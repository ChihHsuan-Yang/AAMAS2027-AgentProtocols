\subsection{Campaign}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Task and problems.} Competition mathematics (Omni-MATH), sampled by
a deterministic seeded manifest so every prefix of the run is itself a valid
random sample. All arms run on the same problems, so every arm comparison is
paired at the problem level. A second domain was designed and dropped: its
Planner contract carries no computation clause, so its components would not be
comparable.

\paragraph{Actors and scale.} Three actor models served from one pinned vLLM
endpoint: \textbf{gpt-oss-120b}, \textbf{gemma-4-31B} and
\textbf{Llama-3.3-70B}. The analysis reads a single frozen, sha256-manifested
snapshot: \textbf{11{,}600 trajectories over 83{,}320 role messages and
17{,}486 gradings}, on 1{,}600 distinct problems. Arms~A, B and C run
$n=1{,}600$ per cell for the first two actors and $n=400$ for Llama-3.3, plus
$n=400$ per actor in Arm~C$'$. Every trajectory in every cell carries the same
budget record (3 outer $\times$ 2 inner deliberation cycles, temperature $0$,
identical \texttt{max\_tokens}); the snapshot contains exactly one distinct
budget. Two further actor candidates were probed and rejected as unavailable
rather than assumed available. The evaluator is gpt-oss-120b in every cell,
which is one of the actors under study --- see \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Provenance of the single-agent control.} The budget-matched
single-agent comparator in \S\ref{subsec:notsingle} is \textbf{not} part of
this campaign or its frozen snapshot. It is the \texttt{single\_agent}
protocol from a previously released trace corpus of the same benchmark, joined
to our trajectories by problem identifier, and it covers the two actors with
$n=1{,}600$. We state this because a control a reader cannot locate is not a
control. It is the reason the comparison is conservative in one direction and
generous in another: that protocol consumed \emph{more} oracle feedback than
our Arm~A ($1.66$--$1.69$ evaluator calls against $1.11$--$1.35$) and roughly
half the actor calls.

\paragraph{Power.} Computed by simulation (4{,}000 replicates, two-sided
$\alpha=0.05$) from discordance rates in the pre-existing corpus. The paired
per-actor main effect is well powered at this scale; the
actor~$\times$~intervention interaction needs roughly twice the sample, so
\textbf{a null on P4 means ``no large interaction detected,'' not ``no
interaction.''} Organizational measures are computed per message, so the
measurement half of the study is well powered at trajectory counts at which
the outcome half is not.

\paragraph{A confound caught before it became a result.} The main run was
gated on a smoke test requiring non-zero Arm~C boundary rejections with
reasons logged, answer-bearing calls equal across arms, and a pinned backend.
An early campaign was stopped and quarantined after 121 trajectories when
$74\%$ of Arm~C rejections turned out to be the model cut off at the token
limit mid-JSON rather than violating a role boundary. Truncation was not
arm-specific but its \emph{cost} was: Arms~A and B tolerate a truncated prose
message where Arm~C rejects unparseable JSON, so left alone this would have
produced a convincing ``enforcement lowers accuracy'' token-budget artifact.
The limit was doubled, a logged salvage path added, and the pre-fix rows
quarantined and never pooled. The residual form of this cost is Arm~C's
attrition, reported in \S\ref{subsec:enforce} rather than removed.
