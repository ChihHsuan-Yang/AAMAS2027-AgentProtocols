\subsection{Campaign}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Task and problems.} Omni-MATH competition
mathematics~\cite{gao2025omnimath}, sampled by a deterministic seeded
manifest so every prefix of the run is a valid random sample; all
conditions run the same problems, so every comparison is paired. An earlier
candidate, a multiple-choice biology benchmark, was rejected before launch
because on a four-option item ``produce a different candidate'' succeeds one
time in three by chance, confounding the mechanism we measure with guessing.

\paragraph{Actors and scale.} Three actor models served from one pinned
vLLM endpoint~\cite{kwon2023vllm}: gpt-oss-120b~\cite{openai2025gptoss},
gemma-4-31B~\cite{gemma2026gemma4} and Llama-3.3-70B~\cite{grattafiori2024llama3}. The primary
analysis reads a single frozen, sha256-manifested snapshot: 11{,}600
trajectories over 83{,}320 role messages and 17{,}486 gradings, on
$1{,}600$ problems sampled uniformly at random from the $4{,}181$
available. The three main conditions run $n=1{,}600$ per cell for gemma-4
and gpt-oss and $n=400$ for Llama-3.3; the context-restored control covers
gemma-4 and gpt-oss at $n=400$ each. Every trajectory carries the same
budget record. The evaluator is gpt-oss-120b in every cell, itself one of
the actors under study (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). The single-agent
comparator is \emph{not} part of this campaign: it is a previously released
trace corpus of the same benchmark joined by problem identifier. Simulated
from discordance rates in that corpus, the paired per-actor main effect is
well powered; a post-hoc detectability analysis finds the
actor~$\times$~intervention interaction powered only for gpt-oss against
gemma-4 (\S\ref{subsec:prereg}). An early campaign stopped for a
truncation confound was never pooled (supplement).

\paragraph{The feedback-matched condition.} It was run after the freeze
($4{,}748$ trajectories) on the same endpoint, problems, budget and
evaluator, for all three actors on Omni-MATH and for gemma-4 and gpt-oss on
SciBench~\cite{wang2024scibench}, manifested separately and never pooled into a
frozen cell; the two gemma-4 cells were run a second time, independently.
A re-run of gemma-4's role-structured condition on $400$ frozen problems
finds no detectable drift in final accuracy ($0.818$ against $0.825$ frozen,
paired $p=0.75$; recovery $0.198$ against $0.146$). Decoding at temperature
$0$ is not deterministic on a shared endpoint: the two role-free
conditions, byte-identical until the first verdict, agree on gemma-4's
first verdict for $1{,}496$ of $1{,}598$ problems, and their first-pass
collapse differs by up to $0.04$ (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). We therefore
treat a cell as one sample and give the second run, where we have one, in
the supplement.
Prompts, runner and analysis scripts are in the supplementary material.
