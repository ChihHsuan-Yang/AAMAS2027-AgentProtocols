\subsection{Campaign}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Task and problems.} Competition mathematics (Omni-MATH), sampled
by a deterministic seeded manifest so that every prefix of the run is itself
a valid random sample. All arms run on the same problems, so every arm
comparison is paired at the problem level. A second domain was designed and
dropped: the runner pins one domain, and the other domain's Planner contract
carries no computation clause, so its components would not be comparable.

\paragraph{Actors.} Three actor models served from one vLLM endpoint:
\textbf{gpt-oss-120b}, \textbf{gemma-4-31B}, and \textbf{Llama-3.3-70B}. The
third was added late and reaches $n=84$ matched problems, enough to
corroborate the collapse rate and not enough for an accuracy contrast; we use
it only for the former. Two further candidates
were probed and rejected as unavailable rather than assumed available. The
evaluator is gpt-oss-120b in every cell, which is one of the actors under
study --- see \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Scale.} 8{,}827 trajectories over 57{,}764 agent messages:
${\approx}1{,}295$ problems per arm for Arms~A, B, and C in each of two
actors, plus 400 problems per actor in Arm~C$'$ and 85--89 per arm for the
third actor. Paired accuracy contrasts rest on
1{,}291--1{,}296 matched problems per actor. Concurrency was set per actor at
levels measured on the live endpoint (25 for gpt-oss, 10 for the gemma
family) rather than assumed; gemma-4 is four to five times slower per call,
so arms do not complete in equal wall-clock at equal concurrency.

\paragraph{Power.} Computed by simulation (4{,}000 replicates, two-sided
$\alpha=0.05$) from discordance rates and net asymmetries observed in the
pre-existing corpus. At the achieved scale the paired per-actor main effect
is well powered for large effects and moderately powered for smaller ones;
the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction requires roughly twice the
sample of a main effect for equal power. \textbf{A null on P4 therefore means
``no large interaction detected,'' not ``no interaction.''} Note the
asymmetry that shaped the design: organizational measures are computed per
message, so the measurement half of the study is well powered at trajectory
counts at which the outcome half is not.

\paragraph{Gates and a confound caught before it became a result.} The main
run was gated on a smoke test requiring, among other checks, that Arm~C
produce non-zero boundary rejections with reasons logged, that answer-bearing
calls be equal across arms, and that the backend be pinned. An early campaign
was stopped and quarantined after 121 trajectories when $74\%$ of Arm~C
rejections turned out to be the model cut off at the token limit mid-JSON
rather than violating a role boundary. Truncation was not arm-specific
($8.4\%$/$11.3\%$/$8.9\%$ over A/B/C) but its \emph{cost} was: Arms~A and B
tolerate a truncated prose message where Arm~C rejects unparseable JSON. Left
alone this would have produced a convincing ``enforcement lowers accuracy''
result that was a token-budget artifact. The token limit was doubled, a
logged salvage path added, and the pre-fix rows quarantined and never
pooled.
