\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that trajectories never
producing a graded answer count as incorrect; significance is exact McNemar
on discordant pairs. Holm correction across the two testable pre-registered
families changes no verdict (P3, gemma-4 Holm $p=0.0120$; P4, all retained).
\textbf{The role-structured-versus-role-free contrast supplying this paper's
headline was pre-registered as secondary and exploratory}, and every
conditional analysis below conditions on a post-treatment variable:
descriptive, never causal.

\subsection{R1. Removing roles changes the organization, not the first
answer}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Removing the role instructions changes nothing else: same three agents,
same order, same budget, same evaluator. \textbf{This claim rests on the
mechanical measures, which involve no judge.} We ask whether \emph{every}
agent independently asserts its own final answer on the first pass, so the
measure is not length-confounded (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}); it rises from
$0.000$--$0.120$ to $0.846$--$0.976$. \textbf{Decomposed by role, the planner
supplies almost all of the change}: the executor is at ceiling either way
($0.907$--$1.000$) and the reviewer, already high, moves
$0.781$/$0.492$/$0.823$ to $0.998$/$0.965$/$0.961$ (gemma-4 / Llama-3.3 /
gpt-oss), while the planner moves $0.000$/$0.000$/$0.183$ to
$0.979$/$0.889$/$0.898$ --- a claim about the \emph{change}, not the level.

\paragraph{Exact duplication is sound and far too narrow.} Byte-identical
text between two agents fires on $0.680$ of gpt-oss role-free first passes
and essentially never elsewhere ($\leq 0.008$) --- a property of the
instrument, not the actors (\S\ref{sec:framework}).

\paragraph{A second opinion, and the leak that stops it being blind.} We put
171 trajectories ($42$/$56$/$44$/$29$ across the four conditions) to a
frontier model against a rubric frozen before the first judgement, with
actor, condition, correctness and the automated label withheld and role
words neutralized. It finds the division of labor intact in $1.000$ of
role-structured trajectories and absent in $0.857$--$1.000$ of role-free
ones, stable at $0.953$ across repetitions. This is \textbf{model
adjudication, not human annotation}, and not ground truth.

\textbf{It is also outcome-blind but \emph{not} condition-blind.}
Neutralization renamed the role words without equalizing their
\emph{presence}: a neutralized \texttt{Agent-P/E/R} token appears in all
$42$ role-structured items and $3$ of $56$ role-free ones (Fisher exact
$p=1.5\times10^{-24}$), so a judge noticing only whether a transcript labels
its speakers could separate the conditions without reading content; output
format separates the enforced condition further. Normalizing to a common
surface would destroy the differences being judged, so we disclose rather
than re-run: \textbf{we do not call the condition-contrastive adjudication
blinded}, and the collapse claim does not rest on it. Judge stability and
the byte metric's precision/recall are within-item, so unaffected.

\paragraph{Two of three actors visibly duplicate; the third is
uninstrumented.} gemma-4's and Llama-3.3's role-free planners write full
independent solutions (median $1{,}876$ and $1{,}892$ characters) before
boxing an answer. Numeric-token overlap confirms it, but only against the
right null: pairing planners with executors from \emph{different} problems
shows the baseline is itself condition-dependent, so the raw difference is
invalid. Against each condition's own null the excess is $+0.449$/$+0.432$
without roles against $+0.159$/$+0.145$ with them --- a shift of $+0.290$
and $+0.287$. \textbf{gpt-oss cannot be read at all}: its role-free
planner emits a median $37$ visible characters in $26.7$ seconds ($2.85$ per
second against $195$ with roles), with \texttt{used\_reasoning\_fallback}
false in all $2{,}302$ sub-100-character messages --- the logging defect
below.

\paragraph{First-pass accuracy does not follow --- the control condition.}
\emph{Not a finding} but the control it is: the ablation removed the
organization without crippling the
solver~\cite{dylan2026equal,orogat2026architectural}. On matched problems
the first-pass difference is \emph{identical} for gemma-4 ($+0.0000$, CI
$[-0.0156,+0.0150]$) and flat for Llama-3.3 ($+0.0077$,
$[-0.0283,+0.0437]$), while it \emph{improves} for gpt-oss ($+0.0503$,
$[+0.0339,+0.0666]$). Whatever roles buy, they do not buy the first answer.

\subsection{A logging defect, and its second consequence}
\label{subsec:logging}

Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content}, falling back to
\texttt{reasoning} only when content was empty; gpt-oss-120b often returns a
short content string alongside a long hidden trace, so the derivation was
discarded while the flag documenting the substitution stayed false. Below
$10$ visible characters per second of latency, \textbf{$75.7\%$ of gpt-oss
role-free messages are affected and $93.4\%$ of its first passes contain
one}; the flag reports $5.8\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are unaffected
($0.1$--$0.5\%$).

Two consequences follow and no third. \textbf{No point estimate of gpt-oss's
duplication rate is recoverable}, and \textbf{the defect bounds
collapse-\emph{mode} interpretation}: calling that 37-character planner a
behavioral mode would attribute our defect to the model, and it is why byte
duplication fires only here. \textbf{It does not manufacture the collapse},
the one place it could reach a headline measure: restricted to first passes
with \emph{no} affected message, role-free collapse is $0.821$ ($n=106$)
against $0.846$ overall and the role-structured rate $0.118$ against
$0.120$ --- the gap is intact on clean messages. Accuracy is graded from an
always-present final answer, and the functional measure reads answer
assertions, not derivations. The campaign was not re-run.

\subsection{R2. Roles change how a team responds to correctness feedback}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig1_v1_grouped.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The phase dissociation.} Removing roles leaves first-pass
accuracy alone but collapses recovery from a wrong first answer; structural
enforcement restores recovery without restoring accuracy. Pre-registered
scoring; error bars bootstrap. Recovery conditions on a post-treatment
subset, and the role-free gap is paired-resolvable only for gemma-4, the
enforcement gap only for Llama-3.3
(\S\S\ref{subsec:recovery},~\ref{subsec:enforce}).}
\label{fig:dissociation}
\end{figure}

Both teams reach the evaluator and are told the same thing: this answer is
wrong. The standing explanation for what follows is premature consensus ---
the team stops early. \textbf{We test and reject it.} Every condition runs
the same six cycles by construction and the role-free team draws \emph{more}
oracle feedback (\S\ref{sec:method}); realized spend is near-identical
($5.52$ versus $5.83$ cycles, gemma-4, first-pass-wrong) and \textbf{no
trajectory was cut short}. So it is not fewer chances, not early stopping,
and not less information.

\textbf{What differs is use.} The role-structured team submits $1.54$ times
and spends $3.98$ cycles revising; the role-free team converts $5.80$ of its
$5.83$ cycles into resubmissions. What stops is refusal --- in $98.1\%$ of
gemma-4's role-free first-pass-wrong trajectories the Reviewer never
declines, against $5.1\%$ with roles ($61.6\%$ versus $4.3\%$ for gpt-oss)
--- and the consequence is a frozen answer, unchanged in $0.845$ of those
trajectories against $0.288$. \textbf{Told they are wrong, one team revises
and the other resubmits.} This is
Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and the point is the
\textbf{inversion}: multi-agency is the prescribed cure that role-free
multi-agency does not deliver. Rubber-stamping is a symptom of answer
lock-in, not established as its cause.

\paragraph{A competing driver we do not dismiss.} Accumulated context is a
real cause of recovery failure~\cite{yang2026contamination}: within the
role-structured condition alone gpt-oss recovery falls $0.923 \to 0.070$
across pooled context quartiles. It does not \emph{account for} the gap,
which survives context matching in the lower quartiles ($0.662$ versus
$0.151$, gemma-4; $0.656$ versus $0.293$, gpt-oss) --- \textbf{but in
gpt-oss's top quartile the ordering reverses} ($0.070$ versus $0.189$).
\textbf{Directional only}: quartiles are cut on the pooled distribution and
context is nearly a function of condition, so the strata are partly
condition indicators (Q1 holds $198$ role-free trajectories against $13$
role-structured).

\subsection{R3. The difference in feedback use becomes a difference in
outcome}
\label{subsec:recovery}


Conditional on the first submission being graded wrong, the role-free team
recovers less often (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}): gemma-4 falls $0.222
\rightarrow 0.051$ ($-0.1709$ $[-0.2247,-0.1171]$), gpt-oss $0.324
\rightarrow 0.247$ ($-0.0766$ $[-0.1361,-0.0153]$). \textbf{Only gemma-4's
survives pairing}: on problems first-pass-wrong under \emph{both} conditions
it recovers on $37$ the role-free team misses against $3$ the other way
(exact McNemar $p=2.0\times10^{-8}$).

\textbf{For gemma-4 this is not a post-treatment conditional.} Its
first-pass rates are equal to four decimals ($0.8025$ either way), so the
role-free team does not enter recovery already behind: in
$\Delta_{\text{final}} = \Delta_{\text{fp}}(1-\text{rec}_A) +
(1-\text{fp}_B)\,\Delta_{\text{rec}}$ the first term vanishes and the
recovery contrast is an exact reparameterization of the intention-to-treat
effect, $-0.1709 \times (1-0.8025) = -0.0338$, the observed final-accuracy
difference, residual zero --- a quantity algebraically equal to the
randomized contrast is not a post-treatment artifact.

\paragraph{Scope across actors, stated once.} The recovery result is
\textbf{clean in gemma-4}, \textbf{directional but unresolved in gpt-oss}
(paired $p=0.208$, reported as directional only; its first-pass rates also
differ by $+0.050$, so the reparameterization above does not extend to it),
and \textbf{absent in Llama-3.3} --- $0.014$ and $0.015$, four recoveries
out of $276$ and four of $268$. That last is a genuine null of the
\emph{role-instruction} manipulation rather than a floor: the same actor's
repair channel exists and is movable, since enforcement raises it to
$0.139$ (paired $0.009$ versus $0.096$, $p=3.8\times10^{-6}$,
\S\ref{subsec:enforce}). The test has ${\approx}80\%$ power against an
excess of $0.04$--$0.05$ where gemma-4's effect is $0.171$: informative
against a gemma-sized effect, uninformative below it. The claim is therefore
that role structure \textbf{can} be load-bearing for feedback-driven
recovery where it is not load-bearing for first-pass solving, not that it
always is.

\paragraph{The accuracy contrasts against grader noise.} Repeated identical
evaluator prompts at temperature $0$ disagree on $20$ of $2{,}790$ distinct
graded strings, a group-level rate of $0.0072$ ($0.0017$--$0.0072$ across
estimators), keyed on the exact candidate bytes. \textbf{We do not divide an
effect size by a disagreement rate} --- the noise largely cancels in a
paired contrast --- but propagate it into the interval, flipping each graded
outcome independently at the per-call rate $0.0062$ inside the bootstrap. gemma-4's survives ($[-0.0494,-0.0175]$); \textbf{gpt-oss's
($[-0.0013,+0.0331]$) and Llama-3.3's ($[-0.0350,+0.0400]$) do not} --- not
resolvable above grader noise rather than absent.

\subsection{A different question: is the team worth its budget?}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

A standing objection holds that a setup where collapse is free never
required multi-agent structure~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}. That asks whether
multi-agent \emph{execution} earns its budget --- a different question from
which phase organization carries --- and a budget-matched single-agent
protocol on the same problems answers it (Table~\ref{tab:main}, lower
panel), \textbf{from a previously released trace corpus rather than our own
campaign} (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). It refuses both easy readings: a
collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own single agent ($+0.0294$), so three
agents emitting the same answer is not functionally one agent, \textbf{while
gemma-4's team is significantly worse than its own in both conditions}
($-0.0225$, $-0.0563$). \emph{Neither} ``multi-agent systems are just single
agents'' \emph{nor} ``more agents help'' survives our data. \textbf{The two
single-agent arms were built from different source commits}, so each
direction is a sound guard while \emph{the gap between actors is not}; the
single agent also drew \emph{more} oracle feedback ($1.66$--$1.69$ against
$1.11$--$1.35$) and half the actor calls, conservative on the decisive
axis.

\subsection{R4. The same dissociation in a second domain}
\label{subsec:xdomain}

Both halves reappear on \textbf{SciBench} --- the complete $574$-problem
matched slice of free-response college science; two actors, $2{,}296$
trajectories, frozen separately. The role-free prompt is byte-identical to
its Omni-MATH form and the role-structured one keeps the same structural
contract with domain wording adapted, so no clause-specific metric crosses
domains. For \textbf{gemma-4}
first-pass accuracy is $0.794$ either way and paired recovery falls $0.212
\rightarrow 0.020$ ($n=99$, $21$ versus $2$ discordant,
$p=6.6\times10^{-5}$), not distinguishable in size from Omni-MATH.
\textbf{gpt-oss}'s cell is underpowered (power $0.06$--$0.42$
at the realized $n=96$, depending on the test assumed) and resolves nothing; Llama-3.3 was not run. \textbf{The story holds again for one actor in a second domain},
not across actors.

\subsection{R5. Structural enforcement is consistent with the
interpretation, and
introduces brittleness}
\label{subsec:enforce}

If what roles supply is a second distinct candidate after failure, making
the division of labor unavoidable at the interface should return that
capacity. It does. The manipulation check passes: the planner authors an
answer in $0.000$ of enforced trajectories in all three actors against
$0.986$/$0.916$/$0.958$ under generic prompts, with $838$/$1{,}183$/$395$
role-boundary rejections per cell (formatting coercions excluded by a
definition fixed in advance).
Enforcement \textbf{un-freezes the answer}: $0.119$/$0.092$/$0.072$ against
the role-free $0.845$/$0.208$/$0.806$, and below the role-structured rate
except for gpt-oss ($0.052$). Enforcement does not change what the planner
\emph{computes} --- evaluated-equation density is $3.11$/$4.78$ per
$1{,}000$ characters against $2.76$/$2.12$ --- so you can structurally stop
an agent answering without stopping it doing the work.

\textbf{Recovery follows for Llama-3.3; for the other two the direction is
consistent but not resolvable against attrition.} Recovery is computed on
every trajectory with a recorded incorrect first verdict, including the
$150$ enforced trajectories that received one then died in regeneration ---
all necessarily first-pass wrong, since a passing verdict ends the loop, and
scored $0$ by the preregistration. On that denominator enforcement recovers
at $0.314$/$0.395$/$0.139$ against $0.222$/$0.324$/$0.014$, but the paired
contrast survives only for Llama-3.3 ($0.009$ versus $0.096$,
$p=3.8\times10^{-6}$); gemma-4 ($p=0.298$) and gpt-oss ($p=0.105$) do not.
Llama's denominator is constructed, not asserted: $400$ attempted, $349$
judged, $251$ wrong on that verdict ($17$ later dying in regeneration,
scored $0$), $35$ recovered. Conditioning on successful grading would
manufacture significance out of the same one-sided attrition we reject for
accuracy below, and flatters enforcement twice over --- raising this cell
to $0.150$ and the enforced first-pass column from $0.779$/$0.761$/$0.281$
to $0.822$/$0.788$/$0.295$.

\textbf{Improved revisability does not become end-to-end accuracy, because
enforcement is brittle} (Table~\ref{tab:armc}). Under the pre-registered
rule the accuracy difference is $-0.0225$ / $-0.0050$ / $+0.0175$: one
significant negative, two nulls. Dropping ungraded trajectories gives
$+0.0190$ / $+0.0361$ / $+0.0453$ --- all positive, all significant, ordered
by actor weakness, exactly the published capability-equalizer
direction~\cite{canedo2026architecture}. \textbf{That reading is
survivorship}: the preregistration fixed in advance that such trajectories
count incorrect, and \textbf{the benefit appears only after conditioning on
successful parsing.} Restoring shared history at fixed enforcement does not
rescue it either ($+0.0450$ and $-0.0125$ pre-registered) --- attrition, not
context.
