\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that ungraded
trajectories count as incorrect. P1--P5 are the pre-registered endpoints and
carry a Holm correction. The A-versus-B contrast supplying this paper's
headline was pre-registered as \emph{secondary and exploratory}; it is
labeled \textbf{post-hoc} everywhere it appears, and its importance does not
change that label.

\subsection{Removing role instructions collapses the organization of every
actor}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Arm~B deletes every role instruction and changes nothing else. How one
measures the surviving organization turns out to decide the answer, so we
report two measures and are explicit about which claim rests on which.

\paragraph{Exact output duplication: sound, and far too narrow.} The natural
automated measure is whether two or more agents emit byte-identical text. We
apply it on the first pipeline pass only, because an all-message rule gives
longer trajectories more opportunities to duplicate and is confounded with
trajectory length. It fires on $0.691$ of gpt-oss Arm~B trajectories and
\textbf{never} on gemma-4 or Llama-3.3 in any arm.

That gap is a property of the instrument, not of the actors. A blinded
adjudication (below) judges gemma-4's Arm~B trajectories redundant at $0.944$
while this metric reports $0.000$: gemma-4's agents paraphrase each other at
length instead of emitting identical strings, so no hash matches even though
nothing is added. \textbf{Exact duplication is therefore a conservative lower
bound --- usable to say that at least this much collapse occurred, and never
to compare how much organization two actors lost.}

\paragraph{Functional collapse, and what a role diagram is supposed to
prevent.} The property the Planner--Executor--Reviewer diagram asserts is that
the three agents do different jobs, so we measure its failure directly: does
\emph{every} agent independently assert its own final answer, making the
pipeline three solvers rather than a division of labor
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse})?

\textbf{All three actors lose the division of labor at essentially the same
rate.} Arm~B is not ``roles blurred'': $0.90$--$0.98$ of \emph{planners} emit
a final answer, against $0.00$--$0.18$ in Arm~A. The pipeline becomes three
independent solvers that mostly agree. Under the byte metric this looked like
a large contrast between actors; under a measure of the property the diagram
actually asserts, that contrast is absent.

\paragraph{Validation by blinded adjudication.} Because the choice of measure
carries the result, we validated it on 180 sampled trajectories against a
rubric frozen before the first judgement, with actor, arm, correctness and
the automated label withheld, and with role words neutralized after we found
they leaked the arm (42 occurrences in Arm~A against 3 in Arm~B). Judged
``no meaningful division of labor'':

\small

\toprule
arm & actor & judged no division & byte flag \\
\midrule
A & gemma-4-31B  & $0.000$ & $0.000$ \\
A & gpt-oss-120b & $0.056$ & $0.000$ \\
\textbf{B} & \textbf{gemma-4-31B}  & $\mathbf{0.944}$ & $0.000$ \\
\textbf{B} & \textbf{gpt-oss-120b} & $\mathbf{0.958}$ & $0.500$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three things follow. \textbf{Arm~A is genuinely differentiated} ($0.000$ and
$0.056$), so the A-versus-B contrast is real and the soft-role baseline is not
a straw man. \textbf{Both actors lose that differentiation in Arm~B} at
$0.944$ and $0.958$. And the byte metric has \textbf{precision $1.000$} (0
false positives in 36 firings) with \textbf{recall $0.343$}: everything it
flags is genuine, and it misses about two-thirds of what a judge sees. This
adjudication was performed by a frontier language model under the protocol
above, not by human annotators, and \S\ref{sec:limitations} states what that
does and does not establish.

\paragraph{The measures survive the obvious attacks.} Deterministic decoding
cannot produce the duplication: \textbf{0 of 893} duplicated first passes
arose from identical prompts, and the endpoint is measurably nondeterministic
at temperature $0$. Normalization \emph{raises} the all-message rate from
$0.842$ to $0.927$, so the exact measure undercounts there too. It is not
retry convergence, since it is measured on the first pass by construction.
Nor is it an easy-problem artifact in the direction that would matter:
functional collapse is high in every difficulty band, and on the hardest
stratum it is $0.636$ (gpt-oss) and $0.921$ (gemma-4).

\subsection{A logging defect bounds what the duplication rate can mean}
\label{subsec:logging}

We found, and report rather than quietly repair, a defect that constrains the
duplication measure specifically. Our runner logged each response's
\texttt{content} field and fell back to the \texttt{reasoning} field only when
\texttt{content} was \emph{empty}. gpt-oss-120b is a reasoning model that
frequently returns a short content string alongside a long hidden reasoning
trace; verified live against the endpoint, one request returned 17 characters
of content and 546 characters of reasoning. Such a response was logged as the
17 characters and the derivation discarded, while the flag meant to document
the substitution stayed false.

Flagging any message whose visible characters per second of latency falls far
below the corpus norm, \textbf{$84.6\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B messages are
affected, and $97.4\%$ of its first passes contain at least one such
message}; the documented flag reports $5.7\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are
essentially unaffected ($0.7$--$5.0\%$), so the defect is concentrated in
exactly the cell where byte duplication fires.

Restricting to first passes whose three messages all clear a visibility
threshold gives no stable estimate, because the clean subsample shrinks as
the threshold rises:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
threshold & clean $n$ & duplication \\
\midrule
$\geq 5$ ch/s  & $235$ & $0.630$ \\
$\geq 10$ ch/s & $88$  & $0.284$ \\
$\geq 20$ ch/s & $34$  & $0.176$ \\
$\geq 50$ ch/s & $12$  & $0.083$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

We therefore draw three conclusions and no fourth. \textbf{No point estimate
of gpt-oss's true duplication rate is recoverable from this run}; $0.691$ is
reported throughout as \emph{measured output duplication}, with this
sensitivity analysis attached. \textbf{Byte-identical outputs may not be
glossed as ``the agents did the same work''} --- with the reasoning discarded
we can state only that they emitted the same output. And the two results this
paper rests on are unaffected: accuracy is graded from the final answer,
which was always present, and the functional and adjudicated collapse
measures read the answer assertions, not the derivations. The logger has been
corrected to capture reasoning unconditionally; the campaign was not re-run
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

The organizational column is nearly flat and the accuracy column is not.
Every actor arrives in Arm~B with the division of labor gone --- $0.845$ to
$0.975$ --- and their accuracy responses run from a significant $-0.0301$ to
an indistinguishable $+0.0238$. \textbf{How much organization an intervention
destroys does not predict whether destroying it costs anything.}

This is the result that survived our own correction, and it is stronger than
the contrast it replaced. An earlier reading of the byte metric had the
actors losing \emph{different} amounts of organization, which left open the
reading that the actor which lost less was protected by what it kept. With
the amount lost held roughly fixed across three actors, that reading is
unavailable: the difference in cost has to come from somewhere other than
the visible organization, and \S\ref{subsec:mechanism} locates it.

\paragraph{The Llama-3.3 row is a collapse-rate corroboration only.} Its
accuracy on this benchmark is $0.22$--$0.35$ and its interval spans zero at
$n=84$. We use it to show that a third actor also loses the organization, and
draw no accuracy conclusion from it.

\paragraph{One conflation we explicitly avoid.} On the subset of problems
where gpt-oss goes from differentiated to byte-duplicated, B$-$A is $+0.0146$
$[-0.0011,+0.0303]$; its whole-arm B$-$A is $+0.0070$ $[-0.0108,+0.0255]$.
Both are indistinguishable from zero, and we write ``no detectable loss''
rather than ``a gain'' for either. Both signs were also stable as the campaign
grew, checked cumulatively in problem-completion order at 25/50/75/100\% of
the run.

\paragraph{It is not a difficulty artifact.} Stratifying the
differentiated~$\rightarrow$~duplicated subset ($n=892$) by pre-treatment
difficulty --- historical one-shot baseline accuracy on the same problem ---
B$-$A is non-negative in every band: HARD ($n=152$) $+0.0329$
$[-0.0395,+0.1053]$, MED ($n=154$) $+0.0455$ $[+0.0000,+0.0974]$, EASY
($n=586$) $+0.0017$ $[-0.0034,+0.0085]$. There is no difficulty band in which
gpt-oss pays. Functional collapse is likewise high in every band, including
the hardest ($0.636$ gpt-oss, $0.921$ gemma-4), so the phenomenon is not
confined to problems where roles were unnecessary anyway.

\subsection{Collapse is not ``the system was always a single agent''}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

The strongest objection to \S\ref{subsec:nodegradation} is that a setup where
collapse is free never required multi-agent structure, so the apparent gain
was an evaluation artifact~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}. We test it rather than
argue it, against the budget-matched single-agent protocol on the same
problems (Table~\ref{tab:transition}, lower panel).

A collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own single agent by 2.5 points, interval
excluding zero. Three agents emitting the same answer under a shared
evaluator loop is therefore \emph{not} functionally one agent: the redundant
computation buys something real. What it does not buy is anything
attributable to \emph{differentiated} roles. This is the guard against our
own overclaim, and what distinguishes our reading from the
structural-collapse critique. The comparison is conservative on the axis this
project has repeatedly found decisive: the single agent receives \emph{more}
oracle feedback (1.66--1.69 evaluator calls against our Arm~A's 1.10--1.34)
and roughly half the actor calls, so the claim is that multi-agent
computation retains value, not that the budgets are matched in every respect.
The same panel carries a fact we report rather
than bury: gemma-4's team is \emph{worse} than its own single agent in both
arms, so for that actor the organization is actively costly at this budget.

\subsection{Architectural enforcement restores the organization}
\label{subsec:enforce}

The manipulation is checked three ways and passes all three.
Answer-authorship compliance reaches exactly $1.000$ in every Arm~C and
Arm~C$'$ cell, against $0.982$ (gemma-4) and $0.604$ (gpt-oss) under soft
roles. Independently measured, the Planner emits an answer in $0.000$ of
Arm~C trajectories for all three actors. And the constraint visibly bound:
651 and 921 role-boundary rejections per Arm~C cell ($0.50$/$0.72$ per
trajectory), counted from boundary violations only, with formatting coercions
logged separately and excluded. Exact duplication falls to $0.000$
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). Enforcement holds the organization apart ---
though, as \S\ref{subsec:structnotcog} shows, only the part of it that
concerns who may speak.

\subsection{But it does not restore performance: the pre-registered primary
test is null}
\label{subsec:p4null}

\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy, and
the pre-registered primary test of this paper's original thesis --- is
null: $-0.0077$ $[-0.0318,+0.0178]$.} P3 is null in both actors as well:
C$-$A is $-0.0147$ $[-0.0324,+0.0031]$ for gemma-4 and $-0.0062$
$[-0.0240,+0.0116]$ for gpt-oss. This is pre-declared Pattern~E, whose
committed interpretation reads: \emph{the central causal theory fails; do not
force it}. We report it as a failure of that theory, not as a supporting
detail of a different one.

Two candidate explanations are ruled out rather than assumed away. Arm~C$'$
restores shared history at fixed enforcement. Under the pre-registered
ungraded-as-incorrect rule C$'-$C is $+0.0463$ $[+0.0123,+0.0802]$ for
gemma-4 and $+0.0000$ $[-0.0402,+0.0372]$ for gpt-oss; restricted to
problems graded in both arms it is $+0.0204$ $[-0.0034,+0.0442]$ and
$-0.0072$ $[-0.0361,+0.0217]$. \textbf{The two conventions disagree for
gemma-4} --- the pre-registered one is reported as primary, and the
difference is attrition, not context, since Arm~C$'$ leaves fewer
trajectories ungraded than Arm~C. Either way the context control does not
rescue Arm~C's accuracy: C$'$ remains at or below Arm~A.
Attrition is Arm-C-specific ($7.3\%$/$5.9\%$ hard failures against $0.000$ in
A and B) and we apply the pre-registered ungraded-as-incorrect rule; dropping
failures instead would make Arm~C look \emph{better} ($0.897$/$0.890$), which
is why the rule was fixed in advance and why we report both.

\paragraph{What the two nulls together mean.} Enforcement restores the
organizational measure completely and the performance not at all. Combined
with \S\ref{subsec:nodegradation}, organization can be destroyed without cost
in one actor and restored without benefit in both. Whatever the diagram is
explaining, it is not the accuracy.

\subsection{Structural boundaries are not cognitive specialization}
\label{subsec:structnotcog}

The pre-registered manipulation check was that Arm~C would drive \Pone{} ---
the Planner abstaining from \emph{computation} --- to ${\approx}1.0$.
\textbf{That objective failed}, because the check named a clause the
intervention does not enforce: the schema forbids emitting an \emph{answer},
and nothing in it stops a Planner writing $=1/27$. We re-designated the
manipulation check to answer authorship and report the \Pone{} outcome as a
finding, in that order.

The finding is that computation does not move. Under enforcement the
Planner's evaluated-equation density is $2.80$ and $3.44$ per 1{,}000
characters against $2.60$ and $2.07$ in Arm~A --- unchanged or \emph{higher}.
Density is the right instrument because the binary rate is anti-correlated
with message length and Arm~C messages are shorter; the same confound makes
Arm~B/gpt-oss score a spurious $0.726$ while emitting bare answers with no
work shown. \textbf{You can structurally stop an agent from answering without
making it stop doing the work.}

\subsection{Mechanism: roles are load-bearing for recovery, not for solving}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

If both actors lose the same organization, the difference in cost has to come
from somewhere else. Decomposing each actor's B$-$A into first-submission
success and recovery after a failed submission, paired on the same problems,
locates it (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Decomposition of the role-removal effect. gemma-4's first-submission
rate is unchanged; its loss is concentrated in recovery. Recovery is
conditioned on a failed first submission, which is post-treatment --- see the
caveat below.}
\label{tab:mechanism}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
actor & $n$ & first pass A$\rightarrow$B & recovery A$\rightarrow$B \\
\midrule
\textbf{gemma-4} & $1{,}295$ & $0.807 \rightarrow 0.806$
  & $\mathbf{0.200 \rightarrow 0.048}$ \\
 & & ($\Delta -0.001$) & ($\Delta -0.152$) \\
gpt-oss & $1{,}288$ & $0.724 \rightarrow 0.771$ & $0.346 \rightarrow 0.241$ \\
 & & ($\Delta +0.047$) & ($\Delta -0.106$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Removing roles costs gemma-4 essentially nothing on problems it solves
outright: its first-submission rate moves by $-0.001$. What changes is what
happens after a wrong first answer, where its recovery rate falls from
$0.200$ to $0.048$. gpt-oss also loses recovery ($0.346 \rightarrow 0.241$)
but gains first-pass ($+0.047$), and the two roughly cancel --- which is the
shape of its null.

What differs is whether the team can disagree with itself. In Arm~B, on first
passes where all three agents state an answer, gemma-4 is unanimous in
$0.961$ of trajectories against gpt-oss's $0.796$, so gpt-oss retains
disagreement five times more often ($0.204$ versus $0.039$). Unanimity is
costly when it is wrong: among trajectories that are unanimous and fail the
first submission, gemma-4 goes on to reach the right answer $0.062$ of the
time and changes its answer at all only $0.056$ of the time, against gpt-oss's
$0.165$ and $0.353$.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Role differentiation is load-bearing for error recovery, not for
first-pass solving --- and only for the actor that cannot otherwise escape a
wrong consensus.}
\end{quote}

This also explains \S\ref{subsec:p4null}. Enforcing answer authorship restores
who may speak; it does not restore whether the agents \emph{differ}. Arm~C
gives back the boundary and not the disagreement, and disagreement is what the
recovering actor needed. Three caveats bound the claim. Recovery is
conditioned on needing a retry, which is post-treatment, so this is a
decomposition and not a causal mediation. The conditioning set is also not
matched across arms: in Arm~A, $59\%$ (gemma-4) and $22\%$ (gpt-oss) of failed
first submissions were never retried before the round budget ran out, against
$0\%$ and $2\%$ in Arm~B, so the Arm~A recovery denominators are a selected
subset and the level of the A-side rates should not be read as an unconditional
recovery probability. And the retry loop is oracle-gated, so ``recovery''
means recovery \emph{with} an oracle signal --- a property of this protocol
family that bounds the mechanism's external validity.
