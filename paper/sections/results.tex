\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem; trajectories never producing a graded answer count as
incorrect (the first-pass contrast of \S\ref{subsec:collapse} excepted),
and significance is exact McNemar on discordant pairs.

\subsection{R1. Removing roles changes the organization, not the first
answer}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Removing the role instructions changes nothing else: same three agents,
same order, same budget, same evaluator. The claim rests on mechanical
measures that involve no judge. We ask whether \emph{every}
agent independently asserts its own final answer on the first pass, so the
measure is not length-confounded (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}); once the
Planner's \textsc{must not} is gone and every agent is asked for its
current best answer, it rises from $0.000$--$0.120$ to $0.846$--$0.976$. \textbf{Decomposed by role, the
planner supplies almost all of the change}: the executor is at ceiling
either way and the reviewer was already high, while the planner moves
$0.000$/$0.183$/$0.000$ to $0.979$/$0.898$/$0.889$ (gemma-4 / gpt-oss /
Llama-3.3) --- a claim about the \emph{change}, not the level.

\paragraph{Content duplication, against the right null.} gemma-4's and
Llama-3.3's role-free planners write full independent solutions
(${\approx}1{,}880$ characters, median) before boxing an answer.
Numeric-token overlap confirms it, but only against a
\emph{per-condition} null: pairing planners with executors from different
problems shows the baseline is itself condition-dependent, so the raw
difference is invalid. Against each condition's own null the excess shifts
$+0.290$ and $+0.287$ when roles are removed. gpt-oss cannot be
read at all: its role-free planner emits a median $37$ visible characters
--- the logging defect below.

\paragraph{A second opinion, and the leak that stops it being blind.} We
put 171 trajectories to a frontier model against a rubric frozen before
the first judgement, with actor, condition, correctness and the automated
label withheld and role words neutralized (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}, last
column; stability $0.953$). It is model adjudication, not human
annotation, and outcome-blind but \emph{not} condition-blind: neutralization renamed the role words without
equalizing their \emph{presence} --- a neutralized token appears in all $42$
role-structured items and $3$ of $56$ role-free ones (Fisher exact
$p=1.5\times10^{-24}$) --- so a judge noticing only whether a transcript
labels its speakers could separate the conditions without reading content.
Normalizing to a common surface would destroy the differences being judged,
so we disclose rather than re-run; we do not call it blinded, and the
collapse claim does not rest on it. Judge stability and the byte metric's precision/recall are
within-item, so unaffected.

\paragraph{First-pass accuracy does not follow.} The ablation removed the
organization without crippling the solver, the parity prior role ablations
report~\cite{dylan2026equal,orogat2026architectural}. On problems graded
under both conditions ($n=1{,}600$ / $1{,}592$ / $389$) the first-pass
difference is \emph{identical} for gemma-4 ($+0.0000$,
$[-0.0156,+0.0150]$) and flat for Llama-3.3 ($+0.0077$), while it
\emph{improves} for gpt-oss ($+0.0503$, $[+0.0339,+0.0666]$;
Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}A). Whatever roles buy, they do not buy the
first answer.

\paragraph{A logging defect, and its second consequence.}
\label{subsec:logging}
Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content}, falling back to
\texttt{reasoning} only when content was empty; gpt-oss-120b often returns
a short content string alongside a long hidden trace, so the derivation was
discarded while the flag documenting the substitution stayed false. The
same string is what the next agent received, identically in every
condition: the defect is in the record, not the run.
$75.7\%$ of gpt-oss role-free messages are affected and $93.4\%$
of its first passes contain one; the flag reports $5.8\%$, and the other
two actors are unaffected. Two consequences follow and no third.
No point estimate of gpt-oss's duplication rate is recoverable,
and the defect bounds collapse-\emph{mode} interpretation:
calling that 37-character planner a behavioral mode would attribute our
defect to the model. \textbf{It does not manufacture the collapse}, the
one place it could reach a headline measure: restricted to first passes
with \emph{no} affected message, role-free collapse is $0.821$ ($n=106$)
against $0.846$ overall and the role-structured rate $0.118$ against
$0.120$ --- the gap is intact on clean messages.

adaptive}

That would be the whole result if performance were a single number, but
a team that answers also has to repair.

\paragraph{What a cycle is.} The pipeline runs a fixed grid of six
deliberation cycles --- three outer rounds of two inner rounds --- each one
pass of Planner~$\rightarrow$~Executor~$\rightarrow$~Reviewer, and a further
cycle runs whether or not the Reviewer approved. The Reviewer decides
whether a cycle ends in a \emph{submission} or a \emph{revision}. On
approval the current answer goes to the oracle-gated evaluator, which
returns PASS --- the trajectory ends --- or FAIL with a non-leaking repair
hint; without approval nothing is submitted and the next cycle carries the
Reviewer's critique forward. Every trajectory therefore splits its realized
cycles into submissions and revisions.

\paragraph{Recovery and final accuracy.} The role-structured team receives
the evaluator's feedback, so the matched comparison is with the role-free
team that receives it too. \textbf{That team recovers more, not less}:
$0.609$ against $0.222$ (marginal, Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}B), and on
the $236$ problems both first answer wrongly, $89$ are recovered only
without roles and $4$ only with them ($p=6.2\times10^{-22}$). Both reach
the same first-pass rate and a correct first answer is never lost, so this
becomes final accuracy: $0.921$ against $0.846$, with $137$ problems
correct only without roles and $17$ only with them ($p=1.7\times10^{-24}$,
conditioned on nothing). By first-pass stratum the $+120$ is $+85$ on the
$236$ problems both miss and $+36$ on the $81+79$ that only one misses,
less one never-judged trajectory. The recoveries are revisions, not
re-gradings: in $192$ of the $193$ the passing answer differs from the one
first rejected ($541$ of $547$ across the five cells with feedback
matched), and grader noise ($0.0072$, the rate at which identical evaluator
prompts disagree) is an order of magnitude below the difference.

\paragraph{Reproducibility, drift and leakage.} The two gemma-4
feedback-matched cells, on which the main contrast rests, were run twice,
independently and with identical code. The second run reproduces the first
(Omni-MATH recovery $0.586$ against $0.609$, final accuracy $0.922$ against
$0.921$; SciBench recovery $0.435$ against $0.419$, its paired test at
$p=0.052$ against $0.013$); we report the first throughout (supplement).
The feedback-matched condition ran after the freeze, but a re-run of the
role-structured condition on $400$ frozen problems reproduces its frozen
cell (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
Repair hints contain the gold answer verbatim in $2.3\%$ of failing
verdicts with roles and $2.2\%$ without; dropping every trajectory that saw
one leaves $0.599$ against $0.212$.

\paragraph{Scope across actors.} Both other actors repeat the ordering, and
in both the feedback leaves first-pass accuracy where it was ($0.765$ and
$0.317$, against $0.762$ and $0.315$ with it withheld). gpt-oss recovers
$0.552$ against $0.324$ with roles (on the $319$ problems both teams first
answer wrongly, $101$ are recovered only without roles and $17$ only with
them, $p=1\times10^{-15}$) and reaches $0.894$ final accuracy against
$0.803$ ($170$ against $24$ problems, $p=3\times10^{-28}$). Llama-3.3
recovers $0.147$ against $0.014$ ($26$ against $1$ of $250$,
$p=4\times10^{-7}$) and reaches $0.415$ against $0.315$ ($50$ against $10$,
$p=1.6\times10^{-7}$); its role-structured team reaches a second verdict in
only $10$ of $276$ failed trajectories, and there enforcement recovers
about as much as the role-free team with feedback ($0.139$, paired
$p=0.17$). Against the feedback-withheld baseline, by contrast, the
role-structured advantage is clean only for gemma-4, directional for
gpt-oss ($0.324$ against $0.247$, paired $p=0.208$) and absent for
Llama-3.3 ($0.014$ against $0.015$).

\subsection{R3. Adaptation needs the feedback; roles set the allocation}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

\paragraph{The feedback-withheld baseline.} Without the feedback, the
role-free team turns nearly its whole budget into submissions. Across
gemma-4's first-pass-wrong trajectories it submits in $5.80$ of $5.83$ cycles
(Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}C), its Reviewer never declines in $98.1\%$
of them, and in $84.5\%$ the entire transcript contains exactly one
distinct answer --- against $5.1\%$ and $28.8\%$ with roles. Nothing in its
prompts carries the feedback (\S\ref{sec:method}): each FAIL is followed
by another cycle over the same history, so from inside the transcript
nothing has happened. Given the verdict and hint, the identical prompts
produce a different team, though not a more organized one --- all three
agents still assert their own answer on $0.974$ of first passes
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}) --- yet the answer moves (one distinct answer in
$6.6\%$ of failed trajectories) and recovery rises from $16/316=0.051$ to
$193/317=0.609$: of the $265$ problems both miss first, $139$ are recovered
only with the feedback and $2$ only without ($p=7\times10^{-39}$). The
first answer is untouched: first-pass accuracy is $0.802$ in both runs, and
the first verdict agrees on $1{,}496$ of the $1{,}598$ problems judged in
both. Of the $16$ recoveries without feedback, $7$ are the same answer
graded again. \textbf{Adaptation needs the feedback, not the roles.}

\paragraph{What roles do instead.} Roles still shape the response to
feedback, as an allocation of the budget (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}C).
The role-structured team spends $3.98$ of its $5.52$ cycles revising
internally and submits $1.54$ times per failed trajectory; with the
feedback but without roles, the split is $0.76$ and $3.20$. Part of this is written into
the contract: the role-structured Reviewer may not approve in the cycle
that receives a FAIL. Each allocation buys something. The role-structured
team's resubmissions are more often right --- its second verdict passes in
$0.489$ of the trajectories that reach one, against $0.438$, and each
verdict after the first yields $0.41$ recoveries against $0.28$ --- but only
$133$ of its $316$ failed trajectories reach a second verdict at all, while
the role-free team draws more verdicts with \emph{fewer} actor calls
($11.9$ against $16.6$ per failed trajectory). With an exact oracle and a
six-verdict cap, verdicts are what pays: counting only the first round of
feedback, the role-free team still recovers $0.438$ against $0.206$. Across
the conditions that receive feedback, recovery rises with submissions ---
$0.222$ at $1.54$, $0.314$ at $2.06$ under enforcement, $0.609$ at $3.20$.

\subsection{R4. The same ordering in a second domain}
\label{subsec:xdomain}

Everything so far is competition mathematics, where a wrong answer is a
discrete object a team can hold or discard. SciBench --- the complete
$574$-problem matched slice of free-response college science, frozen
separately --- tests whether the pattern belongs to the organization or to
the benchmark. The role-free prompt is byte-identical to its Omni-MATH
form; the role-structured one keeps the same structural contract with
domain wording adapted, as does the evaluator. For gemma-4 the ordering
returns (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}, right-hand group). First pass is
flat ($0.794$ with roles; $0.794$ and $0.796$ without). Recovery is
$0.051$ without feedback, $0.419$ with it and $0.254$ with roles; on the
$100$ problems both teams with feedback first answer wrongly, $21$ are recovered
only without roles and $7$ only with them ($p=0.013$), and final accuracy
is $0.880$ against $0.847$ ($31$ against $12$ problems, $p=0.0054$). One
part does not transfer: here the role-structured answer does not lock (one
distinct answer in $7.6\%$ of failed trajectories, against $8.5\%$), and
with one round of feedback the two recover equally ($0.237$ against
$0.239$), so on SciBench the difference is entirely the additional
verdicts. gpt-oss repeats the ordering on SciBench: $0.483$ with feedback
against $0.315$ with roles and $0.231$ without feedback (paired $18$
against $7$, $p=0.043$), and final accuracy $0.892$ against $0.852$ ($30$
against $7$ problems, $p=2\times10^{-4}$).

\subsection{R5. Structural enforcement shifts the allocation, and
introduces brittleness}
\label{subsec:enforce}

Enforced and role-structured prompts carry the same advice slot, so this
contrast is matched on feedback. Enforcement replaces the request with a constraint, making the
division of labor unavoidable at the interface, where no wording can talk
its way around it. The manipulation check
passes: the planner asserts a final answer in $0.000$ of enforced first
passes in all three actors against $0.979$/$0.898$/$0.889$ under generic
prompts, as in \S\ref{subsec:collapse},
with $838$/$1{,}183$/$395$ role-boundary rejections per cell (formatting
coercions excluded by a definition fixed in advance). Relative to the
role-structured team, enforcement \textbf{un-freezes the answer}
($0.119$/$0.092$/$0.072$ against $0.288$/$0.052$/$0.750$, all but gpt-oss)
and submits more often ($2.06$/$2.39$/$1.89$ against $1.54$/$2.21$/$1.06$
per failed trajectory). It does not change what the planner \emph{computes},
so an agent can be structurally stopped from answering without being
stopped from doing the work.

Recovery follows for Llama-3.3; for the other two the direction is
consistent but not resolvable against attrition. Recovery is computed on
every trajectory with a recorded incorrect first verdict, including the
$150$ enforced trajectories that received one then died in regeneration ---
all necessarily first-pass wrong, since a passing verdict ends the loop,
and scored $0$ by the preregistration. On that denominator enforcement
recovers at $0.314$/$0.395$/$0.139$ against $0.222$/$0.324$/$0.014$, but
the paired contrast survives only for Llama-3.3 ($p=3.8\times10^{-6}$);
gemma-4 ($p=0.298$) and gpt-oss ($p=0.105$) do not. The direction is the
one the allocation account of \S\ref{subsec:mechanism} predicts: in every
actor the condition that submits more recovers more. Conditioning instead on
successful grading would manufacture significance out of the same one-sided
attrition we reject for accuracy below.

\textbf{Improved revisability does not become end-to-end accuracy, because
enforcement is brittle.} Under the pre-registered rule the accuracy
difference is $-0.0225$ / $-0.0050$ / $+0.0175$ (gemma-4 / gpt-oss /
Llama-3.3): one significant negative, two nulls. Dropping the
$8.0\%$/$9.9\%$/$17.0\%$ of enforced trajectories that never finish ---
against $0.0$--$0.7\%$ role-structured and $0.0$--$2.5\%$ role-free ---
gives $+0.0190$ / $+0.0361$ / $+0.0453$: all positive, all significant,
ordered by actor weakness, exactly the published capability-equalizer
direction~\cite{canedo2026architecture}. \textbf{That second reading is
survivorship}: the preregistration fixed in advance that such trajectories
count incorrect, and the benefit appears only after conditioning
on successful parsing. Restoring shared history at fixed enforcement does
not rescue it either --- attrition, not context. Both scorings with
intervals are tabulated in the supplement.

The enforced recovery and first-pass rates do not
compose into that accuracy column --- the never-graded trajectories break
the shared denominator the accounting of \S\ref{subsec:recovery} needs
(supplement).
