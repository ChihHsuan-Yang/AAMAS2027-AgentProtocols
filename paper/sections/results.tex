\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that trajectories never
producing a graded answer count as incorrect; significance is exact McNemar
on discordant pairs. Holm correction across the two testable pre-registered
families changes no verdict (P3, gemma-4 Holm $p=0.0120$; P4, all retained).
\textbf{The A-versus-B contrast supplying this paper's headline was
pre-registered as secondary and exploratory.} Every conditional analysis
below conditions on a post-treatment variable and is descriptive, never
causal.

\subsection{R1. Removing role instructions destroys the division of labor}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Arm~B deletes every role instruction and changes nothing else. \textbf{This
claim rests on the mechanical measures, which involve no judge}; the
adjudication below is a second opinion whose blinding is partial. We ask
whether \emph{every} agent independently asserts its own final answer on the
first pipeline pass, so the measure is not confounded with trajectory length
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). It rises from $0.000$--$0.120$ to
$0.846$--$0.976$. \textbf{Decomposed by role, the planner supplies almost
all of the change}: the executor is at ceiling in both arms
($0.907$--$1.000$); the reviewer, already high, moves
$0.781$/$0.492$/$0.823$ to $0.998$/$0.965$/$0.961$ (gemma-4 / Llama-3.3 /
gpt-oss); the planner moves $0.000$/$0.000$/$0.183$ to
$0.979$/$0.889$/$0.898$. ``The planner carries the signal'' is a statement
about the \emph{change}, not the level.

\paragraph{Exact duplication is sound and far too narrow.} Byte-identical
text between two agents fires on $0.680$ of gpt-oss Arm~B first passes and
essentially never elsewhere ($\leq 0.008$) --- a property of the instrument
(recall $0.262$), not of the actors, and \textbf{never used to compare how
much organization two actors lost}.

\paragraph{A second opinion, and the leak that stops it being blind.} We put
171 trajectories --- $42$ Arm~A, $56$ Arm~B, $44$ Arm~C, $29$ Arm~C$'$ --- to
a frontier model against a rubric frozen before the first judgement, with
actor, arm, correctness and the automated label withheld and role words
neutralized. It finds the division of labor intact in $1.000$ of Arm~A
trajectories and absent in $0.857$--$1.000$ of Arm~B, stable across
repetitions at $0.953$. This is \textbf{model adjudication, not human
annotation}, and not ground truth.

\textbf{It is also outcome-blind but \emph{not} condition-blind.}
Neutralization renamed the role words without equalizing their
\emph{presence}: a neutralized \texttt{Agent-P/E/R} token appears in all
$42$ Arm~A items and $3$ of the $56$ Arm~B items (Fisher exact
$p=1.5\times10^{-24}$), so a judge noticing only whether a transcript labels
its speakers could separate the arms without reading content. Format
separates them further: $39$ of $44$ Arm~C items carry a JSON
\texttt{answer} field where every Arm~B item carries \verb|\boxed|.
Normalizing to a common surface form would destroy the differences being
judged, so we disclose rather than re-run. \textbf{We therefore do not call
the arm-contrastive adjudication blinded}, and the collapse claim does not
rest on it. Judge stability and the byte metric's precision/recall are
within-item, so unaffected.

\paragraph{Two of three actors visibly duplicate; the third is
uninstrumented.} gemma-4's and Llama-3.3's Arm~B planners write full
independent solutions (median $1{,}876$ and $1{,}892$ characters) before
boxing an answer. Numeric-token overlap confirms this, but only against the
right null: pairing planners with executors from \emph{different} problems
shows the baseline is itself arm-dependent, so the raw A$-$B difference is
not valid. Against each arm's own null the excess is
$+0.449$/$+0.432$ in Arm~B against $+0.159$/$+0.145$ in Arm~A --- a
replicated shift of $+0.290$ and $+0.287$. \textbf{gpt-oss cannot be read at
all}: its Arm~B planner emits a median of $37$ visible characters in $26.7$
seconds --- $2.85$ characters per second against $195$ in its own Arm~A ---
with \texttt{used\_reasoning\_fallback} false in all $2{,}302$
sub-100-character messages. That is the logging defect below, so
\textbf{for this actor we cannot say what the planner did}.

\subsection{A logging defect, and its second consequence}
\label{subsec:logging}

Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content} field, falling back to
its \texttt{reasoning} field only when content was empty; gpt-oss-120b
often
returns a short content string alongside a long hidden reasoning trace, so
the derivation was discarded while the flag documenting the substitution
stayed false. Flagging messages below $10$ visible characters per second of
latency, \textbf{$75.7\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B role messages are affected and
$93.4\%$ of its first passes contain at least one}; the documented flag
reports $5.8\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are unaffected ($0.1$--$0.5\%$).

Two consequences follow and no third. \textbf{No point estimate of gpt-oss's
duplication rate is recoverable}, and \textbf{the defect also bounds
collapse-\emph{mode} interpretation}: calling that 37-character planner a
behavioral mode would attribute our logging defect to the model, and it is
why byte duplication fires only here --- when only a boxed answer survives,
identical answers are identical bytes. The paper's claims are unaffected:
accuracy is graded from a final answer always present, and the functional and
adjudicated measures read answer assertions, not derivations. The campaign
was not re-run (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{R2. First-pass accuracy does not suffer --- our control
condition}
\label{subsec:firstpass}

\emph{This is not offered as a finding} but as the control condition it is:
the ablation removed the organization without crippling the
solver~\cite{dylan2026equal,orogat2026architectural}. On matched problems
first-pass B$-$A is \emph{identical} for gemma-4 ($+0.0000$, CI
$[-0.0156,+0.0150]$) and flat for Llama-3.3 ($+0.0077$, CI
$[-0.0283,+0.0437]$), while it \emph{improves} for gpt-oss ($+0.0503$, CI
$[+0.0339,+0.0666]$). Whatever roles buy, they do not buy the first
answer.

\subsection{R3. Recovery after a wrong first answer collapses}
\label{subsec:recovery}

Conditional on the first submission being graded wrong, the role-free team
recovers far less often (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}): gemma-4 falls $0.222
\rightarrow 0.051$ ($-0.1709$ $[-0.2247,-0.1171]$), gpt-oss $0.324
\rightarrow 0.247$ ($-0.0766$ $[-0.1361,-0.0153]$). Paired on problems
first-pass-wrong under \emph{both} arms, gemma-4 recovers on $37$ problems
Arm~B misses against $3$ the other way (exact McNemar
$p=2.0\times10^{-8}$).

\textbf{For gemma-4 this is not a post-treatment conditional at all.} Its
first-pass rates are equal to four decimals ($0.8025$ in both arms), so in
$\Delta_{\text{final}} = \Delta_{\text{fp}}(1-\text{rec}_A) +
(1-\text{fp}_B)\,\Delta_{\text{rec}}$ the first term vanishes and the
recovery contrast is an exact reparameterization of the intention-to-treat
effect: $-0.1709 \times (1-0.8025) = -0.0338$, the observed B$-$A
final-accuracy difference, residual zero. A quantity algebraically equal to
the randomized contrast is not an artifact of post-treatment conditioning.
\textbf{This does not extend to gpt-oss}, whose first-pass rates differ by
$+0.050$.

\textbf{Llama-3.3 does not replicate this, and it is a genuine null rather
than a floor.} Its rates are $0.014$ and $0.015$ --- four recoveries out of
$276$ Arm~A and four out of $268$ Arm~B first-pass-wrong trajectories. Its
recovery channel exists and is movable --- enforcement raises it to $0.139$,
paired $0.009$ versus $0.096$, $p=3.8\times10^{-6}$
(\S\ref{subsec:enforce}) --- so what is absent is an effect of the
\emph{role-instruction} manipulation, not the capacity to repair. The test
has ${\approx}80\%$ power against an excess of $0.04$--$0.05$ where
gemma-4's effect is $0.171$: informative against a gemma-sized effect,
uninformative below it.

\paragraph{The accuracy contrasts against grader noise.} Repeated identical
evaluator prompts at temperature $0$ disagree on $20$ of $2{,}790$ distinct
graded strings, a group-level rate of $0.0072$ ($0.0017$--$0.0072$ across
estimators); the key must be the exact candidate bytes. \textbf{We do not
divide an effect size by a disagreement rate} --- different quantities, and
the noise largely cancels in a paired contrast --- but propagate it into the
interval, flipping each graded outcome independently with the per-call rate
$0.0062$ inside the bootstrap. gemma-4's B$-$A survives
($[-0.0494,-0.0175]$); \textbf{gpt-oss's ($[-0.0013,+0.0331]$) and
Llama-3.3's ($[-0.0350,+0.0400]$) do not}, so for those actors the contrast
is not resolvable above grader noise rather than absent.

\subsection{R4. The mechanism is not premature stopping}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

The standing explanation is premature consensus --- the team stops early.
\textbf{We test it and reject it.} Every arm runs the same six deliberation
cycles, and Arm~B in fact draws \emph{more} oracle feedback than Arm~A
(\S\ref{sec:method}). Realized cycle spend is near-identical ($5.52$ vs
$5.83$, gemma-4, first-pass-wrong); what differs is their use. Arm~B
converts $5.80$ of its $5.83$ cycles into submissions while Arm~A submits
$1.54$ times and spends $3.98$ revising. \textbf{No trajectory in any cell
was cut short}; the role-free team never uses a cycle to think again. What
stops is refusal: in $98.1\%$ of gemma-4's Arm~B first-pass-wrong
trajectories the Reviewer never declines to submit, against $5.1\%$ in
Arm~A ($61.6\%$ vs $4.3\%$ for gpt-oss). The consequence is a frozen answer,
unchanged in $0.845$ of those Arm~B trajectories against $0.288$ of Arm~A's
--- Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and the point is the
\textbf{inversion}: multi-agency is the prescribed cure that role-free
multi-agency does not deliver.

\paragraph{A competing driver we do not dismiss.} Accumulated context is a
real cause of recovery failure~\cite{yang2026contamination}: within Arm~A
alone, gpt-oss recovery falls $0.923 \to 0.070$ across pooled context
quartiles. It does not \emph{account for} the arm gap, which survives
context matching in the lower quartiles ($0.662$ vs $0.151$, gemma-4;
$0.656$ vs $0.293$, gpt-oss) --- \textbf{but in gpt-oss's top quartile the
ordering reverses} ($0.070$ vs $0.189$). \textbf{Read these as directional
only}: quartiles are cut on the pooled A$+$B distribution and context is
nearly a function of arm, so the strata are partly arm indicators --- Q1
holds $198$ Arm~B trajectories against $13$ Arm~A.

\subsection{R5. Enforcement restores organization and recovery, not
accuracy}
\label{subsec:enforce}

The manipulation check passes: the planner authors an answer in $0.000$ of
Arm~C trajectories in all three actors, against $0.986$, $0.916$ and $0.958$
under generic prompts, with $838$/$1{,}183$/$395$ role-boundary rejections
per cell (formatting coercions excluded by a definition fixed in advance).
Arm~C also \textbf{un-freezes the answer}: at $0.119$/$0.092$/$0.072$ it is
far below Arm~B's $0.845$/$0.208$/$0.806$ everywhere, and below Arm~A for
gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 ($0.288$, $0.750$); only gpt-oss's Arm~A, the study's
least frozen cell at $0.052$, sits lower.

\textbf{It restores recovery for Llama-3.3; for the other two the direction
is consistent but not resolvable against attrition.} Recovery is computed on
every trajectory with a recorded incorrect first verdict, including the $150$
Arm~C trajectories that received one then died in regeneration --- all
necessarily first-pass wrong, since a passing first verdict ends the loop,
and scored $0$ by the preregistration. On that denominator Arm~C recovers at
$0.314$/$0.395$/$0.139$ against Arm~A's $0.222$/$0.324$/$0.014$, but the
paired contrast survives only for Llama-3.3 ($0.009$ vs $0.096$,
$p=3.8\times10^{-6}$); gemma-4 ($p=0.298$) and gpt-oss ($p=0.105$) do not.
Conditioning on successful grading instead would manufacture significance
out of the same one-sided attrition we reject for accuracy below, and it
flatters Arm~C twice over: it would also lift Arm~C's first-pass column from
the $0.779$/$0.761$/$0.281$ reported here to $0.822$/$0.788$/$0.295$.

\textbf{These Arm~C rates do not compose into Table~\ref{tab:armc}'s
C$-$A, and the attrition is why.} \S\ref{subsec:recovery} teaches the reader
to multiply a recovery contrast by $(1-\text{fp})$ and recover the accuracy
contrast; done here it returns a \emph{positive} value for all three actors
against the table's one significant negative and two nulls. The identity is
exact only when both columns share a denominator, and under Arm~C's
attrition they do not: first pass and recovery are computed over the
trajectories that received an evaluator verdict, final accuracy over all
$1{,}600$/$1{,}600$/$400$ with the never-graded scored $0$ by the
preregistration. Composing the columns silently re-scores those as if they
had been graded --- the as-graded reading, which is what the composed values
reproduce. \textbf{The A-versus-B composition in \S\ref{subsec:recovery} is
valid because Arms~A and B have essentially no attrition}
($0.0$--$2.5\%$).

\textbf{It does not restore final accuracy, and this is where the scoring
rule decides the answer} (Table~\ref{tab:armc}). Under the pre-registered
rule C$-$A is $-0.0225$ / $-0.0050$ / $+0.0175$: one significant negative,
two nulls. Dropping ungraded trajectories gives $+0.0190$ / $+0.0361$ /
$+0.0453$ --- all positive, all significant, ordered by actor weakness,
exactly the published capability-equalizer
direction~\cite{canedo2026architecture}. \textbf{That reading is
survivorship}, and the preregistration fixed in advance that such
trajectories count incorrect: \textbf{the benefit appears only after
conditioning on successful parsing.}

Enforcement also fails to change what the planner \emph{computes}:
evaluated-equation density is $3.11$/$4.78$ per $1{,}000$ characters in

\subsection{R6. The pre-registered primary test is null}
\label{subsec:p4null}

\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy, and
the pre-registered primary test of this paper's original thesis --- is null
in all three pairwise difference-in-differences: $+0.0175$
$[-0.0050,+0.0412]$, $+0.0400$ $[+0.0000,+0.0794]$, $+0.0225$
$[-0.0188,+0.0631]$.} We report it as the failure of the theory it was
written to test. \textbf{Only one of the three is an informative null.} The
largest per-actor C$-$A main effect is $|{-}0.0225|$, so a true interaction
is plausibly ${\leq}0.040$; only the gpt-oss$-$gemma-4 contrast can see that
(MDE $0.033$ at $80\%$ power, $n=1{,}600$). The two Llama contrasts (MDE
$0.069$ and $0.075$ at $n=400$) \textbf{cannot detect an interaction of any
plausible size} and are uninformative rather than evidence of absence.

The pre-declared not-submit condition was a null P4 \emph{and} a main effect
matching the published capability-equalizer direction. Against the final data
the pre-registered C$-$A main effect matches no monotone direction ---
negative for the strongest actor, null for the middle --- so the condition is
not met; it is met on its face only under the as-graded scoring
\S\ref{subsec:enforce} shows to be survivorship. Arm~C$'$ restores shared
history at fixed enforcement and does not rescue the result: C$'-$C is
$+0.0450$ $[+0.0125,+0.0775]$ and $-0.0125$ $[-0.0450,+0.0225]$
pre-registered, $+0.0196$ and $-0.0089$ as graded --- the difference is
attrition, not context.

\subsection{Collapse is not ``the system was always a single agent''}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

We test the standing objection that a setup where collapse is free never
required multi-agent structure~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} against a
budget-matched single-agent protocol on the same problems
(Table~\ref{tab:main}, lower panel). \textbf{This control comes from a previously
released trace corpus rather than our own campaign}, joined by problem
identifier (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). It refuses both easy readings. A
collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own single agent ($+0.0294$), so three
agents emitting the same answer under a shared evaluator loop is not
functionally one agent. \textbf{But gemma-4's team is significantly worse
than its own in both arms} ($-0.0225$, $-0.0563$) --- and gemma-4 is the
actor our recovery result rests on. \emph{Neither} ``multi-agent systems are
just single agents'' \emph{nor} ``more agents help'' survives our own data.
\textbf{The two single-agent arms were built from different source commits},
so each direction is a sound directional guard while \emph{the size of the
gap between actors is not}. The comparison is conservative on the axis this
design makes decisive --- the single agent receives \emph{more} oracle
feedback ($1.66$--$1.69$ evaluator calls against Arm~A's $1.11$--$1.35$) and
half the actor calls.
