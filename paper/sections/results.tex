\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that trajectories which
never produced a graded answer count as incorrect; P1--P5 carry a Holm
correction. \textbf{The A-versus-B contrast supplying this paper's headline
was pre-registered as secondary and exploratory, and is post-hoc wherever it
appears.} Every conditional analysis below conditions on a post-treatment
variable and is descriptive, never causal.

\subsection{R1. Removing role instructions destroys the division of labor in
every actor}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Arm~B deletes every role instruction and changes nothing else. We measure
failure of the property the diagram asserts --- that the agents do different
jobs --- by asking whether \emph{every} agent independently asserts its own
final answer on the first pipeline pass, so the measure is not confounded with
trajectory length (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). It rises from $0.000$--$0.120$
under soft roles to $0.846$--$0.976$.
\textbf{Decomposed by role, the planner carries the entire signal}: the
executor is at ceiling in both arms ($0.907$--$1.000$), while the planner
asserts an answer in $0.000$/$0.000$/$0.183$ of Arm~A first passes (gemma-4 /
Llama-3.3 / gpt-oss) against $0.979$/$0.889$/$0.898$ in Arm~B. The measure is
therefore planner abstention --- \Pone{} inverted and made presence-based, not
a new construct.

\paragraph{Exact duplication is sound and far too narrow.} Byte-identical text
between two agents fires on $0.680$ of gpt-oss Arm~B first passes and
essentially never elsewhere ($\leq 0.008$) --- a gap that is a property of
the instrument, not of the actors. Against blinded adjudication its precision
is $1.000$ (0 false positives in 17 firings) and its recall $0.262$, so it is
a conservative lower bound and we \textbf{never use it to compare how much
organization two actors lost}. Only $2$ of $1{,}094$ duplicated first passes
came from agents receiving identical prompts, so it is not a prompt artifact,
and normalization only raises it ($0.680 \to 0.699$).

\paragraph{Validation by blinded adjudication.} Because the choice of measure
carries the result, we validated it on 171 trajectories against a rubric
frozen before the first judgement, with actor, arm, correctness and the
automated label withheld and role words neutralized. The judge finds the
division of labor intact in $1.000$ of Arm~A trajectories in all three actors
and absent in $0.857$--$1.000$ of Arm~B, stable across independent
repetitions at $0.953$. This is \textbf{model adjudication, not human annotation} and is
not ground truth (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Two of three actors visibly duplicate; the third is
uninstrumented.} gemma-4's and Llama-3.3's Arm~B planners write full
independent solutions (median $1{,}876$ and $1{,}892$ characters) before
boxing an answer. Numeric-token overlap with the executor confirms
duplication, but only against the right null: raw Jaccard is $0.719$/$0.657$
in Arm~B against $0.554$/$0.555$ in Arm~A, and pairing planners with executors
from \emph{different} problems shows the baseline is itself arm-dependent
($0.40$/$0.42$ vs $0.27$/$0.22$, tracking token-set size), so the raw A$-$B
difference is not a valid contrast. Against each arm's own null the excess is
$+0.448$ and $+0.437$ in Arm~B against $+0.157$ and $+0.138$ in Arm~A --- a
replicated shift of $+0.291$ and $+0.300$.
\textbf{gpt-oss cannot be read at all}: its Arm~B planner emits a median of
$37$ visible characters in $26.7$ seconds --- $2.85$ characters per second
against $195$ in its own Arm~A --- with \texttt{used\_reasoning\_fallback}
false in all $2{,}302$ sub-100-character messages. That is the logging defect
of \S\ref{subsec:logging}, not an abstaining planner: the work was done and
discarded by our logger, so \textbf{for this actor we cannot say what the
planner did}. Its numeric-token sets have median size $1$, which is why its
overlap row is excluded from the duplication claim.

\subsection{A logging defect, and its second consequence}
\label{subsec:logging}

Our runner logged the \texttt{content} of each response, falling back to the
\texttt{reasoning} field only when content was \emph{empty}; gpt-oss-120b
often returns a short content string alongside a long hidden reasoning trace,
so the derivation was discarded while the flag documenting the substitution
stayed false. Flagging messages below $10$ visible characters per second of
latency, \textbf{$75.7\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B role messages are affected and
$93.4\%$ of its first passes contain at least one}; the documented flag
reports $5.8\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are essentially unaffected
($0.1$--$0.5\%$).

Three conclusions follow and no fourth. \textbf{No point estimate of gpt-oss's
true duplication rate is recoverable.} \textbf{The defect also bounds
collapse-\emph{mode} interpretation} --- its second and less obvious
consequence: calling that 37-character planner a behavioral mode would
attribute our logging defect to the model, and it is also why byte duplication
fires only here, since when only a boxed answer survives, identical answers
are identical bytes. And the paper's claims are unaffected: accuracy is graded
from a final answer that was always present, and the functional and
adjudicated measures read answer assertions, not derivations. The campaign was
not re-run (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{R2. First-pass accuracy does not suffer --- our control condition}
\label{subsec:firstpass}

\emph{This is not offered as a finding.} That deleting role instructions
leaves first-pass accuracy intact replicates a published
null~\cite{dylan2026equal,orogat2026architectural}; we present it as the
control condition it is --- the ablation removed the organization without
crippling the solver. On matched problems first-pass accuracy is
\emph{identical} for gemma-4 ($0.802 \to 0.802$, $+0.0000$
$[-0.0156,+0.0150]$) and \emph{improves} for gpt-oss ($0.713 \to 0.763$,
$+0.0503$ $[+0.0339,+0.0666]$). Llama-3.3 is flat, at $+0.0077$
$[-0.0283,+0.0437]$. Whatever roles buy, they do not buy the first answer.

\subsection{R3. Recovery after a wrong first answer collapses}
\label{subsec:recovery}

Conditional on the first submission being graded wrong, the role-free team
recovers far less often (Table~\ref{tab:main}): gemma-4 falls
$0.222 \rightarrow 0.051$ ($-0.1709$ $[-0.2247,-0.1171]$), gpt-oss
$0.324 \rightarrow 0.249$ ($-0.0746$ $[-0.1342,-0.0138]$). Paired on problems
first-pass-wrong under \emph{both} arms, gemma-4 recovers on 37 problems
Arm~B misses against 3 the other way ($p=2.0\times10^{-8}$).
\textbf{Llama-3.3 does not replicate this and we report it as a floor
effect}: it recovers at $0.015$ in both arms because at $0.307$ first-pass
accuracy it is too weak a solver for the recovery channel to exist at all. It
corroborates R1 and R4 and contributes nothing to R3.

\paragraph{The accuracy contrasts against grader noise.} Repeated identical
evaluator prompts at temperature $0$ disagree on $24$ of $2{,}791$ distinct
graded strings, a group-level floor of $0.0086$ ($0.0020$--$0.0086$ across
estimators). gemma-4's $|$B$-$A$|$ of $0.0338$ clears it by $3.9\times$;
\textbf{gpt-oss's $0.0163$ and Llama-3.3's $0.0025$ do not}, so for those
actors the final-accuracy contrast is not resolvable above grader noise
rather than absent. The recovery contrasts are one to two orders of magnitude
above any version of the floor.

\subsection{R4. The mechanism is not premature stopping}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

The standing explanation for this failure is premature consensus --- the team
stops early. \textbf{We test it and reject it.} Every arm runs the same six
deliberation cycles and the evaluator fires only on Reviewer approval, so
evaluator calls count \emph{submissions}, not opportunities. Realized cycle
spend is near-identical ($5.52$ Arm~A vs $5.83$ Arm~B, gemma-4,
first-pass-wrong); what differs is their use. Arm~B converts $5.80$ of its
$5.83$ cycles into submissions while Arm~A submits $1.54$ times and spends
$3.98$ revising. \textbf{No trajectory in any cell was cut short}; the
role-free team simply never uses a cycle to think again.
What stops is refusal: in $98.1\%$ of gemma-4's Arm~B first-pass-wrong
trajectories the Reviewer never once declines to submit, against $5.1\%$ in
Arm~A ($61.8\%$ vs $4.3\%$ for gpt-oss). The consequence is a frozen answer,
which never changes at all in $0.845$ of those Arm~B trajectories against
$0.288$ of Arm~A's. This is
Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and the point is the
\textbf{inversion}: multi-agency is the prescribed cure, and multi-agency
without role differentiation does not escape it.

\paragraph{A competing driver we do not dismiss.} Accumulated context is a
real cause of recovery failure~\cite{yang2026contamination}: within Arm~A
alone, gpt-oss recovery falls $1.000 \to 0.070$ across pooled context
quartiles. It does not \emph{account for} the arm gap, which survives context
matching in the lower quartiles ($0.662$ vs $0.151$, gemma-4; $0.656$ vs
$0.293$, gpt-oss) --- \textbf{but in gpt-oss's top quartile the ordering
reverses} ($0.070$ vs $0.192$): where context is longest, Arm~A's own
contamination costs more than the roles return. All three statements stand.

\subsection{R5. Enforcement restores organization and recovery, not accuracy
--- and it costs attrition}
\label{subsec:enforce}

The symmetric intervention makes the division of labor unrepresentable rather
than merely requested. The manipulation check passes: the planner authors an
answer in $0.000$ of Arm~C trajectories in all three actors, against $0.014$,
$0.084$ and $0.042$ under generic prompts, and the constraint visibly bound
with $838$/$1{,}183$/$395$ role-boundary rejections per cell ($0.52$--$0.99$
per trajectory, formatting coercions excluded by a definition fixed in
advance). Arm~C also restores \emph{recovery}, which enforcement was not
designed to do: it reaches $0.412$/$0.461$/$0.150$ against
$0.222$/$0.324$/$0.015$ in Arm~A, above Arm~A in all three actors and
paired-significant in all three
($p=1.6\times10^{-3}$, $1.6\times10^{-3}$, $3.8\times10^{-6}$). It is also
the least frozen arm ($0.064$--$0.122$).

\textbf{It does not restore final accuracy, and this is where the scoring rule
decides the answer} (Table~\ref{tab:armc}). Under the pre-registered rule
C$-$A is $-0.0225$ / $-0.0050$ / $+0.0175$: one significant negative, two
nulls. Dropping ungraded trajectories gives $+0.0190$ / $+0.0361$ / $+0.0453$
--- all positive, all significant, ordered by actor weakness, exactly the
published capability-equalizer direction~\cite{canedo2026architecture}.
\textbf{That reading is survivorship}: Arm~C leaves $8.0\%$/$9.9\%$/$17.0\%$
ungraded against $0.0\%$/$0.1\%$/$0.7\%$ in Arm~A because its validator
rejects unparseable output until the regeneration budget is exhausted, and
the preregistration fixed in advance that such trajectories count as
incorrect. \textbf{The apparent benefit arises only after conditioning on
successful parsing}; we report the pre-registered scoring as primary with the
attrition beside it.

Enforcement also fails to change what the planner \emph{computes}: its
evaluated-equation density is $3.11$ and $4.78$ per $1{,}000$ characters in
Arm~C against $2.76$ and $2.12$ in Arm~A --- unchanged or higher. Density is
the right instrument because the binary abstention rate is anti-correlated
with message length; the same confound makes Arm~B/gpt-oss score a spurious
$0.722$ ``abstention'' on a 37-character message. \textbf{You can
structurally stop an agent from answering without making it stop doing the
work}: enforcement governs who may \emph{speak}, not whether the agents
\emph{differ}.

\subsection{R6. The pre-registered primary test is null}
\label{subsec:p4null}

\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy, and
the pre-registered primary test of this paper's original thesis --- is null in
all three pairwise difference-in-differences: $+0.0175$ $[-0.0056,+0.0412]$,
$+0.0400$ $[-0.0006,+0.0806]$, $+0.0225$ $[-0.0194,+0.0638]$.} We report it as
the failure of the theory it was written to test, not as a supporting detail
of a different one. Interaction endpoints need roughly twice the sample of a
main effect, so this is ``no large interaction detected,'' not ``no
interaction.''

The pre-declared not-submit condition was a null P4 \emph{and} a main effect
matching the published capability-equalizer direction. We evaluated it against
the final data: under the pre-registered scoring the C$-$A main effect matches
no monotone direction --- negative for the strongest actor, null for the
middle --- so the condition is not met. It is met on its face only under the
as-graded scoring \S\ref{subsec:enforce} shows to be survivorship. Arm~C$'$
restores shared history at fixed enforcement and does not rescue the result:
C$'-$C is $+0.0450$ $[+0.0125,+0.0775]$ and $-0.0125$ $[-0.0450,+0.0225]$
under the pre-registered rule, $+0.0196$ $[-0.0028,+0.0419]$ and $-0.0089$
$[-0.0357,+0.0149]$ as graded; the conventions disagree for gemma-4 and the
difference is attrition, not context.

\subsection{Collapse is not ``the system was always a single agent''}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

We test the standing objection that a setup where collapse is free never
required multi-agent structure~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} against a
budget-matched single-agent protocol on the same problems
(Table~\ref{tab:main}, bottom). \textbf{This control comes from a previously
released trace corpus rather than from our own campaign}, joined by problem
identifier; its provenance is stated in \S\ref{sec:experiments}.

The panel refuses both easy readings. A collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own
single agent by $+0.0294$ $[+0.0131,+0.0462]$, so three agents emitting the
same answer under a shared evaluator loop is not functionally one agent.
\textbf{But gemma-4's team is significantly worse than its own single agent
in both arms} ($-0.0225$, $-0.0563$) --- and gemma-4 is the actor our recovery
result rests on. Multi-agent computation is therefore not uniformly valuable:
\emph{neither} ``multi-agent systems are just single agents'' \emph{nor}
``more agents help'' survives our own data. The comparison is conservative on
the axis this design makes decisive --- the single agent receives \emph{more}
oracle feedback ($1.66$--$1.69$ evaluator calls against Arm~A's
$1.11$--$1.35$) and roughly half the actor calls.
