\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that ungraded
trajectories count as incorrect; P1--P5 carry a Holm correction. The
A-versus-B contrast supplying this paper's headline was pre-registered as
\emph{secondary and exploratory} and is labeled \textbf{post-hoc} everywhere
it appears.

\subsection{Removing role instructions collapses the organization of every
actor}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Arm~B deletes every role instruction and changes nothing else. How one
measures the surviving organization decides the answer, so we report two
measures and say which claim rests on which.

\paragraph{Exact duplication is sound and far too narrow.} The natural
automated measure is whether two or more agents emit byte-identical text,
applied to the first pipeline pass so it is not confounded with trajectory
length. It fires on $0.691$ of gpt-oss Arm~B trajectories and \textbf{never}
on gemma-4 or Llama-3.3 in any arm --- a gap that is a property of the
instrument. A blinded adjudication judges gemma-4's Arm~B trajectories
redundant at $0.944$ where this metric reports $0.000$, because its agents
paraphrase at length instead of copying, so no hash matches even though
nothing is added. \textbf{Exact duplication is therefore a conservative lower
bound: usable to say that at least this much collapse occurred, never to
compare how much organization two actors lost.}

\paragraph{Functional collapse.} We therefore measure failure of the property
the diagram asserts --- that the agents do different jobs --- by asking
whether \emph{every} agent independently asserts its own final answer
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}).

\textbf{All three actors lose the division of labor at essentially the same
rate.} Arm~B is not ``roles blurred'': $0.90$--$0.98$ of \emph{planners} emit
a final answer, against $0.00$--$0.18$ in Arm~A. Under the byte metric this
looked like a large contrast between actors; under a measure of the property
the diagram asserts, that contrast is absent.

\paragraph{Validation by blinded adjudication.} Because the choice of measure
carries the result, we validated it on 180 sampled trajectories against a
rubric frozen before the first judgement, with actor, arm, correctness and
the automated label withheld, and role words neutralized after we found they
leaked the arm (42 occurrences in Arm~A against 3 in Arm~B). Three things
follow (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}, last column). \textbf{Arm~A is genuinely
differentiated} ($0.000$ and $0.056$ judged to have no division of labor), so
the soft-role baseline is not a straw man. \textbf{Both actors lose that
differentiation in Arm~B}, at $0.944$ and $0.958$. And the byte metric has
\textbf{precision $1.000$} (0 false positives in 36 firings) and
\textbf{recall $0.343$}: what it flags is genuine, and it misses two-thirds
of what a judge sees. The adjudication used a frontier language model, not
human annotators; \S\ref{sec:limitations} states what that establishes.

\paragraph{The measures survive the obvious attacks.} Deterministic decoding
cannot produce the duplication: \textbf{0 of 893} duplicated first passes
arose from identical prompts, and the endpoint is measurably nondeterministic
at temperature $0$. Normalization \emph{raises} the all-message rate from
$0.842$ to $0.927$, so the exact measure undercounts there too. Retry
convergence is excluded by construction. And it is not an easy-problem
artifact: functional collapse reaches $0.636$ (gpt-oss) and $0.921$
(gemma-4) on the hardest stratum.

\subsection{A logging defect bounds what the duplication rate can mean}
\label{subsec:logging}

We report rather than quietly repair a defect that constrains the duplication
measure. Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content} and fell back to
\texttt{reasoning} only when content was \emph{empty}. gpt-oss-120b often
returns a short content string alongside a long hidden reasoning trace:
verified live, one request returned 17 characters of content and 546 of
reasoning, which our logger would record as the 17 characters while the flag
meant to document the substitution stayed false.

Flagging messages whose visible characters per second of latency falls far
below the corpus norm, \textbf{$84.6\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B messages are
affected and $97.4\%$ of its first passes contain at least one}; the
documented flag reports $5.7\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are essentially
unaffected ($0.7$--$5.0\%$), so the defect sits in exactly the cell where
byte duplication fires. Restricting to first passes whose three messages all
clear a visibility threshold yields no stable estimate, because the clean
subsample shrinks with it: $0.630$ at $n=235$ ($\geq5$ ch/s), $0.284$ at
$n=88$, $0.176$ at $n=34$, $0.083$ at $n=12$ ($\geq50$ ch/s).

Three conclusions follow and no fourth. \textbf{No point estimate of
gpt-oss's true duplication rate is recoverable}; $0.691$ is reported
throughout as \emph{measured output duplication} with this sensitivity
attached. \textbf{Byte-identical outputs may not be glossed as ``the agents
did the same work''} --- with the reasoning discarded we can say only that
they emitted the same output. And the paper's claims are unaffected: accuracy
is graded from a final answer that was always present, and the functional and
adjudicated measures read answer assertions, not derivations. The logger now
captures reasoning unconditionally; the campaign was not re-run
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

The organizational column is nearly flat and the accuracy column is not.
Every actor arrives in Arm~B with the division of labor gone ($0.845$ to
$0.975$) and their accuracy responses run from a significant $-0.0301$ to an
indistinguishable $+0.0238$. \textbf{How much organization an intervention
destroys does not predict whether destroying it costs anything.} With the
amount lost held roughly fixed across three actors, the difference in cost
has to come from somewhere other than the visible organization, and
\S\ref{subsec:mechanism} locates it.

The Llama-3.3 row corroborates the collapse rate only: its accuracy on this
benchmark is $0.22$--$0.35$ and its interval spans zero at $n=84$, so we draw
no accuracy conclusion from it.

\paragraph{One conflation we explicitly avoid.} On the subset of problems
where gpt-oss goes from differentiated to byte-duplicated, B$-$A is $+0.0146$
$[-0.0011,+0.0303]$; its whole-arm B$-$A is $+0.0070$ $[-0.0108,+0.0255]$.
Both are indistinguishable from zero, and we write ``no detectable loss''
rather than ``a gain'' for either. Both signs were also stable as the campaign
grew, checked cumulatively in problem-completion order at 25/50/75/100\% of
the run.

\paragraph{It is not a difficulty artifact.} Stratifying the
differentiated~$\rightarrow$~duplicated subset ($n=892$) by pre-treatment
difficulty (historical one-shot baseline accuracy on the same problem),
B$-$A is non-negative in every band: HARD ($n=152$) $+0.0329$
$[-0.0395,+0.1053]$, MED ($n=154$) $+0.0455$ $[+0.0000,+0.0974]$, EASY
($n=586$) $+0.0017$ $[-0.0034,+0.0085]$. Functional collapse is high in every
band, so the phenomenon is not confined to problems where roles were
unnecessary anyway.

\subsection{Collapse is not ``the system was always a single agent''}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

The strongest objection to \S\ref{subsec:nodegradation} is that a setup where
collapse is free never required multi-agent structure~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}.
We test it against the budget-matched single-agent protocol on the same
problems (Table~\ref{tab:transition}, lower panel).

A collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own single agent by 2.5 points, interval
excluding zero. Three agents emitting the same answer under a shared
evaluator loop is therefore \emph{not} functionally one agent: the redundant
computation buys something real, though nothing attributable to
\emph{differentiated} roles. The comparison is conservative on the axis this
project has found decisive --- the single agent receives \emph{more} oracle
feedback (1.66--1.69 evaluator calls against Arm~A's 1.10--1.34) and roughly
half the actor calls. The same panel carries a fact we report rather than
bury: gemma-4's team is \emph{worse} than its own single agent in both arms,
so for that actor the organization is actively costly at this budget.

\subsection{Architectural enforcement restores the organization}
\label{subsec:enforce}

The manipulation is checked three ways and passes all three.
Answer-authorship compliance reaches exactly $1.000$ in every Arm~C and
Arm~C$'$ cell, against $0.982$ (gemma-4) and $0.604$ (gpt-oss) under soft
roles; independently measured, the Planner emits an answer in $0.000$ of
Arm~C trajectories for all three actors; and the constraint visibly bound,
with 651 and 921 role-boundary rejections per Arm~C cell ($0.50$/$0.72$ per
trajectory), formatting coercions logged separately and excluded. Exact
duplication falls to $0.000$ (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). Enforcement holds
the organization apart --- though, as \S\ref{subsec:structnotcog} shows, only
the part concerning who may speak.

\subsection{But it does not restore performance: the pre-registered primary
test is null}
\label{subsec:p4null}

\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy, and
the pre-registered primary test of this paper's original thesis --- is
null: $-0.0077$ $[-0.0318,+0.0178]$.} P3 is null in both actors as well:
C$-$A is $-0.0147$ $[-0.0324,+0.0031]$ for gemma-4 and $-0.0062$
$[-0.0240,+0.0116]$ for gpt-oss. This is pre-declared Pattern~E, whose
committed interpretation reads: \emph{the central causal theory fails; do not
force it}. We report it as a failure of that theory, not as a supporting
detail of a different one.

Two candidate explanations are ruled out rather than assumed away. Arm~C$'$
restores shared history at fixed enforcement: under the pre-registered
ungraded-as-incorrect rule C$'-$C is $+0.0463$ $[+0.0123,+0.0802]$ for
gemma-4 and $+0.0000$ $[-0.0402,+0.0372]$ for gpt-oss, while restricted to
problems graded in both arms it is $+0.0204$ $[-0.0034,+0.0442]$ and
$-0.0072$ $[-0.0361,+0.0217]$. \textbf{The two conventions disagree for
gemma-4}; we report the pre-registered one as primary, and the difference is
attrition rather than context, since Arm~C$'$ leaves fewer trajectories
ungraded than Arm~C. Either way C$'$ remains at or below Arm~A. Attrition is
Arm-C-specific ($7.3\%$/$5.9\%$ hard failures against $0.000$ in A and B);
dropping failures instead of applying the pre-registered rule would make
Arm~C look \emph{better} ($0.897$/$0.890$), which is why the rule was fixed
in advance and why we report both.

\paragraph{What the two nulls together mean.} Enforcement restores the
organizational measure completely and the performance not at all. Combined
with \S\ref{subsec:nodegradation}, organization can be destroyed without cost
in one actor and restored without benefit in both. Whatever the diagram is
explaining, it is not the accuracy.

\subsection{Structural boundaries are not cognitive specialization}
\label{subsec:structnotcog}

The pre-registered manipulation check was that Arm~C would drive \Pone{} ---
the Planner abstaining from \emph{computation} --- to ${\approx}1.0$.
\textbf{That objective failed}: the check named a clause the intervention
does not enforce, since the schema forbids emitting an \emph{answer} and
nothing stops a Planner writing $=1/27$. We re-designated the manipulation
check to answer authorship and report the \Pone{} outcome as a finding, in
that order.

The finding is that computation does not move. Under enforcement the
Planner's evaluated-equation density is $2.80$ and $3.44$ per 1{,}000
characters against $2.60$ and $2.07$ in Arm~A --- unchanged or \emph{higher}.
Density is the right instrument because the binary rate is anti-correlated
with message length and Arm~C messages are shorter; the same confound makes
Arm~B/gpt-oss score a spurious $0.726$ while emitting bare answers with no
work shown. \textbf{You can structurally stop an agent from answering without
making it stop doing the work.}

\subsection{Mechanism: roles are load-bearing for recovery, not for solving}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

If both actors lose the same organization, the difference in cost has to come
from somewhere else. Decomposing each actor's B$-$A into first-submission
success and recovery after a failed submission, paired on the same problems,
locates it. Removing roles costs gemma-4 essentially nothing on problems it
solves outright --- its first-submission rate moves $0.807 \rightarrow 0.806$
($n=1{,}295$). What changes is what happens after a wrong first answer, where
its recovery falls $0.200 \rightarrow 0.048$. gpt-oss also loses recovery
($0.346 \rightarrow 0.241$) but gains first-pass ($0.724 \rightarrow 0.771$,
$n=1{,}288$), and the two roughly cancel --- which is the shape of its null.

What differs is whether the team can disagree with itself. In Arm~B, on first
passes where all three agents state an answer, gemma-4 is unanimous in
$0.961$ of trajectories against gpt-oss's $0.796$, so gpt-oss retains
disagreement five times more often ($0.204$ versus $0.039$). Unanimity is
costly when it is wrong: among trajectories that are unanimous and fail the
first submission, gemma-4 goes on to reach the right answer $0.062$ of the
time and changes its answer at all only $0.056$ of the time, against gpt-oss's
$0.165$ and $0.353$.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Role differentiation is load-bearing for error recovery, not for
first-pass solving --- and only for the actor that cannot otherwise escape a
wrong consensus.}
\end{quote}

This also explains \S\ref{subsec:p4null}. Enforcing answer authorship restores
who may speak; it does not restore whether the agents \emph{differ}. Arm~C
gives back the boundary and not the disagreement, and disagreement is what the
recovering actor needed. Three caveats bound the claim. Recovery is
conditioned on needing a retry, which is post-treatment, so this is a
decomposition and not a causal mediation. The conditioning set is also not
matched across arms: in Arm~A, $59\%$ (gemma-4) and $22\%$ (gpt-oss) of failed
first submissions were never retried before the round budget ran out, against
$0\%$ and $2\%$ in Arm~B, so the Arm~A recovery denominators are a selected
subset and the level of the A-side rates should not be read as an unconditional
recovery probability. And the retry loop is oracle-gated, so ``recovery''
means recovery \emph{with} an oracle signal --- a property of this protocol
family that bounds the mechanism's external validity.
