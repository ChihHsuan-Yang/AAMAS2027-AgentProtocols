\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that trajectories never
producing a graded answer count as incorrect; significance is exact McNemar
on discordant pairs. Holm correction across the two testable pre-registered
families changes no verdict (P3, gemma-4 Holm $p=0.0120$; P4, all retained).
\textbf{The A-versus-B contrast supplying this paper's headline was
pre-registered as secondary and exploratory}, and every conditional analysis
below conditions on a post-treatment variable: descriptive, never causal.

\subsection{R1. Removing role instructions destroys the division of labor}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Arm~B deletes every role instruction and changes nothing else. \textbf{This
claim rests on the mechanical measures, which involve no judge.} We ask
whether \emph{every} agent independently asserts its own final answer on the
first pass, so the measure is not length-confounded
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}); it rises from $0.000$--$0.120$ to
$0.846$--$0.976$. \textbf{Decomposed by role, the planner supplies almost
all of the change}: the executor is at ceiling in both arms
($0.907$--$1.000$); the reviewer, already high, moves
$0.781$/$0.492$/$0.823$ to $0.998$/$0.965$/$0.961$ (gemma-4 / Llama-3.3 /
gpt-oss); the planner moves $0.000$/$0.000$/$0.183$ to
$0.979$/$0.889$/$0.898$ --- a statement about the \emph{change}, not the
level.

\paragraph{Exact duplication is sound and far too narrow.} Byte-identical
text between two agents fires on $0.680$ of gpt-oss Arm~B first passes and
essentially never elsewhere ($\leq 0.008$) --- a property of the instrument
(recall $0.262$), not the actors, and \textbf{never used to compare how much
organization two actors lost}.

\paragraph{A second opinion, and the leak that stops it being blind.} We put
171 trajectories ($42$/$56$/$44$/$29$ across Arms~A/B/C/C$'$) to a frontier
model against a rubric frozen before the first judgement, with actor, arm,
correctness and the automated label withheld and role words neutralized. It
finds the division of labor intact in $1.000$ of Arm~A and absent in
$0.857$--$1.000$ of Arm~B, stable at $0.953$ across repetitions. This is
\textbf{model adjudication, not human annotation}, and not ground truth.

\textbf{It is also outcome-blind but \emph{not} condition-blind.}
Neutralization renamed the role words without equalizing their
\emph{presence}: a neutralized \texttt{Agent-P/E/R} token appears in all
$42$ Arm~A items and $3$ of $56$ Arm~B items (Fisher exact
$p=1.5\times10^{-24}$), so a judge noticing only whether a transcript labels
its speakers could separate the arms without reading content; output format
separates Arm~C further. Normalizing to a common surface would destroy the
differences being judged, so we disclose rather than re-run. \textbf{We
therefore do not call the arm-contrastive adjudication blinded}, and the
collapse claim does not rest on it; judge stability and the byte metric's
precision/recall are within-item, so unaffected.

\paragraph{Two of three actors visibly duplicate; the third is
uninstrumented.} gemma-4's and Llama-3.3's Arm~B planners write full
independent solutions (median $1{,}876$ and $1{,}892$ characters) before
boxing an answer. Numeric-token overlap confirms it, but only against the
right null: pairing planners with executors from \emph{different} problems
shows the baseline is itself arm-dependent, so the raw A$-$B difference is
invalid. Against each arm's own null the excess is $+0.449$/$+0.432$ in
Arm~B against $+0.159$/$+0.145$ in Arm~A --- a replicated shift of $+0.290$
and $+0.287$. \textbf{gpt-oss cannot be read at all}: its Arm~B planner
emits a median $37$ visible characters in $26.7$ seconds ($2.85$ per second
against $195$ in its own Arm~A), with \texttt{used\_reasoning\_fallback}
false in all $2{,}302$ sub-100-character messages --- the logging defect
below.

\subsection{A logging defect, and its second consequence}
\label{subsec:logging}

Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content}, falling back to
\texttt{reasoning} only when content was empty; gpt-oss-120b often returns a
short content string alongside a long hidden trace, so the derivation was
discarded while the flag documenting the substitution stayed false. Below
$10$ visible characters per second of latency, \textbf{$75.7\%$ of gpt-oss
Arm~B role messages are affected and $93.4\%$ of its first passes contain
one}; the flag reports $5.8\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are unaffected
($0.1$--$0.5\%$).

Two consequences follow and no third. \textbf{No point estimate of gpt-oss's
duplication rate is recoverable}, and \textbf{the defect bounds
collapse-\emph{mode} interpretation}: calling that 37-character planner a
behavioral mode would attribute our defect to the model, and it is why byte
duplication fires only here. \textbf{It does not manufacture the collapse},
the one place it could reach a headline measure: restricted to first passes
with \emph{no} affected message, Arm~B collapse is $0.821$ ($n=106$) against
$0.846$ overall and Arm~A $0.118$ against $0.120$ --- the gap is intact on
clean messages. Accuracy is graded from an always-present final answer, and
the functional measure reads answer assertions, not derivations. The
campaign was not re-run (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{R2. First-pass accuracy does not suffer --- our control
condition}
\label{subsec:firstpass}

\emph{Not a finding} but the control condition it is: the ablation removed
the organization without crippling the
solver~\cite{dylan2026equal,orogat2026architectural}. On matched problems
first-pass B$-$A is \emph{identical} for gemma-4 ($+0.0000$, CI
$[-0.0156,+0.0150]$) and flat for Llama-3.3 ($+0.0077$,
$[-0.0283,+0.0437]$), while it \emph{improves} for gpt-oss ($+0.0503$,
$[+0.0339,+0.0666]$). Whatever roles buy, they do not buy the first
answer.

\subsection{R3. Recovery after a wrong first answer collapses}
\label{subsec:recovery}

Conditional on the first submission being graded wrong, the role-free team
recovers less often (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}): gemma-4 falls $0.222
\rightarrow 0.051$ ($-0.1709$ $[-0.2247,-0.1171]$), gpt-oss $0.324
\rightarrow 0.247$ ($-0.0766$ $[-0.1361,-0.0153]$). \textbf{Only gemma-4's
survives pairing}: on problems first-pass-wrong under \emph{both} arms it
recovers on $37$ problems Arm~B misses against $3$ the other way (exact
McNemar $p=2.0\times10^{-8}$), where gpt-oss's paired contrast gives
$p=0.208$ and is reported as directional only.

\textbf{For gemma-4 this is not a post-treatment conditional.} Its
first-pass rates are equal to four decimals ($0.8025$ in both arms), so in
$\Delta_{\text{final}} = \Delta_{\text{fp}}(1-\text{rec}_A) +
(1-\text{fp}_B)\,\Delta_{\text{rec}}$ the first term vanishes and the
recovery contrast is an exact reparameterization of the intention-to-treat
effect: $-0.1709 \times (1-0.8025) = -0.0338$, the observed B$-$A
final-accuracy difference, residual zero --- a quantity algebraically equal
to the randomized contrast is not a post-treatment artifact.
\textbf{This does not extend to gpt-oss}, whose first-pass rates differ by
$+0.050$.

\textbf{Llama-3.3 does not replicate, and it is a genuine null rather than a
floor.} Its rates are $0.014$ and $0.015$ --- four recoveries out of $276$
Arm~A and four of $268$ Arm~B first-pass-wrong trajectories. Its recovery
channel exists and is movable --- enforcement raises it to $0.139$, paired
$0.009$ versus $0.096$, $p=3.8\times10^{-6}$ (\S\ref{subsec:enforce}) --- so
what is absent is an effect of the \emph{role-instruction} manipulation, not
the capacity to repair. The test has ${\approx}80\%$ power against an excess
of $0.04$--$0.05$ where gemma-4's effect is $0.171$: informative against a
gemma-sized effect, uninformative below it.

\paragraph{The accuracy contrasts against grader noise.} Repeated identical
evaluator prompts at temperature $0$ disagree on $20$ of $2{,}790$ distinct
graded strings, a group-level rate of $0.0072$ ($0.0017$--$0.0072$ across
estimators), keyed on the exact candidate bytes. \textbf{We do not divide an
effect size by a disagreement rate} --- different quantities, and the noise
largely cancels in a paired contrast --- but propagate it into the interval,
flipping each graded outcome independently at the per-call rate $0.0062$
inside the bootstrap. gemma-4's B$-$A survives ($[-0.0494,-0.0175]$);
\textbf{gpt-oss's ($[-0.0013,+0.0331]$) and Llama-3.3's
($[-0.0350,+0.0400]$) do not}, so for those actors the contrast is not
resolvable above grader noise rather than absent.

\subsection{R3b. The dissociation replicates in a second domain}
\label{subsec:xdomain}

Both halves reappear on \textbf{SciBench} --- the complete
$574$-problem matched slice of free-response college science; Arms~A and B,
two actors, $2{,}296$ trajectories, frozen separately. Arm~B is
byte-identical to its Omni-MATH form and Arm~A keeps the same structural
contract with domain wording adapted, so no clause-specific metric crosses domains. For \textbf{gemma-4} first-pass accuracy is
$0.794$ in both arms and paired recovery falls $0.212 \rightarrow 0.020$
($n=99$, $21$ vs $2$ discordant, $p=6.6\times10^{-5}$), not distinguishable
in size from Omni-MATH. \textbf{gpt-oss}'s cell is underpowered (power
$0.06$--$0.45$ at $n=96$) and resolves nothing; Llama-3.3 was not run.
\textbf{The dissociation replicates across two domains for one actor}, not
across actors.

\subsection{R4. The mechanism is not premature stopping}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

The standing explanation is premature consensus --- the team stops
early. \textbf{We test and reject it.} Every arm runs the same six cycles
and Arm~B draws \emph{more} oracle feedback (\S\ref{sec:method}); realized
spend is near-identical ($5.52$ vs $5.83$, gemma-4, first-pass-wrong). What
differs is use: Arm~B converts $5.80$ of $5.83$ cycles into submissions
while Arm~A submits $1.54$ times and spends $3.98$ revising. \textbf{No
trajectory was cut short.} What stops is refusal --- in $98.1\%$ of gemma-4's
Arm~B first-pass-wrong trajectories the Reviewer never declines, against
$5.1\%$ in Arm~A ($61.6\%$ vs $4.3\%$ for gpt-oss) --- and the consequence is
a frozen answer, unchanged in $0.845$ of those against $0.288$ of Arm~A's. This is Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and the
point is the \textbf{inversion}: multi-agency is the prescribed cure that
role-free multi-agency does not deliver.

\paragraph{A competing driver we do not dismiss.} Accumulated context is a
real cause of recovery failure~\cite{yang2026contamination}: within Arm~A
alone gpt-oss recovery falls $0.923 \to 0.070$ across pooled context
quartiles. It does not \emph{account for} the arm gap, which survives
context matching in the lower quartiles ($0.662$ vs $0.151$, gemma-4;
$0.656$ vs $0.293$, gpt-oss) --- \textbf{but in gpt-oss's top quartile the
ordering reverses} ($0.070$ vs $0.189$). \textbf{Directional only}:
quartiles are cut on the pooled A$+$B distribution and context is nearly a
function of arm, so the strata are partly arm indicators (Q1 holds $198$
Arm~B trajectories against $13$ Arm~A).

\subsection{R5. Enforcement restores organization and recovery, not
accuracy}
\label{subsec:enforce}

The manipulation check passes: the planner authors an answer in
$0.000$ of Arm~C trajectories in all three actors against
$0.986$/$0.916$/$0.958$ under generic prompts, with $838$/$1{,}183$/$395$
role-boundary rejections per cell (formatting coercions excluded by a
definition fixed in advance). Arm~C also \textbf{un-freezes the answer}:
$0.119$/$0.092$/$0.072$ against Arm~B's $0.845$/$0.208$/$0.806$, and below
Arm~A except for gpt-oss ($0.052$).

\textbf{It restores recovery for Llama-3.3; for the other two the direction
is consistent but not resolvable against attrition.} Recovery is computed on
every trajectory with a recorded incorrect first verdict, including the $150$
Arm~C trajectories that received one then died in regeneration --- all
necessarily first-pass wrong, since a passing verdict ends the loop, and
scored $0$ by the preregistration. On that denominator Arm~C recovers at
$0.314$/$0.395$/$0.139$ against Arm~A's $0.222$/$0.324$/$0.014$, but the
paired contrast survives only for Llama-3.3 ($0.009$ vs $0.096$,
$p=3.8\times10^{-6}$); gemma-4 ($p=0.298$) and gpt-oss ($p=0.105$) do not.
Llama's denominator is constructed, not asserted: $400$
attempted, $349$ judged, $251$ wrong on that verdict ($17$ later dying in
regeneration, scored $0$), $35$ recovered. Conditioning on successful
grading would manufacture significance out of the same one-sided attrition
we reject for accuracy below, and flatters Arm~C twice over --- raising this
cell to $0.150$ and lifting Arm~C's first-pass column from
$0.779$/$0.761$/$0.281$ to $0.822$/$0.788$/$0.295$.

\textbf{These Arm~C rates do not compose into Table~\ref{tab:armc}'s
C$-$A, and the attrition is why.} The $(1-\text{fp})$ identity of
\S\ref{subsec:recovery} is exact only when both columns share a denominator:
here first pass and recovery are computed over trajectories receiving an
evaluator verdict, final accuracy over all $1{,}600$/$1{,}600$/$400$ with
the never-graded scored $0$. Composing them re-scores those as graded and
returns a \emph{positive} value for all three actors against the table's one
significant negative and two nulls. \textbf{The A-versus-B composition is
valid because Arms~A and B have essentially no attrition}
($0.0$--$2.5\%$).

\textbf{It does not restore final accuracy, and the scoring rule decides the
answer} (Table~\ref{tab:armc}). Under the pre-registered rule C$-$A is
$-0.0225$ / $-0.0050$ / $+0.0175$: one significant negative, two nulls.
Dropping ungraded trajectories gives $+0.0190$ / $+0.0361$ / $+0.0453$ ---
all positive, all significant, ordered by actor weakness, exactly the
published capability-equalizer
direction~\cite{canedo2026architecture}. \textbf{That reading is
survivorship}: the preregistration fixed in advance that such trajectories
count incorrect, and \textbf{the benefit appears only after conditioning on
successful parsing.}

Enforcement does not change what the planner \emph{computes}:
evaluated-equation density is $3.11$/$4.78$ per $1{,}000$ characters in

\subsection{R6. The pre-registered primary test is null}
\label{subsec:p4null}

\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy, the
pre-registered primary test of this paper's original thesis --- is null in
all three pairwise difference-in-differences: $+0.0175$
$[-0.0050,+0.0412]$, $+0.0400$ $[+0.0000,+0.0794]$, $+0.0225$
$[-0.0188,+0.0631]$}, and we report it as the failure of the theory it was
written to test. \textbf{Only one of the three is an informative null.} The
largest per-actor C$-$A main effect is $|{-}0.0225|$, so a true interaction
is plausibly ${\leq}0.040$; only the gpt-oss$-$gemma-4 contrast can see that
(MDE $0.033$ at $80\%$ power, $n=1{,}600$). The two Llama contrasts (MDE
$0.069$, $0.075$ at $n=400$) \textbf{cannot detect an interaction of any
plausible size} and are uninformative rather than evidence of absence.

Our pre-declared not-submit condition was a null P4 \emph{and} a
main effect matching the published capability-equalizer direction; the
pre-registered C$-$A effect matches no monotone direction, so the condition
is met only under the as-graded scoring \S\ref{subsec:enforce} shows to be
survivorship. Arm~C$'$ restores shared history at fixed enforcement and does
not rescue the result (C$'-$C $+0.0450$ and $-0.0125$ pre-registered) ---
attrition, not context.

\subsection{Collapse is not ``the system was always a single agent''}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

We test the standing objection that a setup where collapse is
free never required multi-agent structure~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}
against a budget-matched single-agent protocol on the same problems
(Table~\ref{tab:main}, lower panel), \textbf{from a previously released
trace corpus rather than our own campaign} (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). It
refuses both easy readings. A collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own single
agent ($+0.0294$), so three agents emitting the same answer is not
functionally one agent; \textbf{but gemma-4's team is significantly worse
than its own in both arms} ($-0.0225$, $-0.0563$) --- and gemma-4 carries
our recovery result, a tension we take into \S\ref{sec:conclusion} rather
than resolve. \emph{Neither} ``multi-agent systems are just single agents''
\emph{nor} ``more agents help'' survives our own data. \textbf{The two
single-agent arms were built from different source commits}, so each
direction is a sound guard while \emph{the gap between actors is not}; the
comparison is conservative on the decisive axis, the single agent drawing
\emph{more} oracle feedback ($1.66$--$1.69$ against Arm~A's $1.11$--$1.35$)
and half the actor calls.
