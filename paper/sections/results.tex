\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem; trajectories never producing a graded answer count as
incorrect (the first-pass contrast of \S\ref{subsec:collapse} excepted),
and significance is exact McNemar on discordant pairs, with $p$ to two
significant figures.

\subsection{R1. Removing roles changes the organization, not the first
answer}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Besides deleting the role clauses, the role-free prompt asks every agent
for its current best answer; agents, order, budget and evaluator are
unchanged. We ask whether \emph{every} agent asserts its own final answer
on the first pass, so the measure is not length-confounded
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}): it rises from $0.000$--$0.120$ to
$0.846$--$0.976$. \textbf{Decomposed by role, the planner supplies most of
the change}: the executor answers in both conditions and the reviewer,
already high for two actors, rises in all three, but the planner moves
$0.000$/$0.183$/$0.000$ to $0.979$/$0.898$/$0.889$ (gemma-4 / gpt-oss /
Llama-3.3). Since the footer asks for an answer, this
measures what a Planner does once its \textsc{must not} is gone, not an
emergent tendency --- a claim about the \emph{change}, not the level.

\paragraph{Content duplication and a second opinion.} gemma-4's and
Llama-3.3's role-free planners write full independent solutions
(${\approx}1{,}880$ characters, median) before boxing an answer, and
numeric-token overlap with the executor exceeds its per-condition null by
$+0.449$ and $+0.432$ ($+0.159$ and $+0.145$ with roles). A frontier model judging $171$ trajectories against
a frozen rubric agrees (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}, last column; repeat
agreement $0.953$), but it is outcome-blind, not condition-blind: role words were
renamed rather than equalized, and a renamed token appears in all $42$
role-structured items and $3$ of $56$ role-free ones (Fisher
$p=1.5\times10^{-24}$). The collapse claim does not rest on it.

\paragraph{First-pass accuracy does not follow.} On problems graded under
both conditions ($n=1{,}600$ / $1{,}592$ / $389$) first-pass accuracy
is \emph{identical} for gemma-4 ($+0.0000$, $[-0.0156,+0.0150]$) and flat
for Llama-3.3 ($+0.0077$), while it \emph{improves} for gpt-oss ($+0.0503$,
$[+0.0339,+0.0666]$; Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}A). Whatever roles buy,
they do not buy the first answer.

\paragraph{A logging defect.}
\label{subsec:logging}
Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content}, falling back to
\texttt{reasoning} only when content was empty. gpt-oss-120b often returns
a short content string beside a long hidden trace, so its derivations are
missing from the record ($75.7\%$ of its role-free messages, $93.4\%$ of
its first passes; the flag meant to report this reports $5.8\%$). Agents
pass content only, by design and in every condition, so the defect is in
the record: no point estimate of gpt-oss's duplication is recoverable. It does not
manufacture the collapse: on first passes with no affected message,
role-free collapse is $0.821$ ($n=106$) and role-structured $0.118$.

\subsection{R2. Given the same feedback, the role-free team recovers more}
\label{subsec:recovery}



\paragraph{What a cycle is.} The pipeline runs a fixed grid of six
deliberation cycles --- three outer rounds of two inner rounds --- each one
pass of Planner~$\rightarrow$~Executor~$\rightarrow$~Reviewer. The Reviewer
decides whether a cycle ends in a \emph{submission} or a \emph{revision}.
On approval the current answer goes to the evaluator, which returns PASS
--- the trajectory ends --- or FAIL with a repair hint; without approval
nothing is submitted and the next cycle carries the Reviewer's critique
forward. A trajectory that never submits is graded once when the budget
runs out.

\input{tables/main_results}

\paragraph{Recovery and final accuracy.} The role-structured team receives
the evaluator's feedback, so the matched comparison is with the role-free
team that receives it too (Table~\ref{tab:main}). \textbf{That team
recovers more, not less}: $0.609$ against $0.222$ (marginal,
Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}B), and on the $236$ problems both first
answer wrongly, $89$ are recovered only without roles and $4$ only with them
($p=6.2\times10^{-22}$). Both reach the same first-pass rate and a correct
first answer is never lost, so this becomes final accuracy: $0.921$ against
$0.846$, with $137$ problems correct only without roles and $17$ only with
them ($p=1.7\times10^{-24}$, conditioned on nothing). The recoveries are
revisions, not re-gradings: in $192$ of the $193$ the passing answer
differs from the one first rejected ($541$ of $547$ across the five cells
with feedback matched), and repeated identical evaluator prompts disagree
in only $0.0072$ of cases.

\paragraph{Reproducibility, drift and leakage.} The two gemma-4
feedback-matched cells were run twice, independently and with identical
code. The second run reproduces the first (Omni-MATH recovery $0.586$
against $0.609$, final accuracy $0.922$ against $0.921$; SciBench recovery
$0.435$ against $0.419$, its paired test at $p=0.052$ against $0.013$); we
report the first throughout (supplement). A re-run of the role-structured
condition shows no detectable drift in final accuracy (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). Repair hints
contain the gold answer verbatim in at most $2.3\%$ of failing verdicts in
any cell; for gemma-4 on Omni-MATH, dropping every trajectory that saw one
leaves $0.609$ and $0.222$ at $0.599$ and $0.212$. We did not audit partial
leakage.

\paragraph{Scope across actors.} Both other actors repeat the ordering in
recovery and in final accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:main}), and in both the
feedback leaves first-pass accuracy where it was ($0.765$ and $0.317$,
against $0.762$ and $0.315$ with it withheld). Llama-3.3's role-structured
team rarely submits at all, and there enforcement recovers about as much
as the role-free team with feedback ($0.139$ against $0.147$ marginally;
paired $9$ against $17$ of $219$, $p=0.17$). Against the feedback-withheld
baseline the role-structured advantage is clean only for gemma-4: it is
directional for gpt-oss ($0.324$ against $0.247$ marginally; paired $52$
against $39$ of $327$, $p=0.21$) and absent for Llama-3.3 ($0.014$ against
$0.015$). gpt-oss recovers without feedback, consistent with its answer
moving from cycle to cycle anyway (one distinct answer in $20.8\%$ of its failed
trajectories, against $84.5\%$ for gemma-4).

\subsection{R3. Matched on verdicts, recovery is equivalent; roles gate
submission}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

\paragraph{The feedback-withheld baseline.} Without the feedback, the
role-free team turns nearly its whole budget into submissions: across
gemma-4's first-pass-wrong trajectories it submits in $5.80$ of $5.83$
cycles (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}C), its third agent never declines in
$98.1\%$ of them, and in $84.5\%$ the entire transcript holds exactly one
distinct answer. Nothing in its prompts carries the feedback: each FAIL is
followed by another cycle over the same history. Given the verdict and
hint, the identical prompts produce a different team: the answer moves (one
distinct answer in $6.6\%$ of failed trajectories) and recovery rises from
$16/316=0.051$ to $193/317=0.609$; of the $265$ problems both miss first,
$139$ are recovered only with the feedback and $2$ only without
($p=7.2\times10^{-39}$). First-pass accuracy is $0.80$ in both conditions.
Of the $16$ recoveries without feedback, $7$ are the same answer graded
again. \textbf{Revision needs the feedback, not the roles.}

\paragraph{Matched on verdicts, the two teams recover alike.} The
role-free team with feedback also receives more of it: each submission
returns a verdict and, on a FAIL, a hint written with the gold answer in
view. To compare the two teams at equal exposure (a post-hoc analysis), we take the problems both
first answer wrongly and cut each role-free trajectory off after as many
verdicts as the role-structured team received on the same problem. The
difference disappears in every cell (Table~\ref{tab:main}): $0.165$
against $0.153$ for gemma-4 ($p=0.69$), $0.229$ against $0.216$ for gpt-oss
($p=0.72$), and $0.230$ against $0.190$ and $0.215$ against $0.247$ on
SciBench ($p\geq0.48$); Llama-3.3's role-structured team submits so rarely
that its cell has five events. Pooled and stratified by cell, the
difference is $+0.009$ in favor of roles ($95\%$ paired bootstrap CI
$-0.014$ to $+0.033$; $90\%$ CI inside $\pm0.05$, so the two are equivalent
at that margin), and cutting both trajectories at the smaller of their two
verdict counts changes no conclusion. The comparison crosses the freeze, so
we repeat it against the role-structured re-run made after it: on the $65$
problems both first answer wrongly, recovery is $3$ against $22$ in favor
of the role-free team unmatched ($p=1.6\times10^{-4}$) and $4$ against $1$
verdict-matched ($p=0.38$). Verdict for verdict, a team with roles and one
without make equivalent use of the feedback; the role-free team recovers more
because it asks for more verdicts.

\paragraph{What roles do instead: a gate on submission.} The reason is the
role-structured Reviewer. Across gemma-4's failed trajectories that team
submits $1.27$ times in $5.52$ cycles, against $3.20$ in $3.96$ without
roles (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}C). Of its declining cycles, $53\%$ come
before the first submission, when no feedback exists, and $84$ of its $316$
failed trajectories never submit within the budget: they are graded once at
the end and never see the feedback. One clause forbids approval in a cycle
that receives a FAIL, but it covers only $19\%$ of the declines; in the
later cycles where approval is allowed, the Reviewer still declines $75\%$
of the time, against $4\%$ for the role-free team's third agent. On
Omni-MATH the clause covers $37\%$ of gpt-oss's declines and $4\%$ of
Llama-3.3's, whose role-structured team submits $0.32$ times per failed
trajectory and never submits in $205$ of $276$; on SciBench it covers $40\%$
and $60\%$ (supplement). The gate looks like precision --- recoveries per
verdict after the first are higher with roles ($0.41$ against $0.28$ for
gemma-4) --- but that compares different trajectories; on the same problem
and the same number of verdicts, the two teams are equivalent.

\paragraph{Removing the gate alone.} The direct test keeps the
role-structured prompts byte for byte but submits the team's answer in every
cycle, whatever the Reviewer decides (exploratory, one run: gemma-4 on the
$400$ Omni-MATH problems of the post-freeze re-run; supplement). First-pass
accuracy is unchanged ($0.768$ against $0.773$), the team draws $4.20$ verdicts per failed trajectory against $1.44$,
and recovery rises from $0.198$ to $0.613$, as high as the role-free team's
$0.549$ on the same problems. On problems both first answer wrongly, the
forced team recovers $26$ that the gated one misses and $2$ the other way
($p=3.0\times10^{-6}$); against the role-free team the split is $7$ to $7$ ($p=1$).
With the role contract intact, removing the gate removed the gap: against a
ground-truth evaluator and six cycles, a withheld submission is a lost
recovery.

\subsection{R4. The same ordering in a second domain}
\label{subsec:xdomain}

SciBench's complete text-only slice of $574$ free-response college
science problems, frozen separately, tests whether the pattern belongs to
the organization or to competition mathematics. The role-free prompt is
byte-identical to its Omni-MATH form; the role-structured one keeps the same
structural contract with domain wording adapted, as does the evaluator. For
both actors the ordering returns (Table~\ref{tab:main}): first pass is
flat, recovery is highest with feedback and no roles ($0.419$ and $0.483$
against $0.254$ and $0.315$ with roles), and final accuracy is higher
without roles ($p=0.0054$ and $1.9\times10^{-4}$). The recovery contrast is
weaker ($p=0.013$ and $0.043$), and as
on Omni-MATH it vanishes when both teams are credited with the same number
of verdicts. One part does not transfer: gemma-4's role-structured answer
does not lock (one distinct answer in $7.6\%$ of failed trajectories,
against $8.5\%$ for the role-free team with feedback), and by the second verdict the two recover
alike ($0.237$ against $0.239$; supplement).

\subsection{R5. Structural enforcement shifts the allocation, and
introduces brittleness}
\label{subsec:enforce}

Enforced and role-structured prompts carry the same advice slot, so this
contrast is matched on feedback. Enforcement replaces the request with a
constraint: the Planner's answer becomes unrepresentable. The manipulation
check passes: the planner asserts a final answer in $0.000$ of enforced
first passes in all three actors against $0.979$/$0.898$/$0.889$ without
roles, with $838$/$1{,}183$/$395$ role-boundary rejections per cell. It does
not change what the planner \emph{computes}, which is why the adjudication
still finds no division in $0.071$--$0.188$ of enforced first passes: an
agent can be structurally stopped from answering without being stopped
from doing the work. Relative to the role-structured team, enforcement
loosens the gate: it submits more often ($2.02$/$2.33$/$1.84$ against
$1.27$/$2.10$/$0.32$ per failed trajectory) and, for gemma-4 and Llama-3.3,
\textbf{unlocks the answer} (one distinct answer in $0.119$ and $0.072$
of failed trajectories against $0.288$ and $0.750$; for gpt-oss $0.092$
against $0.052$).

Recovery follows most clearly for the weakest actor: Llama-3.3's rises
about tenfold, from $0.014$ to $0.139$ ($p=3.8\times10^{-6}$); for gemma-4
($0.314$ against $0.222$, $p=0.30$) and gpt-oss ($0.395$ against $0.324$,
$p=0.11$) the direction is the same but not resolvable against attrition.
Recovery is computed on every trajectory with a recorded incorrect first
verdict, including the $150$ enforced trajectories that received one then
died in regeneration, scored $0$ by the pre-registration; conditioning
instead on successful grading would manufacture significance out of
one-sided attrition.

\textbf{Improved revisability does not become end-to-end accuracy, because
enforcement is brittle.} Under the pre-registered rule the accuracy
difference is $-0.0225$ / $-0.0050$ / $+0.0175$ (gemma-4 / gpt-oss /
Llama-3.3): one significant negative, two nulls. Dropping the
$8.0\%$/$9.9\%$/$17.0\%$ of enforced trajectories that never finish ---
against $0.0$--$0.8\%$ role-structured and $0.0$--$2.5\%$ role-free ---
gives $+0.0190$ / $+0.0361$ / $+0.0453$: all positive, all significant,
ordered by actor weakness, exactly the published capability-equalizer
direction~\cite{canedo2026architecture}. \textbf{That second reading is
survivorship}: the pre-registration fixed in advance that such trajectories
count incorrect. Restoring shared history at fixed enforcement raises
gemma-4's pre-registered accuracy ($+0.0450$, $p=0.0079$; as graded
$+0.0196$, $p=0.14$) but not gpt-oss's ($-0.0125$, $p=0.56$). Both scorings with intervals are in the supplement.
