\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that ungraded
trajectories count as incorrect; P1--P5 carry a Holm correction. The
A-versus-B contrast supplying this paper's headline was pre-registered as
secondary and exploratory, and is labeled \textbf{post-hoc} throughout.

\subsection{Removing role instructions collapses the organization of every
actor}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Arm~B deletes every role instruction and changes nothing else. How one
measures the surviving organization decides the answer, so we report two
measures and say which claim rests on which.

\paragraph{Exact duplication is sound and far too narrow.} The natural
automated measure is whether two or more agents emit byte-identical text,
applied to the first pipeline pass so it is not confounded with trajectory
length. It fires on $0.690$ of gpt-oss Arm~B trajectories and essentially
never on gemma-4 or Llama-3.3 in any arm ($\leq 0.008$) --- a gap that is a
property of the instrument. A blinded adjudication judges gemma-4's Arm~B
trajectories redundant at $0.944$ where this metric reports $0.000$: its
agents paraphrase at length instead of copying, so no hash matches even
though nothing is added. \textbf{Exact duplication is therefore a
conservative lower bound, usable to say that at least this much collapse
occurred and never to compare how much organization two actors lost.}

\paragraph{Functional collapse.} We therefore measure failure of the property
the diagram asserts --- that the agents do different jobs --- by asking
whether \emph{every} agent independently asserts its own final answer
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}).

\textbf{All three actors lose the division of labor at essentially the same
rate.} Arm~B is not ``roles blurred'': $0.90$--$0.98$ of \emph{planners} emit
a final answer, against $0.00$--$0.18$ in Arm~A. Under the byte metric this
looked like a large contrast between actors; under a measure of the property
the diagram asserts, it is absent.

\paragraph{Validation by blinded adjudication.} Because the choice of measure
carries the result, we validated it on 180 sampled trajectories against a
rubric frozen before the first judgement, with actor, arm, correctness and
the automated label withheld, and role words neutralized after we found they
leaked the arm (42 occurrences in Arm~A against 3 in Arm~B). Three things
follow (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}, last column). \textbf{Arm~A is genuinely
differentiated}, so the soft-role baseline is not a straw man; \textbf{both
actors lose that differentiation in Arm~B}; and the byte metric has
\textbf{precision $1.000$} (0 false positives in 36 firings) with
\textbf{recall $0.343$}, so what it flags is genuine and it misses two-thirds
of what a judge sees. The adjudication used a frontier language model, not
human annotators; \S\ref{sec:limitations} states what that establishes.

\paragraph{The measures survive the obvious attacks.} \textbf{0 of 907}
duplicated first passes arose from identical prompts, and the endpoint is
measurably nondeterministic at temperature $0$, so deterministic decoding
cannot produce the duplication; normalization \emph{raises} the all-message
rate from $0.842$ to $0.927$, so the exact measure undercounts there too;
retry convergence is excluded by construction; and functional collapse
reaches $0.64$ (gpt-oss) and $0.92$ (gemma-4) on the hardest stratum, so it
is not an easy-problem artifact.

\subsection{A logging defect bounds what the duplication rate can mean}
\label{subsec:logging}

We report rather than quietly repair a defect that constrains the duplication
measure. Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content} and fell back to
\texttt{reasoning} only when content was \emph{empty}; gpt-oss-120b often
returns a short content string alongside a long hidden reasoning trace (one
verified request returned 17 characters of content and 546 of reasoning), so
the derivation was discarded while the flag documenting the substitution
stayed false. Flagging messages whose visible characters per second of
latency falls far below the corpus norm, \textbf{$84.7\%$ of gpt-oss Arm~B
messages are affected and $97.3\%$ of its first passes contain at least one};
the documented flag reports $5.7\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are essentially
unaffected ($0.7$--$5.0\%$), so the defect sits in exactly the cell where
byte duplication fires. Restricting to first passes whose three messages all
clear a visibility threshold yields no stable estimate, the clean subsample
shrinking with it: $0.614$ at $n=241$ ($\geq5$ ch/s) through $0.083$ at
$n=12$ ($\geq50$ ch/s).

Three conclusions follow and no fourth. \textbf{No point estimate of
gpt-oss's true duplication rate is recoverable}; $0.690$ is reported
throughout as \emph{measured output duplication} with this sensitivity
attached. \textbf{Byte-identical outputs may not be glossed as ``the agents
did the same work''}, only as the same output. And the paper's claims are
unaffected: accuracy is graded from a final answer that was always present,
and the functional and adjudicated measures read answer assertions, not
derivations. The campaign was not re-run (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

Every actor arrives in Arm~B with the division of labor gone ($0.846$ to
$0.975$) while their accuracy responses run from a significant $-0.0296$ to
an indistinguishable $+0.0400$. \textbf{How much organization an intervention
destroys does not predict whether destroying it costs anything.} With the
amount lost held roughly fixed, the difference in cost must come from
somewhere other than the visible organization, and \S\ref{subsec:mechanism}
locates it.

The Llama-3.3 row corroborates the collapse rate only: its accuracy on this
benchmark is $0.25$--$0.31$ and its interval spans zero at $n=125$, so we draw
no accuracy conclusion from it.

\paragraph{One conflation we explicitly avoid.} On the subset where gpt-oss
goes from differentiated to byte-duplicated, B$-$A is $+0.0155$
$[+0.0000,+0.0309]$ against a whole-arm $+0.0076$ $[-0.0107,+0.0259]$: both
indistinguishable from zero, so we write ``no detectable loss'' rather than
``a gain'' for either. Both signs were stable at 25/50/75/100\% of the run.

\paragraph{It is not a difficulty artifact.} Stratifying that subset
($n=892$) by pre-treatment difficulty (historical one-shot accuracy on the
same problem), B$-$A is non-negative in every band: HARD ($n=152$) $+0.0329$
$[-0.0395,+0.1053]$, MED ($n=154$) $+0.0455$ $[+0.0000,+0.0974]$, EASY
($n=586$) $+0.0017$ $[-0.0034,+0.0085]$. Functional collapse is high in every
band, so the phenomenon is not confined to problems where roles were
unnecessary anyway.

\subsection{Collapse is not ``the system was always a single agent''}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

We test the standing objection that a setup where collapse is free never
required multi-agent structure~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} against the
budget-matched single-agent protocol on the same problems
(Table~\ref{tab:transition}). A collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own single
agent by 2.5 points, interval excluding zero, so three agents emitting the
same answer under a shared evaluator loop is \emph{not} functionally one
agent: the redundant computation buys something real, though nothing
attributable to \emph{differentiated} roles. The comparison is conservative
on the axis this project has found decisive --- the single agent gets
\emph{more} oracle feedback (1.66--1.69 evaluator calls against Arm~A's
1.10--1.34) and roughly half the actor calls. The same panel carries a fact
we report rather than bury: gemma-4's team is \emph{worse} than its own
single agent in both arms, so for that actor the organization is actively
costly at this budget.

\subsection{Architectural enforcement restores the organization}
\label{subsec:enforce}

The manipulation is checked three ways and passes all three.
Answer-authorship compliance reaches exactly $1.000$ in every Arm~C and
Arm~C$'$ cell, against $0.993$ (gemma-4) and $0.817$ (gpt-oss) under soft
roles; the Planner emits an answer in $0.000$ of Arm~C trajectories for all
three actors; and the constraint visibly bound, with 694 and 971
role-boundary rejections per Arm~C cell ($0.53$/$0.74$ per trajectory),
formatting coercions excluded. Enforcement holds the organization apart ---
though, as \S\ref{subsec:structnotcog} shows, only the part concerning who
may speak.

\subsection{But it does not restore performance: the pre-registered primary
test is null}
\label{subsec:p4null}

\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy, and
the pre-registered primary test of this paper's original thesis --- is
null: $-0.0121$ $[-0.0369,+0.0125]$.} P3 is null in both actors as well:
C$-$A is $-0.0174$ $[-0.0349,+0.0000]$ for gemma-4 and $-0.0053$
$[-0.0228,+0.0122]$ for gpt-oss. This is pre-declared Pattern~E, whose
committed interpretation reads: \emph{the central causal theory fails; do not
force it}. We report it as a failure of that theory, not as a supporting
detail of a different one.

Two candidate explanations are ruled out rather than assumed away. Arm~C$'$
restores shared history at fixed enforcement: under the pre-registered
ungraded-as-incorrect rule C$'-$C is $+0.0547$ $[+0.0182,+0.0912]$ for
gemma-4 and $-0.0031$ $[-0.0429,+0.0337]$ for gpt-oss, while restricted to
problems graded in both arms it is $+0.0204$ $[-0.0034,+0.0442]$ and
$-0.0072$ $[-0.0361,+0.0217]$. \textbf{The two conventions disagree for
gemma-4}; we report the pre-registered one as primary, and the difference is
attrition rather than context, since Arm~C$'$ leaves fewer trajectories
ungraded than Arm~C. Either way C$'$ remains at or below Arm~A. Attrition is
Arm-C-specific ($7.9\%$/$5.9\%$ hard failures against $0.000$ in A and B);
dropping failures instead of applying the pre-registered rule would make
Arm~C look \emph{better} ($0.897$/$0.889$), which is why the rule was fixed
in advance and why we report both.

\paragraph{What the two nulls together mean.} Enforcement restores the
organizational measure completely and the performance not at all. Combined
with \S\ref{subsec:nodegradation}, organization can be destroyed without cost
in one actor and restored without benefit in both. Whatever the diagram is
explaining, it is not the accuracy.

\subsection{Structural boundaries are not cognitive specialization}
\label{subsec:structnotcog}

The pre-registered manipulation check was that Arm~C would drive \Pone{} ---
the Planner abstaining from \emph{computation} --- to ${\approx}1.0$.
\textbf{That objective failed}: the check named a clause the intervention
does not enforce, since the schema forbids emitting an \emph{answer} and
nothing stops a Planner writing $=1/27$. We re-designated the manipulation
check to answer authorship and report the \Pone{} outcome as a finding, in
that order. That finding is that computation does not move: under enforcement
the Planner's evaluated-equation density is $2.80$ and $3.44$ per 1{,}000
characters against $2.60$ and $2.07$ in Arm~A --- unchanged or \emph{higher}.
Density is the right instrument because the binary rate is anti-correlated
with message length and Arm~C messages are shorter; the same confound makes
Arm~B/gpt-oss score a spurious $0.726$ while emitting bare answers with no
work shown. \textbf{You can structurally stop an agent from answering without
making it stop doing the work.}

\subsection{Mechanism: roles are load-bearing for recovery, not for solving}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

If both actors lose the same organization, the difference in cost comes from
somewhere else. Decomposing each actor's B$-$A into first-submission success
and recovery after a failed submission, paired on the same problems, locates
it. Removing roles costs gemma-4 nothing on problems it solves outright
($0.804 \rightarrow 0.804$, $n=1{,}318$); what changes is what follows a wrong
first answer, where recovery falls $0.202 \rightarrow 0.050$. gpt-oss also
loses recovery ($0.341 \rightarrow 0.242$) but gains first-pass
($0.720 \rightarrow 0.766$, $n=1{,}310$), and the two roughly cancel --- the
shape of its null.

What differs is whether the team can disagree with itself. In Arm~B, on first
passes where all three agents state an answer, gemma-4 is unanimous in
$0.961$ of trajectories against gpt-oss's $0.796$, so disagreement survives
five times more often in gpt-oss. Unanimity is costly when wrong: among
trajectories unanimous and failing the first submission, gemma-4 reaches the
right answer $0.062$ of the time and changes its answer at all only $0.056$
of the time, against gpt-oss's $0.165$ and $0.353$.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Role differentiation is load-bearing for error recovery, not for
first-pass solving --- and only for the actor that cannot otherwise escape a
wrong consensus.}
\end{quote}

This also explains \S\ref{subsec:p4null}: enforcement restores who may speak,
not whether the agents \emph{differ}.

\paragraph{Not fewer chances --- fewer uses of them.}
Every arm runs the same six deliberation cycles, and the evaluator fires only
on Reviewer approval, so evaluator calls count \emph{submissions}, not
opportunities. Cycle spend is near-identical ($5.5$ vs $5.8$, gemma-4,
first-pass-wrong); what differs is its use. Arm~B converts almost every cycle
into a submission ($5.82$ of $5.84$); Arm~A submits $1.53$ times and spends
$4.01$ cycles revising. In $98.5\%$ of gemma-4's Arm~B trajectories the
Reviewer never once declines to submit, against $5.3\%$ in Arm~A, and at full
spend the answer is never revised at all in $0.866~[0.823,0.906]$ of Arm~B
against $0.353~[0.294,0.417]$ of Arm~A.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
Conditioning on needing a retry --- and on cycles or context below --- is
post-treatment, so this is a decomposition, not a causal mediation. The
conditioning set is unmatched: in Arm~A, $59\%$ (gemma-4) and $22\%$ (gpt-oss)
of failed first submissions were never \emph{resubmitted} before the budget
ran out, against $0\%$ and $2\%$ in Arm~B, so A-side levels are not
unconditional recovery probabilities. Every trajectory in every cell did
receive further cycles. Accumulated context is a real competing driver
\cite{yang2026contamination}: within Arm~A alone gpt-oss recovery falls
$0.702 \rightarrow 0.061$ across context terciles. It does not account for the
arm gap, which survives context matching in the lower quartiles ($0.649$ vs
$0.148$, gemma-4; $0.694$ vs $0.236$, gpt-oss) --- but in gpt-oss's top
quartile the ordering \emph{reverses} ($0.072$ vs $0.167$): where context is
longest, Arm~A's own contamination costs more than roles return. Finally the
loop is oracle-gated: no cell recovers before the first oracle verdict.
