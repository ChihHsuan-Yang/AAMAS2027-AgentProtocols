\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that trajectories never
producing a graded answer count as incorrect; significance is exact McNemar
on discordant pairs. Holm correction across the two testable pre-registered
families changes no verdict (P3, gemma-4 Holm $p=0.0120$; P4, all retained).
\textbf{The role-structured-versus-role-free contrast supplying this paper's
headline was pre-registered as secondary and exploratory}, and every
conditional analysis below conditions on a post-treatment variable:
descriptive, never causal.

The results run in five steps: \textbf{R1} the organization is gone and the
first answer does not notice; \textbf{R2} where it costs something is after
the team is told it is wrong, and the standing explanation for that is
rejected; \textbf{R3} the behavioral split becomes an outcome split;
\textbf{R4} both halves repeat in a second domain; \textbf{R5} the
intervention run backwards, enforcing the division of labor.

\subsection{R1. Removing roles changes the organization, not the first
answer}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Removing the role instructions changes nothing else: same three agents,
same order, same budget, same evaluator. \textbf{This claim rests on the
mechanical measures, which involve no judge.} We ask whether \emph{every}
agent independently asserts its own final answer on the first pass, so the
measure is not length-confounded (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}); it rises from
$0.000$--$0.120$ to $0.846$--$0.976$. \textbf{Decomposed by role, the
planner supplies almost all of the change}: the executor is at ceiling
either way ($0.907$--$1.000$) and the reviewer was already high, while the
planner moves $0.000$/$0.000$/$0.183$ to $0.979$/$0.889$/$0.898$ (gemma-4 /
Llama-3.3 / gpt-oss) --- a claim about the \emph{change}, not the level.

\paragraph{Content duplication, against the right null.} gemma-4's and
Llama-3.3's role-free planners write full independent solutions (median
$1{,}876$ and $1{,}892$ characters) before boxing an answer. Numeric-token
overlap confirms it, but only against a per-condition null: pairing planners
with executors from \emph{different} problems shows the baseline is itself
condition-dependent, so the raw difference is invalid. Against each
condition's own null the excess shifts $+0.290$ and $+0.287$ when roles are
removed. \textbf{gpt-oss cannot be read at all}: its role-free planner
emits a median $37$ visible characters in $26.7$ seconds ($2.85$ per second
against $195$ with roles) --- the logging defect below. Byte-identical text
between agents fires on $0.680$ of gpt-oss role-free first passes and
essentially never elsewhere, which is a property of the instrument rather
than of the actors (\S\ref{sec:framework}).

\paragraph{A second opinion, and the leak that stops it being blind.} We put
171 trajectories to a frontier model against a rubric frozen before the
first judgement, with actor, condition, correctness and the automated label
withheld and role words neutralized. It finds the division of labor intact
in $1.000$ of role-structured trajectories and absent in $0.857$--$1.000$ of
role-free ones, stable at $0.953$ across repetitions. This is \textbf{model
adjudication, not human annotation}, and not ground truth. \textbf{It is
also outcome-blind but \emph{not} condition-blind}: neutralization renamed
the role words without equalizing their \emph{presence}, and a neutralized
\texttt{Agent-P/E/R} token appears in all $42$ role-structured items and $3$
of $56$ role-free ones (Fisher exact $p=1.5\times10^{-24}$), so a judge
noticing only whether a transcript labels its speakers could separate the
conditions without reading content; output format separates the enforced
condition further. Normalizing to a common surface would destroy the
differences being judged, so we disclose rather than re-run: \textbf{we do
not call the condition-contrastive adjudication blinded}, and the collapse
claim does not rest on it. Judge stability and the byte metric's
precision/recall are within-item, so unaffected.

\paragraph{First-pass accuracy does not follow --- the control condition.}
\emph{Not a finding} but the control it is: the ablation removed the
organization without crippling the
solver~\cite{dylan2026equal,orogat2026architectural}. On matched problems
the first-pass difference is \emph{identical} for gemma-4 ($+0.0000$,
$[-0.0156,+0.0150]$) and flat for Llama-3.3 ($+0.0077$), while it
\emph{improves} for gpt-oss ($+0.0503$, $[+0.0339,+0.0666]$;
Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}A). Whatever roles buy, they do not buy the
first answer.


\subsection{A logging defect, and its second consequence}
\label{subsec:logging}

Our runner logged each response's \texttt{content}, falling back to
\texttt{reasoning} only when content was empty; gpt-oss-120b often returns a
short content string alongside a long hidden trace, so the derivation was
discarded while the flag documenting the substitution stayed false. Below
$10$ visible characters per second of latency, \textbf{$75.7\%$ of gpt-oss
role-free messages are affected and $93.4\%$ of its first passes contain
one}; the flag reports $5.8\%$. gemma-4 and Llama-3.3 are unaffected
($0.1$--$0.5\%$). Two consequences follow and no third. \textbf{No point
estimate of gpt-oss's duplication rate is recoverable}, and \textbf{the
defect bounds collapse-\emph{mode} interpretation}: calling that
37-character planner a behavioral mode would attribute our defect to the
model. \textbf{It does not manufacture the collapse}, the one place it
could reach a headline measure: restricted to first passes with \emph{no}
affected message, role-free collapse is $0.821$ ($n=106$) against $0.846$
overall and the role-structured rate $0.118$ against $0.120$ --- the gap is
intact on clean messages. Accuracy is graded from an always-present final
answer, and the functional measure reads answer assertions, not
derivations. The campaign was not re-run.

\subsection{R2. Roles change how a team responds to correctness feedback}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

\textbf{The manipulation worked, and at the only place most evaluations
look, it cost nothing.} That would be the whole result if performance were a
single number. It is not: a team that answers also has to repair. So
consider the other phase, where both teams are told the same thing --- this
answer is wrong --- and reading what they do next correctly requires being
exact about the units it is measured in.

\paragraph{What a cycle is, and what a team can spend one on.} The pipeline
runs a fixed grid of \textbf{six deliberation cycles} --- three outer rounds
of two inner rounds --- and a cycle is one pass of
Planner~$\rightarrow$~Executor~$\rightarrow$~Reviewer. The grid does not
change: a further cycle runs whether or not the Reviewer approved. What the
Reviewer's verdict decides is whether the cycle ends in a
\textbf{submission} or a \textbf{revision}. On approval the current answer
goes to the oracle-gated evaluator, which returns PASS --- the trajectory
ends there --- or FAIL with a non-leaking repair hint that becomes the next
cycle's advice. Without approval nothing is submitted, no verdict is
consumed, and the next cycle instead carries the Reviewer's own critique
forward as its advice. Every trajectory therefore splits its realized cycles
into submissions and revisions, and \emph{that split is the one thing the
two conditions are free to differ in}: the grid, the agents, the budget, the
evaluator and the hint text are identical by construction. The two
conditions have the same number of chances to change their answer. They
differ in what each chance is spent on.

\paragraph{Premature stopping, tested and rejected.} The standing explanation
for a team that fails to recover is premature consensus: it agrees with
itself, stops early, and never uses the budget it was given.
\textbf{We test that and reject it}, on three observations that point the
same way. Both conditions \emph{hold} six cycles by construction. Both
\emph{realize} nearly all of them --- $5.52$ versus $5.83$, gemma-4,
first-pass-wrong --- and \textbf{no trajectory was cut short}. And the
role-free team draws \emph{more} oracle verdicts, not fewer: $1.95$
evaluator calls per trajectory against $1.11$ (\S\ref{sec:method}).

That last observation is the decisive one. Every rival explanation of the
form ``the role-free team had less to work with'' predicts \emph{fewer}
verdicts, because a verdict is precisely how a team learns its answer is
wrong; the role-free team draws roughly twice as many and still recovers
less. No budget, no stopping rule and no information asymmetry is left to
attribute the gap to --- what remains is what the team does with the cycle.

\textbf{What differs is use} (Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}C): the
role-free team converts nearly its whole budget into resubmissions and does
not rework the answer once. The behavioral hinge is the Reviewer's decline:
in $98.1\%$ of gemma-4's role-free first-pass-wrong trajectories the
Reviewer never declines at all, against $5.1\%$ with roles ($61.6\%$ versus
$4.3\%$ for gpt-oss). An approval ends the cycle in a verdict on the answer
the team already has; a decline is the only way a cycle gets spent on
producing a different one.

\paragraph{What the frozen-answer rate measures.} The consequence is an
answer that does not move. We count a trajectory as \textbf{frozen} if the
entire transcript --- every role message across all six cycles --- contains
exactly one distinct answer string. Not that the team re-derived the same
value: that the team never at any point wrote down a second candidate to
compare against the first. On gemma-4's first-pass-wrong trajectories that
is $0.845$ of the role-free condition against $0.288$ with roles --- in five
of every six, the team spends its remaining budget and ends holding the
answer the evaluator already rejected, having never produced an alternative
for the Reviewer to prefer or reject.

The funnel separates the two failure shapes. Of gemma-4's $316$
role-structured first-pass-wrong trajectories, $133$ submit a second time,
$128$ of those hold a second distinct answer at some point, and $70$ end
correct --- every recovery that condition has. Of the $316$ role-free ones,
\emph{all} $316$ submit again and only $49$ ever hold a second distinct
answer string; $10$ of those end correct, and $6$ further recoveries occur
on trajectories that never held a second answer at all --- the same
candidate drawing both verdicts (\S\ref{subsec:recovery}).
\textbf{The role-free team is not failing to get another
turn; it is using every turn to send back what it already sent.} This is
Degeneration-of-Thought~\cite{liang2024divergent}, and the point is the
\textbf{inversion}: multi-agency is the prescribed cure that role-free
multi-agency does not deliver. Rubber-stamping is a symptom of answer
lock-in, not established as its cause: we observe the Reviewer approving and
the answer not moving, and cannot separate a Reviewer that has stopped
objecting from one whose objection reaches a Planner no longer differentiated
enough to act on it.

\paragraph{A competing driver we do not dismiss.} Accumulated context is a
real cause of recovery failure~\cite{yang2026contamination} and it does not
account for this gap, which survives context matching in the lower
quartiles --- \textbf{though in gpt-oss's top quartile the ordering
reverses}. The stratification is \textbf{directional only}: context is
nearly a function of condition, so the strata are partly condition
indicators. Full quartile panel in the supplement.

\subsection{R3. The difference in feedback use becomes a difference in
outcome}
\label{subsec:recovery}

A behavioral difference matters only if an outcome depends on it. A team
could resubmit mechanically and still finish correct --- if the grader
accepted the second showing of the same answer, or if teams only resubmit on
problems they would have failed anyway. They do not. Conditional on the
first submission being graded wrong, the role-free team recovers less often
(Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}B): gemma-4 falls $0.222 \rightarrow 0.051$
($-0.1709$ $[-0.2247,-0.1171]$), gpt-oss $0.324 \rightarrow 0.247$
($-0.0766$ $[-0.1361,-0.0153]$). \textbf{Only gemma-4's survives pairing}:
on problems first-pass-wrong under \emph{both} conditions it recovers on
$37$ the role-free team misses against $3$ the other way (exact McNemar
$p=2.0\times10^{-8}$).

\textbf{For gemma-4 this is not a post-treatment conditional.} Its
first-pass rates are equal to four decimals ($0.8025$ either way), so the
role-free team does not enter recovery already behind: in
$\Delta_{\text{final}} = \Delta_{\text{fp}}(1-\text{rec}_A) +
(1-\text{fp}_B)\,\Delta_{\text{rec}}$ the first term vanishes and the
recovery contrast is an exact reparameterization of the intention-to-treat
effect, $-0.1709 \times (1-0.8025) = -0.0338$, the observed final-accuracy
difference, residual zero --- a quantity algebraically equal to the
randomized contrast is not a post-treatment artifact.

\paragraph{Scope across actors, stated once.} The recovery result is
\textbf{clean in gemma-4}, \textbf{directional but unresolved in gpt-oss}
(paired $p=0.208$, reported as directional only; its first-pass rates also
differ by $+0.050$, so the reparameterization above does not extend to it),
and \textbf{absent in Llama-3.3} --- $0.014$ and $0.015$, four recoveries
out of $276$ and four of $268$. That last is a genuine null of the
\emph{role-instruction} manipulation rather than a floor: the same actor's
repair channel exists and is movable, since enforcement raises it to
$0.139$ (paired $0.009$ versus $0.096$, $p=3.8\times10^{-6}$,
\S\ref{subsec:enforce}). The test has ${\approx}80\%$ power against an
excess of $0.04$--$0.05$ where gemma-4's effect is $0.171$: informative
against a gemma-sized effect, uninformative below it. The claim is therefore
that role structure \textbf{can} be load-bearing for feedback-driven
recovery where it is not load-bearing for first-pass solving, not that it
always is.

\paragraph{Final accuracy, against grader noise.} Pooled over both phases
the role-free minus role-structured difference is $-0.0338$
($p=1.6\times10^{-5}$, gemma-4), $+0.0163$ ($p=0.062$) and $+0.0025$
($p=1.00$), exact McNemar on discordant pairs. Repeated identical evaluator
prompts at temperature $0$ disagree on $20$ of $2{,}790$ distinct graded
strings, a group-level rate of $0.0072$, keyed on the exact candidate bytes.
\textbf{We do not divide an effect size by a disagreement rate} --- noise
largely cancels in a paired contrast --- but propagate it into the interval
at the per-call rate $0.0062$ inside the bootstrap. gemma-4's survives
($[-0.0494,-0.0175]$); \textbf{gpt-oss's and Llama-3.3's do not} --- not
resolvable above grader noise rather than absent.

\paragraph{Is the role-free team just a single agent?}
\label{subsec:notsingle}
A standing objection
holds that a setup where collapse is free never needed multi-agent structure
at all~\cite{zhang2026rethinking} --- that the comparison is between a team
and a solo solver rather than between two organizations. A budget-matched
single-agent protocol on the same problems refuses both easy readings, and
we report \textbf{both directions together}: a collapsed gpt-oss team beats
its own single agent ($+0.0294$ $[+0.0131,+0.0456]$), so three agents
emitting one answer is not functionally one agent, \textbf{while gemma-4's
team is significantly worse than its own single agent in both conditions}
($-0.0225$, $-0.0563$). Neither ``multi-agent systems are just single
agents'' nor ``more agents help'' survives. The comparator is \textbf{from a
previously released trace corpus, not our frozen snapshot}, and \textbf{its
two actor arms were built from different source commits}, so each direction
is a sound guard and \emph{the gap between actors is not}; it also drew
\emph{more} oracle feedback ($1.66$--$1.69$ against $1.11$--$1.35$),
conservative on the decisive axis. Full panel in the supplement.

\label{subsec:xdomain}

Everything so far is competition mathematics, where a wrong answer is a
discrete object a team can hold or discard. A domain with free-response
answers, different subject matter and a much higher first-pass rate tests
whether the split is a property of organization or of the benchmark. Both
halves reappear on \textbf{SciBench} --- the complete $574$-problem matched
slice of free-response college science; two actors, $2{,}296$ trajectories,
frozen separately (Figure~\ref{fig:xdomain}). The role-free prompt is
byte-identical to its Omni-MATH form and the role-structured one keeps the
same structural contract with domain wording adapted, so no clause-specific
metric crosses domains. For \textbf{gemma-4} first-pass accuracy is $0.794$
either way and paired recovery falls $0.212 \rightarrow 0.020$ ($n=99$,
$p=6.6\times10^{-5}$), not distinguishable in size from Omni-MATH.
\textbf{gpt-oss}'s cell is underpowered (power $0.06$--$0.45$ at the
realized $n=96$, depending on the test assumed) and resolves nothing;
Llama-3.3 was not run. \textbf{The story holds again for one actor in a
second domain}, not across actors.

\subsection{R5. Structural enforcement is consistent with the
interpretation, and
introduces brittleness}
\label{subsec:enforce}

Every result so far comes from taking organization \emph{away}. That
direction alone cannot distinguish ``role structure supplies revisability''
from ``the role prompts carried some other useful content we also deleted.''
So we run the intervention the other way, replacing the request with a
constraint: making the division of labor unavoidable at the interface ---
where no wording can talk its way around it --- should return the capacity
to produce a second distinct candidate. It does. The manipulation check
passes: the planner authors an answer in $0.000$ of enforced trajectories in
all three actors against $0.986$/$0.916$/$0.958$ under generic prompts, with
$838$/$1{,}183$/$395$ role-boundary rejections per cell (formatting
coercions excluded by a definition fixed in advance). Enforcement
\textbf{un-freezes the answer}: $0.119$/$0.092$/$0.072$ against the
role-free $0.845$/$0.208$/$0.806$, and below the role-structured rate except
for gpt-oss. It does not change what the planner \emph{computes}
(evaluated-equation density $3.11$/$4.78$ per $1{,}000$ characters against
$2.76$/$2.12$), so an agent can be structurally stopped from answering
without being stopped from doing the work.

\textbf{Recovery follows for Llama-3.3; for the other two the direction is
consistent but not resolvable against attrition.} Recovery is computed on
every trajectory with a recorded incorrect first verdict, including the
$150$ enforced trajectories that received one then died in regeneration ---
all necessarily first-pass wrong, since a passing verdict ends the loop, and
scored $0$ by the preregistration. On that denominator enforcement recovers
at $0.314$/$0.395$/$0.139$ against $0.222$/$0.324$/$0.014$, but the paired
contrast survives only for Llama-3.3 ($0.009$ versus $0.096$,
$p=3.8\times10^{-6}$); gemma-4 ($p=0.298$) and gpt-oss ($p=0.105$) do not.
Conditioning instead on successful grading would manufacture significance
out of the same one-sided attrition we reject for accuracy below, and
flatters enforcement twice over --- raising Llama's cell to $0.150$ and the
enforced first-pass column from $0.779$/$0.761$/$0.281$ to
$0.822$/$0.788$/$0.295$.

\textbf{Improved revisability does not become end-to-end accuracy, because
enforcement is brittle} (Table~\ref{tab:armc}). Under the pre-registered
rule the accuracy difference is $-0.0225$ / $-0.0050$ / $+0.0175$: one
significant negative, two nulls. Dropping ungraded trajectories gives
$+0.0190$ / $+0.0361$ / $+0.0453$ --- all positive, all significant, ordered
by actor weakness, exactly the published capability-equalizer
direction~\cite{canedo2026architecture}. \textbf{That reading is
survivorship}: the preregistration fixed in advance that such trajectories
count incorrect, and \textbf{the benefit appears only after conditioning on
successful parsing.} Restoring shared history at fixed enforcement does not
rescue it either ($+0.0450$ and $-0.0125$ pre-registered) --- attrition, not
context.

\textbf{These enforced rates do not compose into
Table~\ref{tab:armc}'s accuracy column, and the attrition is why}: the
$(1-\text{fp})$ identity of \S\ref{subsec:recovery} is exact only when both
columns share a denominator, and here they do not --- composing them
re-scores the never-graded trajectories as graded and returns a
\emph{positive} value for all three actors. The
role-structured-versus-role-free composition is valid because those two
have essentially no attrition ($0.0$--$2.5\%$).
