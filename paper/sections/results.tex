\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All contrasts are paired at the problem level with bootstrap intervals
clustered by problem, under the pre-registered rule that ungraded
trajectories count as incorrect. P1--P5 are the pre-registered endpoints and
carry a Holm correction. The A-versus-B contrast supplying this paper's
headline was pre-registered as \emph{secondary and exploratory}; it is
labeled \textbf{post-hoc} everywhere it appears, and its importance does not
change that label.

\subsection{Removing role instructions: collapse differs enormously by actor}
\label{subsec:collapse}

Arm~B deletes every role instruction and changes nothing else. We measure the
\emph{realized} organization by whether two or more agents in a trajectory
emit byte-identical text. Two rules are reported
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). The \textbf{count-matched} rule inspects only the
first pipeline pass (the first three role messages) and is
\textbf{primary}, because the all-message rule gives longer trajectories more
opportunities to collapse and is therefore confounded with trajectory length.

count-matched contrast is \emph{wider}, not narrower.}
\label{tab:collapse}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
 & & \multicolumn{2}{c}{collapse rate} \\
\cmidrule(lr){3-4}
arm & actor & count-matched & all-message \\
\midrule
A  & gemma-4-31B  & $0.000$ & $0.084$ \\

\textbf{B} & \textbf{gpt-oss-120b} & $\mathbf{0.698}$ & $0.842$ \\

Removing role instructions destroys the visible organization of one actor and
leaves the other's untouched. Arm~B is not ``roles blurred'': $0.90$ of
gpt-oss's \emph{planners} emit a final answer (against $0.17$ in Arm~A) and
the executor reproduces it unchanged in $82\%$ of cases. The pipeline becomes
three independent solvers that agree.

\paragraph{The measure survives the obvious attacks.} Deterministic decoding
cannot produce the equality: \textbf{0 of 1{,}364} collapsed groups arose
from identical prompts, and the endpoint is measurably nondeterministic at
temperature $0$. Normalization \emph{raises} the rate from $0.842$ to
$0.927$, so the exact measure undercounts. It is not retry convergence
(1{,}079 events in the first outer round against 591 later), and not an
easy-problem artifact --- gpt-oss collapses at $0.827$ on the \emph{hardest}
stratum ($0.709$ medium, $0.900$ easy). gemma-4's smaller all-message rate
has a different source, concentrated in long trajectories ($0.978$ at ten or
more role messages, $0.000$ at three): for that actor collapse is a symptom
of being stuck, not a starting condition. Its duplicate groups are genuinely
across-role (436 versus 22 within-role).

Two facts, pointing opposite ways. gpt-oss loses its organization on 879
matched problems and does not get worse --- the interval on $+0.0171$
excludes zero, so in this subset losing the division of labor is marginally
beneficial. gemma-4 never collapses on the first pass and \emph{still} loses
accuracy when the same instructions are removed. \textbf{Visible collapse and
performance loss are dissociated in both directions: collapse without loss,
and loss without collapse.} This retires the tempting mediation story:
gemma-4's loss cannot be explained by its organization degrading, because
under the primary rule it does not degrade. The whole-arm interaction is
$-0.0450$ $[-0.0687,-0.0221]$.

\paragraph{One conflation we explicitly avoid.} gpt-oss's \emph{whole-arm}
B$-$A is $+0.0078$ $[-0.0102,+0.0259]$ --- indistinguishable from zero. The
$+0.0171$ above is the differentiated~$\rightarrow$~collapsed \emph{subset}.
We write ``no detectable loss'' for the arm and never ``a gain''; the two
numbers are not interchangeable. Both signs were also stable as the campaign
grew, checked cumulatively in problem-completion order at 25/50/75/100\% of
the run.

\paragraph{It is not a difficulty artifact.} Stratifying the
$\mathrm{A}_{\text{diff}} \rightarrow \mathrm{B}_{\text{coll}}$ subset
($n=884$) by pre-treatment difficulty --- historical one-shot baseline
accuracy on the same problem --- B$-$A is non-negative in every band:
HARD ($n=147$) $+0.0408$ $[-0.0272,+0.1156]$, MED ($n=152$) $+0.0461$
$[+0.0000,+0.0987]$, EASY ($n=585$) $+0.0017$ $[-0.0034,+0.0085]$. There is no
difficulty band in which gpt-oss pays for collapse, and collapse is most
frequent precisely where it would be most expected to cost something.

\subsection{Collapse is not ``the system was always a single agent''}
\label{subsec:notsingle}

The strongest objection to \S\ref{subsec:nodegradation} is that a setup where
collapse is free never required multi-agent structure, so the apparent gain
was an evaluation artifact~\cite{zhang2026rethinking}. We test it rather than
argue it, against the budget-matched single-agent protocol on the same
problems (Table~\ref{tab:transition}, lower panel).

A collapsed gpt-oss team beats its own single agent by 2.5 points, interval
excluding zero. Three agents emitting the same string under a shared
evaluator loop is therefore \emph{not} functionally one agent: redundant
parallel computation buys something real. What it does not buy is anything
attributable to \emph{differentiated} roles. This is the guard against our
own overclaim, and what distinguishes our reading from the
structural-collapse critique. The same panel carries a fact we report rather
than bury: gemma-4's team is \emph{worse} than its own single agent in both
arms, so for that actor the organization is actively costly at this budget.

\subsection{Architectural enforcement restores the organization}
\label{subsec:enforce}

The manipulation is checked three ways and passes all three.
Answer-authorship compliance reaches exactly $1.000$ in every Arm~C and
Arm~C$'$ cell, against $0.981$ (gemma-4) and $0.617$ (gpt-oss) under soft
roles. Independently measured, the Planner emits an answer in $0.00$ of Arm~C
trajectories for both actors. And the constraint visibly bound: 557 and 811
role-boundary rejections per Arm~C cell ($0.46$/$0.66$ per trajectory),
counted from boundary violations only, with formatting coercions logged
separately and excluded. Collapse falls to ${\approx}0$ under both rules
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). Enforcement holds the organization apart.

\subsection{But it does not restore performance: the pre-registered primary
test is null}
\label{subsec:p4null}

\textbf{P4 --- the actor~$\times$~intervention interaction on accuracy, and
the pre-registered primary test of this paper's original thesis --- is
null: $-0.0073$ $[-0.0335,+0.0180]$.} P3 is null in both actors as well:
C$-$A is $-0.0098$ $[-0.0278,+0.0082]$ for gemma-4 and $-0.0025$
$[-0.0205,+0.0155]$ for gpt-oss. This is pre-declared Pattern~E, whose
committed interpretation reads: \emph{the central causal theory fails; do not
force it}. We report it as a failure of that theory, not as a supporting
detail of a different one.

Two candidate explanations are ruled out rather than assumed away. Arm~C$'$
restores shared history at fixed enforcement, and C$'-$C is null in both
actors ($+0.0179$ gemma-4; $-0.0115$ gpt-oss, both intervals spanning zero),
so the null is not an artifact of Arm~C's reduced context.
Attrition is Arm-C-specific ($6.5\%$/$5.4\%$ hard failures against $0.000$ in
A and B) and we apply the pre-registered ungraded-as-incorrect rule; dropping
failures instead would make Arm~C look \emph{better} ($0.904$/$0.902$), which
is why the rule was fixed in advance and why we report both.

\paragraph{What the two nulls together mean.} Enforcement restores the
organizational measure completely and the performance not at all. Combined
with \S\ref{subsec:nodegradation}, organization can be destroyed without cost
in one actor and restored without benefit in both. Whatever the diagram is
explaining, it is not the accuracy.

\subsection{Structural boundaries are not cognitive specialization}
\label{subsec:structnotcog}

The pre-registered manipulation check was that Arm~C would drive \Pone{} ---
the Planner abstaining from \emph{computation} --- to ${\approx}1.0$.
\textbf{That objective failed}, because the check named a clause the
intervention does not enforce: the schema forbids emitting an \emph{answer},
and nothing in it stops a Planner writing $=1/27$. We re-designated the
manipulation check to answer authorship and report the \Pone{} outcome as a
finding, in that order.

The finding is that computation does not move. Under enforcement the
Planner's evaluated-equation density is $2.70$ and $3.47$ per 1{,}000
characters against $2.55$ and $2.08$ in Arm~A --- unchanged or \emph{higher}.
Density is the right instrument because the binary rate is anti-correlated
with message length and Arm~C messages are shorter; the same confound makes
Arm~B/gpt-oss score a spurious $0.739$ while emitting bare answers with no
work shown. \textbf{You can structurally stop an agent from answering without
making it stop doing the work.}

\subsection{Mechanism: roles are load-bearing for recovery, not for solving}
\label{subsec:mechanism}

Why does one actor pay and the other not? The obvious explanation --- that
gpt-oss's first agent had already solved the problem, so the rest was
decoration --- is \textbf{refuted}. In Arm~B its first planner message is
correct only $0.481$ of the time, and $0.380$ of its final answers are rescued
from a wrong first answer.

Decomposing each actor's B$-$A into first-submission success and recovery
after a failed submission, paired on the same problems, locates the effect
exactly (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Decomposition of the role-removal effect. gemma-4's first-pass rate
is unchanged to three decimals; its entire loss is recovery.}
\label{tab:mechanism}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
actor & $n$ & first pass A$\rightarrow$B & recovery A$\rightarrow$B \\
\midrule
\textbf{gemma-4} & $1{,}280$ & $0.809 \rightarrow 0.809$
  & $\mathbf{0.490 \rightarrow 0.045}$ \\
 & & ($\Delta -0.001$) & ($\Delta -0.445$) \\
gpt-oss & $1{,}274$ & $0.729 \rightarrow 0.775$ & $0.431 \rightarrow 0.249$ \\
 & & ($\Delta +0.046$) & ($\Delta -0.182$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Removing roles costs gemma-4 \emph{nothing} on problems it solves outright.
Its entire loss is recovery collapsing from $0.490$ to $0.045$: without roles,
when it gets a problem wrong the first time, it essentially never recovers.
gpt-oss also loses recovery ($0.431 \rightarrow 0.249$) but gains first-pass
($+0.046$), and the two roughly cancel.

What differs is whether the team can disagree with itself. In Arm~B gemma-4
reaches unanimity in $0.954$ of trajectories, is wrong in $0.436$ of those,
and escapes that state only $0.638$ of the time. gpt-oss retains disagreement
five times more often ($0.251$ versus $0.046$) and escapes wrong consensus
$0.772$ of the time.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Role differentiation is load-bearing for error recovery, not for
first-pass solving --- and only for the actor that cannot otherwise escape a
wrong consensus.}
\end{quote}

This also explains \S\ref{subsec:p4null}. Enforcing answer authorship restores
who may speak; it does not restore whether the agents \emph{differ}. Arm~C
gives back the boundary and not the disagreement, and disagreement is what the
recovering actor needed. Two caveats bound the claim. Recovery is conditioned
on needing a retry, which is post-treatment, so this is a decomposition and
not a causal mediation. And the retry loop is oracle-gated, so ``recovery''
means recovery \emph{with} an oracle signal --- a property of this protocol
family that bounds the mechanism's external validity.
