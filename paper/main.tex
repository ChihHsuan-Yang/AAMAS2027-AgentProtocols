\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems are specified as role diagrams --- Planner,
Executor, Reviewer --- and the diagram is read as a mechanism. We ask which
phase of the work that organization carries. Holding agents, topology, call
budget, context policy, verifier and grading fixed, we delete every role
instruction and, separately, enforce the same division of labor at the
interface, across three actor models and $11{,}600$ trajectories from one
frozen snapshot.

Deleting the role instructions destroys the intended division of labor in
all three actors: the rate at which every agent independently asserts its
own final answer rises from $0.000$--$0.120$ to $0.846$--$0.976$.
First-pass accuracy does not follow --- unchanged for gemma-4,
\emph{improved} for gpt-oss ($+0.050$), flat for Llama-3.3 --- a parity
consistent with prior role ablations and reported here as our control
condition. \textbf{The difference appears only after a team is told its
answer is wrong.} Both teams hold the same six deliberation cycles by
construction and the role-free team draws \emph{more} oracle verdicts, so
it is neither stopped early nor given less information; what differs is
use. In gemma-4 the role-structured team spends its cycles revising while
the role-free team resubmits, leaving its answer unchanged in $0.845$ of
first-pass-wrong trajectories against $0.288$. That split becomes an
outcome split: recovery falls $0.222 \rightarrow 0.051$ (gemma-4, exact
McNemar $p=2\times10^{-8}$), with first-pass rates equal to four decimals,
so the role-free team does not enter recovery already behind. Both halves
return on $574$ free-response college-science problems. Enforcing the
division of labor at the interface un-freezes the answer and returns
revisability, at $8$--$17\%$ attrition that keeps it from reaching
end-to-end accuracy. \textbf{Role structure \emph{can} be load-bearing for
feedback-driven recovery where it is not load-bearing for first-pass
solving} --- cleanly in one actor, directionally in a second, absent in a
third.
\end{abstract}
