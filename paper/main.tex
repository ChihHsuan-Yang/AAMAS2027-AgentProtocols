\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems are specified as role diagrams --- Planner,
Executor, Reviewer --- and the diagram is read as a mechanism. We ask which
phase of the work that organization carries. Holding agents, topology, call
budget, verifier and grading fixed, we delete every role
instruction and, separately, enforce the same division of labor at the
interface, across three actor models ($11{,}600$ trajectories from a
frozen snapshot, plus a feedback-matched control).

Deleting the role instructions destroys the intended division of labor in
all three actors --- the rate at which every agent independently asserts its
own final answer rises from $0.000$--$0.120$ to $0.846$--$0.976$ --- while
first-pass accuracy does not follow. The phases come apart only after a
rejected first answer, and there the deciding variable is whether the
verifier's feedback reaches the team. A role-free team whose prompts carry
no feedback slot almost never recovers ($0.051$ for gemma-4, against
$0.222$ with roles); given the identical feedback string, the same
role-free team recovers $0.609$ and reaches $0.921$ final accuracy, above
the role-structured team's $0.846$. On problems both teams first answer
wrongly, $89$ are recovered only without roles and $4$ only with them
(exact McNemar $p=6\times10^{-22}$), and the ordering returns on $574$
college-science problems ($0.419$ vs.\ $0.254$). The same ordering holds in
the other two actors (gpt-oss: $0.552$ vs.\ $0.324$; Llama-3.3: $0.147$
vs.\ $0.014$). Roles still shape what happens after feedback, as a budget
allocation rather than a capability: the role-structured Reviewer must
decline after a failure, so that team revises internally and resubmits
$1.54$ times per failed trajectory against $3.20$. Each of its
resubmissions is more often right, but there are far fewer of them.
\textbf{Roles appear load-bearing for revision exactly when their prompts
are the only channel carrying the feedback}; ablations of agent roles must
hold information channels, not just budgets, fixed.
\end{abstract}
