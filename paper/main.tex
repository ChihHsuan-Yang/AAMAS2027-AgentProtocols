\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems are specified as role diagrams --- Planner, Executor,
Reviewer --- and the diagram is read as a mechanism. We ask which phase of the
work that organization actually carries. Holding agents, call budget, context
policy, verifier and grading fixed, we delete every role instruction (Arm~B)
and, separately, make the division of labor structurally unrepresentable
(Arm~C), across three actor models on $11{,}600$ trajectories from one frozen
snapshot.

Removing roles destroys the division of labor in all three actors: the rate at
which every agent independently asserts its own final answer rises from
$0.000$--$0.120$ to $0.846$--$0.976$, with the planner carrying the entire
signal. A blinded, rubric-frozen adjudication --- model adjudication, not human
annotation --- reproduces this, judging the division of labor intact in
$100\%$ of Arm~A trajectories and absent in $86$--$100\%$ of Arm~B. First-pass
accuracy does not follow: it is unchanged for gemma-4 and improves for gpt-oss
($+0.050$), a parity consistent with prior role ablations and reported here as
our control condition. The cost appears only \emph{after} a wrong first
answer, where recovery collapses ($0.222 \rightarrow 0.051$ gemma-4,
$0.324 \rightarrow 0.249$ gpt-oss; Llama-3.3 recovers at $0.015$ in both arms,
a floor effect that does not replicate the result). The role-free team is not
stopped early --- it holds the same six cycles and spends $5.80$ of $5.83$ on
resubmission rather than revision. Architectural enforcement restores both
organization and recovery but not final accuracy, and costs $8$--$17\%$
attrition; our preregistered primary test is null. Role structure is
load-bearing for recovering from a wrong answer, not for producing the first
one.
\end{abstract}
