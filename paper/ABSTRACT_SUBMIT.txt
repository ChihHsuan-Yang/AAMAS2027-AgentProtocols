Multi-agent LLM systems are specified as role diagrams -- Planner, Executor, Reviewer -- and the diagram is read as a mechanism. We ask when the roles are load-bearing. Holding agents, topology, call budget, evaluator and grading fixed, we delete every role instruction and, separately, enforce the same division of labor at the interface, across three actor models (11,600 trajectories from a frozen snapshot), and add an exploratory feedback-matched role-free condition after the freeze. Deleting the role instructions destroys the intended division of labor on Omni-MATH -- the rate at which every agent asserts its own final answer rises from 0.000--0.120 to 0.846--0.976 -- while first-pass accuracy does not fall. Roles look load-bearing for repairing a rejected answer only against a role-free team whose prompts never carried the evaluator's feedback (gemma-4 recovers 0.222 with roles, 0.051 without). Given the same feedback string, the role-free team recovers more: 0.609 against 0.222 for gemma-4 (on problems both first answer wrongly, 89 recovered only without roles and 4 only with them; exact McNemar p=6.2x10^-22), with higher final accuracy for every actor on Omni-MATH and on SciBench. Yet when each team is credited with the same number of evaluator verdicts on the same problem, the role-free advantage disappears: pooled over the five actor--domain cells the difference is +0.009 in favor of roles (95% CI -0.014 to +0.033), and no robustness check puts the role-free team significantly ahead. Per verdict, roles add little; what they mainly change is how often the team asks for feedback. The role-structured Reviewer withholds approval: on Omni-MATH, gemma-4's team submits 1.27 times per failed trajectory against 3.20, and 84 of its 316 failed trajectories never submit at all. In a further exploratory run on 400 problems, overriding the Reviewer with the role prompts unchanged raises gemma-4's recovery from 0.198 (gated re-run) to 0.613, as high as the role-free team's 0.549. Structure is not adaptability: a clean division of labor is not evidence that a team acts on corrective information, and role ablations must hold information channels fixed, not only budgets.
